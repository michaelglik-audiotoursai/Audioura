Your first stop is Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers). Stand directly in front of the display in the Linde Family Wing at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. From this vantage point, you can fully appreciate the vivid interplay of colors and the intricate details of Joan Miró's lithographs, which might be overlooked from a distance. The proximity allows you to discern the texture and depth of the Louis Broder's vellum, an essential element in experiencing the fusion of visual and literary art.

In 1971, Louis Broder published "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" (The Lizard with Golden Feathers), an illustrated book featuring forty color lithographs by Joan Miró. Before this publication, such a vibrant fusion of art and literature was not part of Broder's repertoire. By choosing to publish this work, Broder contributed to elevating the status of illustrated books as significant artistic expressions. This decision also helped cement Joan Miró's reputation as a master of printmaking, bringing his vivid and imaginative creations to a broader audience. Miró's lithographs in this book are printed on exquisite publisher's vellum, a choice that enhances the brilliance of his colors and the finesse of his lines. This tactile quality invites a closer examination, revealing the layers of Miró's whimsical yet profound imagery. The work exemplifies the exhibition's thesis that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative ventures. Boris Fridman's gift of this particular piece to the museum enriches the collection, showcasing the evolution of books as integrated artworks. As you explore these lithographs, consider how art transcends the canvas, transforming the space into a vivid narrative landscape.

Stop 2: Au Soleil du Plafond by Juan Gris

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3399, -71.0896

Orientation: Stand just a few feet from "Au Soleil du Plafond" for an ideal view of the intricate interplay between text and image. From here, you can appreciate the elaborate lithographs and understand how they transform the concept of a book into a visual and poetic journey.

In the mid-1910s, L. Rosenberg began a project for a book titled "Au Soleil du Plafond," with Juan Gris as its designer and Pierre Reverdy as its author. Gris completed only half of the intended work before passing away in 1927. Reverdy's poetic text accompanies the visual works, showcasing the collaboration between text and image. The book's production was commissioned from Mourlot Frères, a renowned lithography workshop in Paris, by Tériade, known for their lithography expertise, ensuring the exceptional quality of the prints. Printed on Arches wove paper, these lithographs capture the essence of Gris's cubist style, transforming the traditional book into a vibrant, visual experience. The piece exemplifies how artists like Gris redefined books as integrated works of art, aligning with the exhibition's thesis.

Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism by Salvador Dalí

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3399, -71.0896

Orientation: ** As you stand here, position yourself so you can examine both the illustrations and the text of "Moses and Monotheism." Notice the way the images captivate your attention against the sheepskin pages, just as the words command your contemplation. In doing so, you uncover an intimate dialogue between word and image.

Salvador Dalí, in 1974, embarked on an artistic endeavor by illustrating Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism," a text that delved into complex theories about the origins of monotheism. The project resulted in a set of ten drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin, vividly brought to life by Dalí's imaginative interpretations. Freud, known for his pioneering work in psychoanalysis, authored this provocative text, proposing that Moses was not a Hebrew but an Egyptian, which was a radical theory for its time. The Hogarth Press, known for its literary prowess, published this edition, exemplifying the bold integration of literature and fine art. This creative venture underscores the exhibition's theme by illustrating how Dalí infused Freud's controversial ideas with surrealist visuals, turning the book into a multi-sensory experience.
