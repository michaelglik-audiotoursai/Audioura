In 1971, Joan Miró created "Le Lézard aux plumes d'or" (The Lizard with Golden Feathers), an illustrated book featuring forty color lithographs. Broder's involvement in the project ensured that Miró's imaginative vision was realized through meticulous production, enhancing the book’s artistic value. The Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, now houses this work as part of its collection, thanks to a generous gift. The lithographs within this book are printed on Japan and bound in the publisher’s vellum, underscoring the high-quality craftsmanship that Broder was known for. Miró's bold, abstract forms dance across the pages, each lithograph a fusion of color and composition that speaks to the artist's distinctive style. By integrating text and image, "Le Lézard aux plumes d'or" exemplifies the exhibition’s thesis that the collaborative efforts between artists and publishers like Broder revolutionized the book as an art form.

Orientation: ** Stand before "Au Soleil du Plafond" and let your eyes trace the intersection of literature and visual art. Directly in front of you, the interplay of text and image becomes palpable, revealing the symbiotic relationship between Pierre Reverdy's poetry and Juan Gris's lithographic artistry.

This choice gave life to 11 lithographs, printed by Mourlot Frères on Arches paper, a material chosen for its enduring quality and its ability to capture the vibrancy of the artworks. Juan Gris, renowned for his contributions to Cubism, worked meticulously with Reverdy, known for his vivid and emotive poetry, although they never completed the project together in life. In a single cohesive book, the bold lithographs by Gris echo the rhythmic cadence of Reverdy's text, highlighting the exhibition's argument: the transformative power of collaborative artistry in book form. Now displayed in the gallery named after Torf, the Linde Family who facilitated many such exhibits, this work exemplifies the unique capacity of the book as a canvas for artistic innovation. Tériade's commitment to the project ensured that these lithographs reached audiences decades after their inception, allowing new generations to engage with Gris's unfinished ideas.This exhibit not only honors the collaboration between Gris and Reverdy but also invites reflection on how light and space are portrayed through their combined genius, connecting thematically to broader, existential questions explored in the gallery.

Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism by Salvador Dalí

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3394, -71.0895

Orientation: As you stand before the "Moses and Monotheism" exhibit, position yourself so you can see the details of Salvador Dalí’s illustrations clearly. This vantage point allows you to appreciate the intricate interplay of image and text, a hallmark of the exhibit, and observe the texture of the sheepskin on which these illustrations are printed.

In 1938, Salvador Dalí met Sigmund Freud at Freud's home, uniting two pivotal figures—one from the surrealist art movement and the other from the field of psychoanalysis. This encounter happened just a year before Freud's 1939 publication of "Moses and Monotheism," a book that explored the origins of monotheism and significantly influenced Dalí’s artistic journey. The Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, showcases Dalí's illustrations for this book, allowing visitors to delve into the intersection of Dalí's surrealist vision and Freud's psychoanalytic theories. The illustrations, part of a set of ten, are printed on sheepskin, which underscores the tactile and visual richness of the work. Published by The Hogarth Press, the choice of material and medium reflects the avant-garde approach to book-making as an art form. This exhibit exemplifies how Dalí, through these illustrations, engaged with Freud's ideas, capturing the complexity and depth of psychoanalytic thought. Here, the integration of text and imagery transforms the book into a cohesive artwork, embodying the exhibition's thesis that artists like Dalí revolutionized the book as an art form through such dynamic collaborations.
