**The Work**

This is a portrait of Madame de Pompadour, painted in oil on canvas in 1759. Its importance comes from where it falls in a sequence. François Boucher painted Madame de Pompadour seven times, and this is the seventh and last. Whatever he had worked out about her face, her bearing and her presentation over the six earlier portraits, this canvas is where that long effort ends.

Oil on canvas suited that purpose. It is a medium that can be built up and revised in layers, and Boucher was revising one sitter across an entire series. Each new portrait was a chance to refine the image, and this is the version he left as his final word.

The painting was not made for a private room. It was first exhibited at Versailles, the centre of court life, so it was shown where the sitter's image carried the most weight. Afterward it passed to her brother. A portrait displayed before the court became a family possession.

**The Artist**

François Boucher was a painter of the French Rococo, the light, ornamental style that dominated eighteenth-century French art and decoration. By 1759 he had returned to this one sitter again and again. Few painters of the period are linked so closely to a single patron and subject. This canvas marks the end of that working relationship on canvas. After this, there would be no eighth portrait.

**The Emotional Reading**

The sense of finality is part of what you are seeing. You are looking at the last time Boucher painted Madame de Pompadour, the point where seven attempts to capture one woman came to rest.
