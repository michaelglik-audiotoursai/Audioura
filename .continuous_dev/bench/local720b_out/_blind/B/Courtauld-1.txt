# Audio Description: Van Gogh's *Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear*

**Orientation**

Stand about an arm's length from Van Gogh's iconic *Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear* and look at the green coat and the fur cap. From here you can see raised ridges of thick paint and the marks of the tool that laid them down. From across the room the surface flattens into colour, and that texture disappears.

**The Work**

Vincent van Gogh painted this in 1889. He faces us in a green overcoat and a blue cap trimmed with black fur. A white bandage covers one ear and wraps beneath his chin.

The cap is more than costume. He had just bought it to hold the heavy bandage in place and to keep out the winter cold. It records the hard conditions he was working under that January.

Behind him, three things share the wall. On the left is an open window. Beside it stands a canvas on an easel, bearing only a few marks that cannot be made out. On the right is a Japanese woodblock print, *Geishas in a Landscape*, made by Satō Torakiyo in the 1870s. Van Gogh owned that print. He collected hundreds of Japanese prints, and he copied this one into the painting.

Those prints taught him bold colour and new ways of arranging space, and he used both to modernise his style. Here they show in the strong, flat blocks of green, blue and orange that sit against one another without blending.

The ridges in front of you are impasto, paint applied so thickly that it holds the marks of the painting knife. Van Gogh used it to give the picture energy and passion. He had admired it in the work of Adolphe Monticelli, a painter whose heavily built-up canvases he praised in a letter to his brother Theo in 1888.

One detail is easy to miss. The bandage appears to cover his right ear, but the wound was to his left. He painted himself from a mirror, so the image is reversed. In a letter to Theo dated 17 January 1889, he mentioned a new self-portrait, which is believed to be this one.

**The Artist**

Van Gogh had left Paris for Arles, in the south of France, hoping to found the Studio of the South, a community of artists who would support one another. He rented four rooms in the Yellow House and invited Paul Gauguin, the French painter, to join him.

The plan fell apart within weeks. On the evening of 23 December 1888, Gauguin threatened to leave, and Van Gogh approached him with a razor. Later that night he cut off his own left ear and took it to a woman in Arles named Rachel. She was distressed, and she called the police, who arrested Van Gogh for lunacy.

This portrait was painted only weeks after that night. The shared studio he had worked toward was gone. He shows himself beside an easel, still dressed for work.

**The Emotional Reading**

The painting holds the wound and the work side by side. The bandage is framed by an easel and a print he loved, and the paint itself is laid on thickly and with force.

**In the Collection**

Samuel Courtauld bought the painting in 1928. It now stands at the centre of the Gallery's Impressionist and Post-Impressionist group, alongside Manet's *A Bar at the Folies-Bergère*, another of the collection's best-known paintings. It remains the self-portrait in which Van Gogh recorded his injury in paint.
