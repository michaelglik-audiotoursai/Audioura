# Sappho and Alcaeus: Audio Description

**Orientation**

Begin a few steps back from *Sappho and Alcaeus* so the whole wide panel fits in your view. The painting is nearly twice as wide as it is tall, and from a distance you can see how it is built: a single musician on one side, a curved bank of listeners on the other, and the sea beyond them. Then move in close to the white marble seats where the women sit. Letters are carved into the stone, and up close they become readable. That lettering holds the painter's most deliberate change to history.

**The Work**

This is an oil painting on a wooden panel, about 66 by 122 centimetres. It imagines a concert on the Greek island of Lesbos in the late 7th century BC. The performer is Alcaeus of Mytilene, a lyric poet from the island's chief city. He plays the kithara, the large stringed instrument used by professional Greek musicians. Facing him is Sappho, the most celebrated woman poet of the ancient world and his fellow islander. Several of her female friends sit with her.

Sappho leans forward, listening closely. Her arm rests on a cushion, and on that cushion lies a laurel wreath, the traditional prize for a winning performer. It is presumably meant for Alcaeus. The painting gives no sign of whether he will receive it.

The scene illustrates a passage by Hermesianax, a Greek poet whose lines survive only because a later writer quoted them. That writer was Athenaeus, who preserved them in his *Deipnosophistae*, or *The Philosophers' Banquet*, a sprawling collection of dinner-table conversation and quotation. So the painting rests on a fragment of a fragment, a scene reconstructed from a text that itself survives only secondhand.
