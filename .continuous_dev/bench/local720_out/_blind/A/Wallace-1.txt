# A Dance to the Music of Time, by Nicolas Poussin

## Where to Stand

Stand first at arm's length from the left edge of *A Dance to the Music of Time*, level with the stone pillar beside the dancers. From here you can see that its carved head has two faces, one young and one old. From across the room it reads as a plain garden statue. Then step back to the centre of the room and look up into the sky. The dancers' rhythm is repeated by a second procession overhead, moving across the dawn clouds.

## The Work

Four figures hold hands and dance in a ring, facing outward. On the right, Time sits and plays a lyre. He is the winged, naked, grey-bearded old man. Above them, the day is beginning. Aurora, goddess of the dawn, flies ahead of the chariot of Apollo, the sun-god. Apollo holds a ring that stands for the Zodiac, the circle of constellations that marks the passing year. Around him travel the Hours, the goddesses of time's divisions. Down on the ground, the seasons turn. Up in the sky, the hours turn. Time keeps both in step.
