## The Work

A man of twenty-six turns toward you, one hand on his hip, his elbow pushing out at the viewer. We know his age because Frans Hals painted it into the picture. In the top right corner, a small Latin inscription reads *aetatis suae 26, anno 1624*: "in his twenty-sixth year, in the year 1624." He was therefore born in 1597 or 1598.

We do not know his name. Nineteenth-century titles in Dutch, English and French cast him as a soldier, perhaps an officer in the part-time civic militias that the Dutch liked to have painted in large group portraits. Hals painted several of those groups, and so did Rembrandt, the Amsterdam painter whose *Night Watch* of 1642 is the most famous example. But this man could just as easily have been a wealthy civilian. The art historian Pieter Biesboer has suggested he may be Tieleman Roosterman, a Dutch cloth merchant born in 1598, whom Hals portrayed on another occasion.

Whoever he was, he paid for display. His silk costume was gorgeous and very expensive, and Hals arranged the picture to show it off. The low viewpoint and the turning pose, which Hals used in other portraits too, push the embroidered sleeve and the lace cuff toward the front of the picture. Up close, you can see the trick behind the effect. Embroidery that must have taken someone weeks to stitch is rendered in long, rapid brushstrokes. That speed is what makes the portrait feel lively and spontaneous rather than stiff.
