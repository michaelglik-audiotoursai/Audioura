Stand directly in front of the painting by Georges Seurat in The Courtauld Gallery to examine the wall surface closely, where stepping in reveals how light and shadow are built entirely from small dots of pure colour juxtaposed in varying concentrations and intensity. 

That painting is *Young Woman Powdering Herself*, created between 1888 and 1890. It depicts the young model Madeleine Knobloch, who was the artist's mistress and bore him two sons, giving the composition a deeply personal meaning. A scanner created by the Italian Institute of Optics revealed an even more personal touch hidden beneath the surface: the vase of flowers in the top left-hand corner was originally a portrait reflection of Seurat at work, which would have been his only known self-portrait. Georges Seurat lived from December 2, 1859, to March 29, 1891, and worked as a Neo-Impressionist painter who, together with Paul Signac—the French Neo-Impressionist artist who developed pointillism alongside Seurat—invented pointillism. This work belongs to the collection of The Courtauld, acquired through the Samuel Courtauld Trust.
