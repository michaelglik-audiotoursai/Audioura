Orientation: "Au Soleil du Plafond," created by Juan Gris in collaboration with Pierre Reverdy, is part of the MFA's exploration into how artists like Gris reshaped the book as a complete art form. In 1955, this set of works was published by Éditions Verve and printed by Mourlot Frères on Arches paper, consisting of 11 lithographs. This collaboration represents a critical intersection of art and text, where the images and words meld into a singular, immersive experience.

In this creation, Gris's lithographs are printed on Arches paper, a choice that lends texture and durability to each page. Comprising 11 lithographs, this work showcases Gris's ability to merge geometric precision with vibrant expressions of color. Reverdy's contributions extend beyond mere text; his words complement Gris’s images, providing an interplay that speaks volumes about their shared vision. The Louis Broder, Éditions Verve, and the printer, Mourlot Frères, a renowned lithographic printing company in Paris, played pivotal roles in bringing this collaboration to life, showing just how essential these behind-the-scenes players are in the art-making process. Reverdy, a French poet known for his influence on the Surrealists, brought to the table his mastery of language and abstraction. His poetic lines dance alongside Gris's lithographs, creating a layered experience where text and image become inseparable. This collaboration exemplifies the exhibition's argument that the artist's book is an integrated art form. By fusing visual and verbal art in such a cohesive manner, Gris and Reverdy redefined the boundaries of artistic expression within a book format. The decision by Éditions Verve to commission Gris, known for his Cubist works, underscores the importance of creative partnerships in advancing the livre d'artiste tradition. Gris's modernist approach, combined with Reverdy's lyrical abstraction, resulted in a work that resonated well beyond its pages. The inclusion of this piece in the exhibition highlights how such collaborations continue to influence and expand our understanding of art as a multifaceted discipline. As you ponder this work, consider the enduring impact of these creative unions. The partnership between Gris and Reverdy, facilitated by Éditions Verve and Mourlot Frères, not only advanced the artistic book form but also left a lasting impression on the perception of light and belief in art.
