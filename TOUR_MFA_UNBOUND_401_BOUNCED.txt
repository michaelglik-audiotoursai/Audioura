Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Position yourself at the center of the room to fully appreciate Salvador Dalí's striking illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." From this vantage point, the intricate details and symbolic depth of Dalí's work come to life, inviting you to delve into the revolutionary concept of monotheism as depicted through the lens of the artist's vision.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí collaborated with Freud, who authored this profound exploration into the origins of monotheism. Freud's controversial hypothesis that Moses was of Egyptian descent shifted paradigms, and Dalí's illustrations served to enhance and amplify this intellectual discourse. The medium of illustrations in this exhibit showcases a unique intersection of image and text, bringing Freud's words to visual manifestation through Dalí's surreal and thought-provoking artistic style. Freud's intention with "Moses and Monotheism" was to challenge existing beliefs and explore the implications of a different narrative surrounding Moses' birth and lineage. The reception of this work was marked by shock and intrigue, as viewers grappled with the provocative ideas put forth by Freud.
