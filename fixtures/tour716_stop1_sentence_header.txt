Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: the episode 1 of season 1 biking tour, Salem, MA - Biking Tour
Tour-Category: biking

Stop 1 is the Salem Maritime National Historic Site.

Coordinates: 42.5212, -70.8864

Orientation: Begin at the wharf, where the masts of the Friendship rise over Derby Street.

Welcome to Salem. The Salem Maritime National Historic Site preserves the wharves and warehouses from which Salem's merchant fleet once sailed to the far side of the world. Stand here a moment and picture the harbour crowded with rigging.

Directions: From the wharf, ride north along Derby Street for about four hundred metres. Type/Specialty: Historic house museum Specific Examples: The gabled seventeenth-century mansion — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: House of the Seven Gables

Coordinates: 42.5190, -70.8780

The House of the Seven Gables, built in 1668, inspired Nathaniel Hawthorne's novel of the same name. Its dark clapboard and steep gables have watched the harbour for three and a half centuries.

Directions: Continue west on Derby Street, then turn onto Essex Street.

Stop 3: Salem Witch Museum

Coordinates: 42.5230, -70.8960

The Salem Witch Museum recounts the trials of 1692, when nineteen people were hanged on Proctor's Ledge. The building itself, a former church, lends a sombre weight to the telling.

Directions: Ride two blocks east to the museum quarter.

Stop 4: Peabody Essex Museum

Coordinates: 42.5225, -70.8895

The Peabody Essex Museum grew from the collections of sea captains who brought home art and curiosities from Asia and the Pacific. It is one of the oldest continuously operating museums in the country.

Directions: The final stop is a short ride south to the recreated colonial settlement.

Stop 5: Pioneer Village

Coordinates: 42.5080, -70.8960

Pioneer Village, built in 1930, is the oldest living-history museum in the United States, recreating Salem as it stood in 1630 with wigwams, dugouts and thatched cottages.

This tour followed Salem's thread from the wharves to the first settlement. That's 5 stops in all.

Sources: This tour draws on information from www.nps.gov and www.pem.org.
