From this vantage point, the lithographs unfold across the pages with a clarity that reveals their intricate relationship to the words of Pierre Reverdy, inscribed in their original lithographic form.

In 1916-1917, L. Rosenberg initiated the project for the book "Au Soleil du Plafond," with Juan Gris set to design it. Gris, however, passed away in 1927, having completed only half of the intended work. Despite his early death, the project was eventually completed and published in Paris by Éditions Verve in 1955, preserving Gris's artistic vision. Reverdy's involvement with Gris resulted in a seamless confluence of image and text, an embodiment of the exhibition's thesis that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through rich collaborations. The lithographs, produced by Mourlot Frères, a French lithography workshop founded in 1852, on Arches wove paper, consist of 11 plates that capture the vivid imagination of Gris. This set, rarely on view and typically housed in archives, underscores the significance of the livre d'artiste — a genre where artistic and literary elements converge to form a singular, cohesive piece of art. In these lithographs, the dialogue between Gris and Reverdy transcends time, reflecting the interconnectedness of their crafts. In 1938, Salvador Dalí met Sigmund Freud in London and took the opportunity to draw Freud's portrait.
