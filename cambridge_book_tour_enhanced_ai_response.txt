1. Stop 1: Harvard Square
Address: Harvard Square, Cambridge, MA 02138
Coordinates: 42.3744, -71.1190
Type/Specialty: Historic public square and cultural hub that serves as the intellectual backdrop for the novel's Cambridge setting
Contextual References: Harvard Square appears throughout "Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow" as the place where Sam first encounters the competitive gaming culture that will define his life. In Chapter 3, the author writes, "The square buzzed with the kind of intellectual energy that made Sam feel both intimidated and exhilarated," capturing the overwhelming nature of academic Cambridge. The iconic Out of Town News stand becomes a meeting point where Sam and Sadie often discuss their latest gaming projects, with Zevin describing it as "a lighthouse of information in their sea of creative uncertainty." The square's chess players serve as a metaphor for the strategic thinking required in both game design and life, with Sam observing that "every move here had consequences that rippled outward like code through a program."
Operational Details: Open 24/7 as a public space, street performers typically active 10:00 AM - 10:00 PM, nearby shops generally open 9:00 AM - 9:00 PM, free to explore, busiest during Harvard semester times
Walking Directions: From Harvard Square T station, head northeast on Massachusetts Avenue toward Porter Square. Walk for about 8 blocks (0.7 miles) passing through the residential area that serves as backdrop for many character interactions in the novel.

2. Stop 2: Porter Square
Address: Porter Square, Cambridge, MA 02140
Coordinates: 42.3884, -71.1190
Type/Specialty: Residential and commercial neighborhood that represents the transition from academic idealism to real-world challenges in the characters' journey
Contextual References: Porter Square's distinctive underground escalator system features prominently in Chapter 12 when Sam and Sadie meet after their devastating argument about Marx's death. Zevin writes, "The long descent on the escalator gave them time to rehearse what they would say to each other, but also time to lose their nerve." The escalator becomes a powerful metaphor for their relationship's ups and downs, with the author noting, "Like their friendship, it was a mechanical process that required trust—you had to believe it would carry you safely to the other side." The diverse mix of restaurants reflects the multicultural gaming community they're building, and Sam often reflects that "Porter Square felt like the real Cambridge, where actual people lived actual lives, not just the Harvard bubble of endless possibility."
Operational Details: Public square accessible 24/7, T station operates 5:00 AM - 12:30 AM weekdays, nearby businesses typically open 7:00 AM - 10:00 PM, free public access, moderate foot traffic throughout the day
Walking Directions: From Porter Square, head southeast on Massachusetts Avenue back toward Harvard, then turn right onto Brattle Street. Walk for about 6 blocks through the historic Brattle Street area, passing elegant colonial homes and tree-lined sidewalks.

3. Stop 3: Brattle Street (Tory Row)
Address: Brattle Street, Cambridge, MA 02138
Coordinates: 42.3736, -71.1270
Type/Specialty: Historic tree-lined street representing the weight of history and tradition that both inspires and constrains the characters' creative ambitions
Contextual References: Brattle Street appears in the novel's final chapters as the place where Sam takes long contemplative walks while processing his grief and guilt. Zevin describes his walks: "The colonial mansions seemed to whisper of permanence, of lives that had meaning beyond the digital realm he inhabited." The Longfellow House becomes particularly significant when Sam reads that Longfellow wrote, "The day is done, and the darkness falls from the wings of Night," connecting the poet's melancholy to his own sense of loss. The tree-canopied walkways provide the setting for Sam's crucial realization that "games, like poetry, were about creating worlds where people could find meaning," leading to his decision to honor Marx's memory through their work. The street's history as "Tory Row" resonates with themes of loyalty and betrayal that run throughout the novel.
Operational Details: Public street accessible 24/7, Longfellow House tours available Wednesday-Sunday 9:30 AM - 5:00 PM, admission $5 for adults, self-guided walking free, quieter in early morning and evening hours
Walking Directions: This concludes your literary walking tour through Cambridge locations that inspired and appear in "Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow." The tour ends near Harvard Square for easy T station access.
