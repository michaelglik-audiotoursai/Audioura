Within it, the 1971 masterpiece "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" (The Lizard with Golden Feathers) captivates with its striking blend of art and text, fully realized when viewed up close.

In 1971, Louis Broder, a renowned Parisian publisher, released "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," featuring the evocative artistry of Joan Miró. This illustrated book was revolutionary, composed of 40 vibrant color lithographs in a limited edition of 100. Through this collaboration, Broder significantly advanced the illustrated book as a form, emphasizing the synergy between artist and publisher. The decision to print this edition on publisher’s vellum added a tactile richness that complemented Miró's bold visual language. Additionally, Boris Fridman, a prominent donor, gifted this edition to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. His contribution expanded the museum's Surrealist-era collection, providing visitors with a deeper appreciation of collaborative book art. Here in the Linde Family Gallery, this work sits at the nexus of text and image, inviting viewers to explore the cultural narrative of artist-publisher partnerships.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí embarked on an artistic journey to visually interpret Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." Dalí, known for his surrealist approach, chose to delve into Freud's complex exploration of the origins of monotheism, producing a series of illustrations that brought a new dimension to the text. Freud originally penned the work during the late 1930s, amidst personal and global upheaval, which influenced the book’s intense examination of belief and identity. The illustrations, while rarely on view and usually kept in archives, embody the dynamic interplay between Freud's written word and Dalí's visual interpretation. The lively frenzy of Dalí's lines contrast sharply with the calculated prose of Freud, enhancing the book's exploration of monotheism’s roots. Stop 3: Au Soleil du Plafond by Juan Gris

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3391, -71.0879

Orientation: "Au Soleil du Plafond," designed by Juan Gris with text by Pierre Reverdy, represents a significant moment in the history of the livre d'artiste. Published in 1955 by Éditions Verve, this work brings together the artistic vision of Gris and the poetic voice of Reverdy, showcasing the collaborative nature of bookmaking as an art form.

In 1927, Juan Gris passed away from kidney failure, leaving his final project, "Au Soleil du Plafond," incomplete. However, in 1955, the project found new life when Éditions Verve published it, featuring text by Pierre Reverdy, a french poet of the surrealist movement. The lithographs, printed by Mourlot Frères, reflect the intricate interplay between image and text, a hallmark of the exhibition's thesis that artists revolutionized the book as an art form. The collaboration between Gris and Reverdy exemplifies the seamless blend of visual and literary elements, where typography and imagery work in concert to convey meaning. This work is more than a collection of images; it is a dialogue between two creative minds, preserved in a form that challenges the traditional boundaries of visual and literary art. Gris's innovative approach continues to influence the perception of books as holistic works of art. In 1915, art dealer Léonce Rosenberg conceived an artist's book pairing Pierre Reverdy’s poems with illustrations by Juan Gris [art-books.com]. The project came to an abrupt halt when Gris died in 1927 at age forty, leaving only eleven completed plates [araderbooks.com]. Nearly thirty years later, publisher Tériade revived the abandoned collaboration and issued *Au Soleil du Plafond* in 1955 with lithographs printed by Mourlot Frères [araderbooks.com, emuseum.toledomuseum.org, invaluable.com].
