In 1974, Salvador Dalí, a prominent surrealist artist known for eccentricity, crafted a series of illustrations for Sigmund Freud's *Moses and Monotheism*, transforming the psychoanalyst's text into a vivid artistic exploration. Freud, a pioneer of psychoanalysis, authored this work in 1939, challenging traditional narratives with his controversial theory on the origins of monotheism. The Hogarth Press, an acclaimed English publishing house, brought Freud's groundbreaking text to the public, marking a pivotal moment in the intersection of psychology and theology. Their decision to enlist Dalí was a deliberate move, merging Freud’s dense theories with the surrealist’s imaginative visuals. This work exemplifies the exhibition's argument: Dalí and Freud's collaboration redefined the book as a unified artwork. Displayed in these illustrations, usually kept in archives, echo the themes that persist throughout the exhibition. Orientation: Gris, a pivotal figure in the Cubist movement, joined forces with Reverdy to create this work, embodying the exhibition’s theme that the book itself is an artwork.

The piece consists of 11 lithographs on Arches paper, a choice that enhances the depth and vibrancy of the colors. Each lithograph serves not just as a visual delight but as an integral part of the narrative crafted by Reverdy’s poetic insights. Juan Gris, known for his contributions to the Cubist movement, worked meticulously to bring Reverdy’s verses to life through his distinct style. His collaboration with Reverdy was not merely an act of illustration but a deeply integrated artistic endeavor. This collaboration was made possible by the Louis Broder Tériade, who was instrumental in bringing such creative partnerships to fruition. Tériade's decision to commission Gris and Reverdy advanced the collaborative book form, breaking new ground in how text and image could coexist. The significance of this work lies in its ability to transcend traditional artistic boundaries. Gris’s bold compositions, coupled with Reverdy’s evocative text, invite the viewer into a world where art and literature are inextricably linked. By integrating these elements, "Au Soleil du Plafond" becomes a pivotal piece within the exhibition, illustrating how artists like Gris and Reverdy redefined the book as a holistic artistic expression.
