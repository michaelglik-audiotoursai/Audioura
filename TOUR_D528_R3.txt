Your first stop is Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers). As you stand before Joan Miró's "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers)" at the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston, position yourself to see the intricate interplay of colors in the lithographs unfold before you. From this vantage point, the harmonious fusion of vibrant colors and textures becomes evident, a testament to Miró's unique artistic language.

Joan Miró originally created a series of lithographs in 1967 to illustrate his poem Le Lézard aux plumes d'or, printed in Paris by Mourlot. For technical reasons, Miró decided to destroy the initial print run, and the lithographic stones were sanded down, preventing any second printing from the original matrices. In 1971, Miró executed a completely new set of lithographs to realize the project. Mourlot printed this newly drawn edition, which was published in Paris by Louis Broder. This collaboration marked the culmination of Broder and Miró's creative partnership, being the last project they completed together. Through Broder's efforts, the book not only captured the essence of Miró's artistry but also advanced the exhibition's thesis by highlighting how artists revolutionized the book as an art form. Printed by Mourlot Frères, a renowned Parisian lithographic studio, this work exemplifies the potential of the livre d'artiste, where text, image, and craftsmanship converge in a single volume. The Museum of Fine Arts in Boston is now home to this remarkable piece, thanks to the generous gift from Boris Fridman, who recognized its cultural significance and ensured its preservation for future generations to explore and appreciate.
