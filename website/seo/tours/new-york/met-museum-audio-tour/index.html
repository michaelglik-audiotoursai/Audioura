What you'll hear — 5 stops

  1. The Great Wave of Kanagawa

    The Great Wave of Kanagawa — one of the highlights on this tour, with its full story narrated in the Audioura app.

  2. Allegory of the Catholic Faith

    Painted between 1670 and 1672, "Allegory of the Catholic Faith" embodies a moment when the Dutch Republic, largely Protestant, was marked by religious division and control.

  3. View of Toledo

    El Greco—born Domenikos Theotokopoulos, known in Spain as “the Greek”—created View of Toledo around 1598 to 1600.

  4. Melencolia I

    Albrecht Dürer engraved Melencolia I in 1514, using a copper plate to achieve an extraordinary range of tones and textures within a sheet measuring just 9 1/2 by 7 5/16 inches.

  5. The Death of Socrates

    The philosopher’s body, draped in white, commands the center; his gesture is precise, the outstretched hand grasping the cup as his followers recoil or bow their heads.

Frequently asked questions

Does The Met do audio tours?

Open the Audioura app, search for this tour, and press play at the first stop. The app guides you from stop to stop by voice, so your phone can stay in your pocket.

How long is the Metropolitan Museum of Art audio tour?

The Metropolitan Museum of Art tour has 5 stops with about 12 minutes of narration. Because it's self-guided you can pause, linger or pick it up again whenever you like.

Is this a self-guided tour?

Yes. Audioura is a self-guided audio tour: you set your own pace and the app narrates each stop as you reach it, triggered by GPS or a tap. Triple-click your headphone button to jump between stops without looking at your phone.
