What you'll hear — 5 stops

  1. The Great Wave off Kanagawa

    The most striking feature from just a step back—directly in front of the glass—is the sweep of the wave: the curling, foam-tipped crest suspended mid-crash, poised above the tiny boats and distant Mount Fuji.

  2. The Rhinoceros

    In July 1515, Giovanni Giacomo Penni published a poem in Rome that featured the first known print of a rhinoceros, illustrated by a primitive woodcut.

  3. Library of Ashurbanipal

    This effort resulted in a library that included works on literature, medicine, rituals, and even the Epic of Gilgamesh, one of the oldest known literary works.

  4. Portland Vase

    The Portland Vase stands modest in scale but imposing in effect. Its body is smooth and cool-hued, with a surface cut with astonishing precision.

  5. Rosetta Stone

    Londoners sometimes tell of the day in 1917 when, under threat of bombs falling from the sky, the museum’s most prized objects vanished overnight.

Frequently asked questions

Does the British Museum have an audio guide?

Yes. Audioura is a self-guided audio tour: you set your own pace and the app narrates each stop as you reach it, triggered by GPS or a tap. Triple-click your headphone button to jump between stops without looking at your phone.

Is there a virtual tour of the British Museum?

Open the Audioura app, search for this tour, and press play at the first stop. The app guides you from stop to stop by voice, so your phone can stay in your pocket.

Do I need the British Museum audio app?

Audioura's tours play inside the app rather than as a separate download. Install the app, open this tour and press play — the first tour is free.
