In 1971, Louis Broder published "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," an illustrated book by Joan Miró featuring forty color lithographs. This collaboration allowed Miró, known for his vivid imagination and playful forms, to illustrate his own poems, lending the work a deeply personal touch. Broder's dedication to the project helped cement a lasting friendship with Miró, lasting until Broder's death. The work's importance is underscored by its inclusion in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston's collection, thanks to the gift of Boris Fridman. Fridman's contribution significantly enriched the museum's holdings of artist books, advancing the exhibition's argument that such collaborative ventures revolutionized the book as an art form. Joan Miró created the color lithographs for his illustrated book Le Lézard aux plumes d’or in direct collaboration with the Parisian print studio Mourlot Frères. At the workshop, Miró relied on his dedicated printer Jean Célestin to pull impressions from the stones. When technical issues arose during early production, an entire proof edition had to be abandoned before the final plates could be realized.
