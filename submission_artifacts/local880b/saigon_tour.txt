Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779785, 106.699020

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, two tall bell towers, and a statue of the Virgin Mary.

Orientation: This route spans approximately 6 km, taking you from the Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon to Thi Bar Saigon, traversing a landscape that seamlessly blends urban life with historical architecture. As you navigate the streets, you will witness how Saigon's architectural splendor and vibrant street life weave a tapestry of resilience and transformation, revealing forgotten stories and hidden connections between iconic landmarks and everyday spaces. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From the main street of Đồng Khởi, head east on Lê Duẩn, then turn right onto Công xã Paris. The cathedral will be on your left. Stand just across the street where the wide sidewalk opens onto a clear view of the cathedral’s twin bell towers. The red brick walls rise straight and steady, their warm hue softened by the afternoon light. From here, the hum of city life mingles with the faint ringing of the cathedral’s bells, calling out across the traffic and chatter.

The cathedral before you was designed by Jules Bourard and completed in 1880, during the period of French colonial administration. Originally called the Church of Saigon, it marked the French Catholic presence after their conquest of Cochinchina. Its two bell towers rise meters high, dominating the skyline with confident, deliberate symmetry. The red bricks were shipped from Marseille, each one a tangible piece of France embedded in Saigon’s streets, showing how the colonists literally imported their culture and faith. The cathedral’s neo-Romanesque style, with its stones and bricks assembled to remind visitors of European cathedrals, transplanted a piece of French identity onto Vietnamese soil.

Before this imposing structure was built, a modest wooden church stood nearby by the Charner Canal in 1863, marking the earliest attempts by the Catholic Church to serve French settlers and local converts. That wooden building was soon outgrown, leading to the decision to erect the larger stone cathedral you see today. This building became a center not only for religious services but also for community celebrations, especially during Christmas when Catholic expatriates gathered here to share traditions far from home.

In 1959, the statue of Our Lady of Peace was installed in the cathedral’s forecourt, introducing the name “Notre-Dame Cathedral.” This statue anchors the space visually and spiritually, offering a symbol of calm amid the city’s bustle. Today, the cathedral still resounds with the sound of its bells and the footsteps of worshippers, linking past and present. This place stands as a witness to how faith, colonial ambitions, and community rituals shaped Saigon’s urban heart.

Stop 2: Saigon Central Post Office

Coordinates: 10.779852, 106.699844

Type/Specialty: Historic Post Office

Specific Examples: Beautiful colonial architecture, a large interior with a vaulted ceiling, and vintage postal services.

Orientation: Standing in Paris Commune Square, you face the Saigon Central Post Office’s broad façade, its arched windows and green shutters framed by delicate ironwork.

The structure before you was completed between 1886 and 1891, a project overseen by architect Auguste Henri Vildieu with significant design input from his assistant Alfred Foulhoux. This timing places it firmly in the late 19th century, when Saigon was part of French Indochina. The building’s architecture combines Gothic, Renaissance, and French influences, an eclectic style that marries Western structural forms with Eastern decorative touches. Within this post office’s walls, a pivotal moment in Vietnam’s communication history unfolded in 1864. That year, long before this current building took shape, the first letters bearing the ‘stork’ stamp—the very first stamp issued in Vietnam—were sent from Saigon to destinations worldwide. The stork design symbolized messages carried across vast distances, connecting this city to the global postal network for the first time. This was a concrete step in linking Vietnam to international commerce and correspondence, a change that reshaped how information moved in and out of the region. Look above the main hall and you will find a portrait of Ho Chi Minh, a figure linking Vietnam’s colonial past to its modern identity. The muted scent of ink and paper mingles with the shade of green walls and ironwork, a quiet reminder that here, communication has always been both practical and symbolic.

Directions: From the post office, head south on Công xã Paris, then turn left onto Nguyễn Văn Bình. Continue straight for meters. You may hear the soft rustle of pages turning in the nearby shops. Continue to Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780564, 106.699820

Type/Specialty: Book Street

Specific Examples: Numerous bookshops, cozy reading corners, and local cafés.

Orientation: Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street is a book street in Ho Chi Minh City. It is known for numerous bookshops, cozy reading corners, and local cafés.

You are standing on Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street, a pedestrian-only cultural hub in the heart of District 1, Ho Chi Minh City. Since July 4, 2000, this street has carried the name of Mr. Nguyễn Văn Bình, a famous intellectual. The street stretches about 100 meters from Hai Ba Trung Street to Notre Dame Cathedral, nestled alongside landmarks like the Ho Chi Minh City Post Office. Here, you find over 20 uniquely designed bookstores offering books in multiple languages and genres, including literature and magazines. This street blends traditional streetscapes with cafés and green spaces, creating a new type of urban public space in Vietnam. Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street gathers book publishers from across the country and includes areas for bookstore cafés, newspapers, magazines, stationery, and book exhibitions. As you walk, you experience a true book space connecting books, bookmakers, and readers in the center of Saigon.

Directions: Head southwest for meters, and you'll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll! It is roughly 650 meters away.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.776994, 106.695305

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The grand main hall, historical exhibits, and beautiful gardens.

Orientation: Approach the broad, modernist facade set on a quiet street where the hum of nearby traffic blends with the rustle of palm leaves. The calm here belies the gravity sealed within these walls.

Construction began on 1 July 1962, replacing the previous palace that had been badly damaged by bombs dropped by dissident Republic of Vietnam Air Force pilots. President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered this new building to rise from those ruins, but he never lived to see it finished. In November 1963, before the palace’s completion, Diệm and his brother Ngô Đình Nhu were assassinated during a coup led by General Dương Văn Minh, abruptly ending their rule and shaping the nation’s turbulent course.

The smooth concrete walls and broad glass windows offer no hint of the tense decisions once made within, nor of the bunker-like rooms where leaders planned amid growing conflict. The stillness contrasts sharply with the noise that shattered the calm here on 30 April 1975, when a tank breached the gates—now a powerful symbol of transition. After reunification, this building continued as a government office until 1976, when the capital shifted to Hanoi. Today, it functions as a convention hall and museum open to the public, preserving memories in its textured walls and wide corridors. The palace stands as a witness to both the everyday and the extraordinary, a place where history’s weight still presses against the present.

Directions: Head northwest for meters, and you'll arrive at the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the stroll! It is roughly 450 meters away.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779511, 106.692090

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Photographic exhibits, military equipment displays, and personal stories from the war.

Orientation: Stand before the museum’s entrance on Võ Văn Tần Street, where the steady hum of city life presses close. Listen for the muted clatter of footsteps on the pavement and the occasional distant motorbike, sounds weaving the present with the echoes of past struggles preserved here.

You are at the War Remnants Museum, located at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Ho Chi Minh City. The museum focuses on researching, collecting, preserving, and displaying evidence of the war of aggression against Vietnam. Inside, you see photographs, artifacts, and personal stories that document the devastation of the war and its lasting impact on the Vietnamese people. Part of the museum recreates the Con Dao prison, although the real prisons are located on Con Dao itself. This museum attracts about half a million visitors every year, making it one of the most visited museums in Vietnam.

Directions: Head east for about 1.3 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at the beautiful Saigon Opera House. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776613, 106.703170

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Lavish French colonial architecture, performances of opera and ballet, and guided tours.

Orientation: This vantage lets you take in the opera house’s intricate façade up close—the ornate arches and delicate balconies tracing a silhouette against the busy city backdrop.

This is the Saigon Opera House, a stunning example of French colonial architecture designed by Félix Olivier and his colleagues, which opened in 1900. While it was originally a cultural venue for operas and ballets, from 1956 to 1967 it became the home of the unicameral National Assembly of South Vietnam. After 1967, the building housed both the Lower House and Senate until 1975, marking its shift from art to politics. In 1975, the Opera House hosted the People's Congress of Deputies of the Provisional Revolutionary Government of the Republic of South Vietnam, a pivotal moment in the city’s history. Today, as you admire its ornate façade and intricate plasterwork, remember that this building witnessed not only performances but also the dramatic political changes that shaped modern Ho Chi Minh City.

Directions: Head west for meters, and you'll arrive at Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building). Enjoy the stroll! It is roughly 250 meters away.

Stop 7: Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building)

Coordinates: 10.776543, 106.700910

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, beautifully landscaped gardens, and iconic nighttime illumination.

Orientation: The grand colonial structure stretches horizontally, its rectangular windows and sculpted cornices inviting a closer look at the craftsmanship. Notice the trimmed gardens around the base, where the statue of a man teaching a child stands quietly amid the green.

You are standing in front of the Ho Chi Minh City Hall, also known as the People's Committee Building, a key administrative building in the heart of the city. This building was constructed between 1898 and 1909 during the French colonial period. Originally, it served as the City Hall but now houses the office of the city's Chairman of the People's Committee, the People's Council, and other government departments. In the garden courtyard, you can see a statue of Ho Chi Minh teaching a child, which was placed here after the building became the People's Committee Building. This site is recognized as one of Saigon's iconic landmarks and is surrounded by some of the city's best modernist architecture.

Directions: Head west for meters, and you'll find yourself at Café Apartments, 42 Nguyễn Huệ. Enjoy your stroll! It is roughly 650 meters away.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775263, 106.706880

Type/Specialty: Waterfront

Specific Examples: Scenic river views, boat tours, and bustling waterfront activity.

Orientation: Stand close to the edge of Bạch Đằng Pier, where the wide expanse of the Saigon River opens before you. To your left, the smooth hulls of boats bob steadily, moored for departure, their engines humming lightly.

Bạch Đằng Pier has long served as a vital boat quay and ferry terminal, connecting the bustling city centre with Thủ Thiêm Pier across the water. The steady rhythm of arrivals and departures shows its important role in Ho Chi Minh City’s transportation network. Each day, commuters, traders, and tourists pass through, linking the urban fabric to the enduring lifeline of the Saigon River.

Beyond its function as a transit point, Bạch Đằng Pier is also the departure site for tours to the Củ Chi Tunnels, a complex of underground passages used during the Vietnam War. From here, visitors board boats that carry them away from the city’s surface bustle and into the subterranean labyrinth where Viet Cong fighters once maneuvered. These tunnels tell a story of strategy and survival, offering a stark contrast to the vibrant life above. This journey connects the riverfront’s geography to one of the most tangible wartime legacies, framing Bạch Đằng Pier as a link between everyday urban movement and historical remembrance.

As boats glide smoothly from the pontoon, the river’s current and the scent of water mixed with faint diesel in the air serve as reminders that this place remains a conduit for both commerce and the stories that shape the city’s modern identity. After passing the café apartments at 42 Nguyễn Huệ, you can see how the city’s layers unfold here—from residential life to historical embarkation—all beside the steady flow of the Saigon River.
