Caty Mui Ne Resort to Ta Cu Mountain - Driving Tour
Tour-Category: driving

Stop 1: Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip

Coordinates: 10.981999, 108.251183

Type/Specialty: Coastal scenery and local fishing culture

You are standing near Ham Tien Beach, known for its swaying coconut palms and blue waters. Since the mid-1990s, this area has gradually transformed into a resort destination. Mui Ne ward has two main beaches, Ganh Beach and Suoi Nuoc Beach, both featuring some resorts, shops, and restaurants.

Directions: Drive west for about 17.5 kilometers, and you’ll reach Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River. Enjoy the scenic ride!

Stop 2: Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River

Coordinates: 10.9281, 108.1003

Type/Specialty: City and river views

Look for the dance of daily commerce here: market stalls close by, clusters of locals navigating narrow lanes beside the water, and the steady rhythm of river life unfolding beneath broad skies. The name “Phan Thiết” carries a lineage traced back to the 15th century, linked to the Cham Prince Po Thit, who governed this coastal region as a military leader. Po Thit, brother of Princess Po Sah Inư and son of King Par Ra Chăm Chanh—also called Trà Chanh—held a strategic fortification known as Camp Po Thit. Over the centuries, the Vietnamese adapted the name for their own tongue, pronouncing it “Phan Thiết,” thus preserving the imprint of Cham authority in the very identity of the city that now flourishes along these riverbanks. This river, once named the Phan River at the founding of Phan Thiet, stretches approximately 7.2 kilometers as it carves through the town. Its waters have long proven vital, threading together the livelihoods that depend on fishing and trade. During the French colonial period, the river’s estuary marked a division: on one side lay the wards encircling the Ca Ty River’s mouth; on the other, the coastal villages of Bình Hưng, Lạc Đạo, Đức Thắng, and Hưng Long.

Directions: Continue driving west from Phan Thiet for about 10 minutes until you see the dragon fruit fields lining the road. The vibrant pink and green hues are striking against the landscape. Continue to Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road.

Stop 3: Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road

Coordinates: 10.9350, 108.1090

Type/Specialty: Agricultural tours and fruit tasting

You are in Binh Thuan, the largest dragon fruit-growing region in Vietnam, often called the country's Dragon Kingdom. Vietnam is the only country in Southeast Asia that cultivates dragon fruit on a commercial scale and exports it. Thousands of local farmers in Binh Thuan grow dragon fruit thanks to the sunny, dry climate here. Dragon fruit is one of the many tropical fruits widely grown in this province, contributing to its strong agricultural reputation.

Directions: Continue to Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends.

Stop 4: Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends

Type/Specialty: Natural beauty and cultural significance

You are about 40 minutes away from Ta Cu Mountain, located in Ham Thuan Nam District. When you arrive, you take a modern cable car to explore the mountain. This mountain is known for having one of the biggest and most unique statues in Southeast Asia.

Directions: Continue to The cable car ascent.

Stop 5: The cable car ascent

Type/Specialty: Scenic cable car ride

The cables stretch taut into the sky, disappearing into mist as you rise higher. With every meter gained, the quiet deepens, and you feel yourself moving between two very different worlds.

Directions: Continue to Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda.

Stop 6: Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda

Coordinates: 10.813277, 107.894085

Type/Specialty: Buddhist temple and cultural site

You are standing at Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda, also known as the Mountain Pagoda, located in Thuan Nam Town, Binh Thuan province. You can see the large concrete statue of the reclining Buddha here, which was built in 1963 by sculptor Truong Dinh Y and measures 49 meters long. Today, you can reach the pagoda by a modern cable car that takes you over the green forest canopy to the mountain top. The pagoda has been restored over time and now appears nearly new, welcoming visitors and pilgrims alike. Local legend ties Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda to healing associated with Empress Dowager Tu Du, a story told by locals rather than documented history.

Directions: Continue to The Reclining Buddha.

Stop 7: The Reclining Buddha

Type/Specialty: Cultural landmark

You are standing at the Reclining Buddha on Ta Cu Mountain, Vietnam. It is the biggest reclining Buddha statue in Vietnam. Around you, the mountain is covered with lush green forest and beautiful scenery. The Reclining Buddha Pagoda here has several worship halls, three entrances, intricate animal sculptures, and colorful paintings of deities. The site is open all year round for visitors like you.

Directions: Continue to The summit views and the drive home.

Stop 8: The summit views and the drive home

Type/Specialty: Panoramic views

The road beneath glistens faintly in the sun, hinting at the journey that awaits on the drive home. This tension between preservation and progress is etched into the land visible from here—where the quiet mountain meets the restless sea.
