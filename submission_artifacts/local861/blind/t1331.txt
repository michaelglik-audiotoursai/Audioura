This exquisite instrument is part of a larger narrative that intertwines the history of the palace itself, built in the first half of the seventeenth century, which was bequeathed to the city of Nice in 1901 to serve as a museum. Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle) is a painted panel from the late sixteenth century, now displayed within the Palais Lascaris—a seventeenth-century aristocratic building in Nice that today houses musical instruments. The panel’s precise materials and artist are unrecorded in the museum’s documentation, but it stands as a rare visual testimony to Raquel, a figure whose historical presence is less known but whose role in politics was critical during her era. Created at a time when Europe was shaped by complex political and religious tensions, artworks like this often served as subtle affirmations of status or political allegiance. The piece reflects the period’s stylistic tendencies toward restrained color palettes and precise figuration, techniques that conveyed dignity and gravitas without overt dramatization.

In 1902, Gabriel Fauré performed his piano compositions in the salon of Palais Lascaris, a space where this panel now shares silent witness to centuries of cultural layering. The panel’s quiet presence in this former aristocratic residence, now a repository for musical histories, invites reflection on the unexpected intersections between visual art and the sounds that once filled these rooms. Raquel’s gaze, steady and reserved, resonates amid the echoes of performances once held here, linking the stillness of paint with the flow of music through time.

This tour highlights the intersection of visual art and music within the historical context of Palais Lascaris. The presence of works like Raquel invites us to reflect on how art and sound intertwine, shaping our understanding of cultural heritage over time.
