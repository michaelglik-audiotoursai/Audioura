You are about to explore the Palais Lascaris in Nice, a historic palace transformed into a museum dedicated to the enchanting world of music. The Palais Lascaris not only preserves a diverse array of historical instruments but also reflects the cultural shifts that have shaped music through the centuries. Your first stop is Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle). The cool hush of the Palais Lascaris draws you to a light-filled landing, where “Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle)” catches the eye at the transition between salons. Pause several steps back to take in the entire painted panel—only from here does the full scale and placement reveal how the figure was meant to preside over the passage of guests, bridging two centuries of changing taste.

“Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle)” stands as a witness to the shifting artistic ideals at the close of the 1500s, poised between lingering Renaissance clarity and the swelling energy of the Baroque. The panneau, created by an unknown artist in the last years of the sixteenth century, was painted with the mixed technique then emerging in Italy and southern France: tempera laid beneath delicate early oil glazes. Each brushstroke anchors her in her time: hands gently parted, gaze turned not outward but inward, she projects self-possession touched with expectancy. The story of this panel is inseparable from the walls that hold it. The Vintimille-Lascaris family commissioned the grandeur of their Palais in the seventeenth century, seeking to assert their status among Nice’s elite; by preserving and displaying works like Raquel, they attached their name and prestige not only to music and lavish salons but to the evolving art of their day. In Raquel’s enigmatic presence, the Palais Lascaris preserves a visual memory of a moment when art, music, and society all held their breath on the edge of a new era.

This tour highlights the interplay between art, music, and societal aspirations as exemplified by works like Raquel. Understanding this connection allows us to appreciate how cultural expressions reflect and shape the identities of their time.
