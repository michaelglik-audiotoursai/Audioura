Stop 1: Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip

The coast here stretches for approximately 50 kilometers and has developed into a resort destination since the mid-1990s. Mũi Né ward, borders the South China Sea and includes two beaches, Ganh Beach and Suoi Nuoc Beach, with resorts and shops.

Directions: Head west for about 16.7 kilometers, and you’ll arrive at Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River. Enjoy the drive!

Stop 2: Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River

Coordinates: 10.9332, 108.1062

Type/Specialty: City and river

Phan Thiet’s name itself carries the imprint of its past. In the 15th century, this area was governed militarily by the Cham Prince Po Thit, brother to Princess Po Sah Inư and son of King Par Ra Chăm Chanh, also known as Trà Chanh. Over time, the Vietnamese adapted the name to their own tongue, pronouncing it "Phan Thiết." This linguistic transformation ties the city directly to its Cham heritage and the complex cultural shifts that shaped the region. The Ca Ty River, originally known as the Phan River when the town was established, stretches about 7.2 kilometers. One remarkable moment in Phan Thiet’s recent history unfolded in 1995, when a total solar eclipse cast its shadow over the city.

Directions: Continue south on Vo Van Kiet Street, then follow the signs onto National Route 1A. You’ll see rows of dragon fruit plants lining the roadside, creating a stunning landscape. Continue to Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road — it is roughly 24.7 km away.

Stop 3: Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road

Coordinates: 10.7591, 107.9663

Type/Specialty: City and river

You are in Binh Thuan, one of Vietnam's most famous dragon fruit-growing regions. The province benefits from a sunny, dry climate that supports thousands of local farmers. Binh Thuan is actually the country's largest area for dragon fruit cultivation. People often call this place Vietnam's 'Dragon Kingdom' because of its extensive plantations. Vietnam is the only country in Southeast Asia that grows dragon fruit on a commercial scale. Most of this commercial cultivation happens right here in Binh Thuan. Besides dragon fruit, the region also grows other tropical fruits like mangosteen, mango, and orange.

Directions: Take National Route 1A south, then turn left onto Ta Cu Mountain Road. As you ascend, you might catch a glimpse of the majestic Buddha statue peeking through the trees. Continue to Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends — it is roughly 23.1 km away.

Stop 4: Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends

Coordinates: 10.928631, 108.088493

Type/Specialty: Mountain and cultural site

You are at Ta Cu Mountain, an extinct volcano located in Ham Thuan Nam District, about 40 minutes and Phan Thiet City. This mountain is the most famous spiritual attraction in Binh Thuan, known for spiritual practice atop its summit. You can take a modern cable car to reach the top of Ta Cu Mountain, where two famous pagodas stand: Linh Son Truong Tho and Long Doan. Here, you find the longest reclining Buddha statue in Southeast Asia, one of the biggest and most unique statues in the region. Ta Cu Mountain is located in Thuan Nam Town within Ham Thuan Nam District, making it accessible for visitors from nearby cities.

Directions: Drive north for about a minute, and you'll reach the cable car ascent. Enjoy the ride!

Stop 6: Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda

Coordinates: 10.813277, 107.894085

Type/Specialty: Buddhist temple

You are standing at Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda, also known as the Mountain Pagoda, located on Ta Cu Mountain in Thuan Nam town, Ham Thuan Nam district, Binh Thuan province. This pagoda was founded by Master Tran Huu Duc at the end of the 19th century and received royal recognition from Emperor Tu Duc after Master Duc successfully treated Empress Dowager Tu Du. The emperor named this pagoda Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda as a sign of gratitude for the healing. Behind the main hall, you can see a reclining Buddha statue that is 49 meters long and 11 meters high, installed during the 1963 restoration. The pagoda is situated at an elevation of 400 meters and is one of two pagodas on Ta Cu Mountain, making it a popular pilgrimage destination. After Master Huu Duc passed away in 1887, another pagoda called Linh Son Long Doan, or Lower Pagoda, was built below, while this one is known as the Upper Pagoda. Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda is steeped in a legend of healing that is still passed down among the locals.

Directions: Head northeast for about 25 kilometers, and you'll arrive at The Reclining Buddha. Enjoy the drive!

Stop 7: The Reclining Buddha

Coordinates: 10.928631, 108.088493

Type/Specialty: Cultural landmark

You are standing at the Reclining Buddha on Ta Cu Mountain, Vietnam. It is the biggest reclining Buddha statue in Vietnam. Around you, the Reclining Buddha Pagoda features several worship halls, three entrances, intricate animal sculptures, and colorful paintings of deities. The mountain is covered in lush green forest and offers beautiful scenery. The site is open all year round and can be reached by cable car or a 1.5 to 2 hour uphill hike if you choose to trek.

Directions: After enjoying the views, retrace your steps back to the cable car station. As you descend, take a moment to appreciate the changing colors of the landscape. Continue to The summit views and the drive home.
