This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, beautiful stained glass windows, and a serene atmosphere.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. This tour will take you from the iconic Cathedral to the bustling Thi Bar, covering an approximate straight-line distance of 5 km through a city characterized by its dynamic street life and colonial architecture. As you explore, you will uncover the layers of Ho Chi Minh City's turbulent history and cultural evolution, revealing a place that continually reinvents itself while preserving the echoes of its past. Your first stop is Cathedral. From the central square, head southeast on Lê Duẩn Street for about 5 minutes until you reach the Cathedral. Stand near the center of Paris Commune Square, facing the towering red-brick structure rising just ahead. The square’s open space allows for a clear view of the cathedral’s soaring bell towers and the white granite statue at its threshold.

The Saigon Notre-Dame Cathedral’s distinctive red bricks come from Marseille, imported to create its unmistakable hue. These bricks, rare in local architecture, give the cathedral a warm, earthy tone that contrasts with the glossy glass and steel of Ho Chi Minh City’s financial towers. Their rough texture absorbs sunlight differently through the day, casting a glow that shifts from soft pinks in the morning to burnt orange as evening approaches. The building’s neo-Romanesque style, marked by rounded arches and twin bell towers, anchors this historic core of Saigon, now Ho Chi Minh City, which in 2025 is Vietnam’s most populous and economically powerful urban center. In front of the cathedral stands a 4.6-meter tall statue of Mary, carved from a single block of white granite weighing eight tons. Installed in 1959, this statue’s smooth, pale surface contrasts sharply with the cathedral’s weathered bricks. The statue commands the square, its presence a focal point for local rituals and quiet moments amid the city’s bustle. Its weight and scale speak to the effort invested in shaping this spiritual landmark decades after the cathedral’s completion in 1880. The square itself functions as the city’s kilometre zero, the exact spot from which distances across Ho Chi Minh City are measured. As the city’s busiest international transport hub pulses nearby, the cathedral remains a steady presence, its imported bricks and monumental statue a quiet echo of Saigon’s layered identity.

Directions: Enjoy the stroll! Continue to Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic post office

Specific Examples: Gorgeous interior with high ceilings, vintage postal services, and a large map of Vietnam.

Orientation: The building’s large arched windows and intricate ironwork invite your gaze upward, where the interplay of light and shadow reveals the fusion of styles crafted within its walls.

The Saigon Central Post Office, completed between 1886 and 1891, embodies a moment when French Indochina sought to imprint its presence through architecture. Though Eiffel’s name is often associated with the site, it was Foulhoux who shaped the current structure, blending Gothic, Renaissance, and French stylistic elements into an eclectic whole. This architectural hybridity reflects the layered ambitions of the colonial era, where European design met local context. Step inside, and the high vaulted ceilings stretch above you, while the sound of your footsteps mingles with the faint rustle of paper and the distant murmur of postal clerks. The interior is spacious yet intimate, dominated by a vast map of Vietnam that once served as a vital tool for communication across the region. Amid the chaos of conflict, this building maintained the fragile threads of connection, linking families and armies through the written word.

Directions: From the Post Office, head southwest on Công xã Paris, then turn left onto Lê Thánh Tôn Street. Continue for about 5 minutes until you reach Book Street. Enjoy the smell of coffee wafting from nearby cafes as you approach.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural street market

Specific Examples: Quaint book stalls, cozy reading corners, and local coffee shops.

Orientation: Book Street is a cultural street market in Ho Chi Minh City. It is known for quaint book stalls, cozy reading corners, and local coffee shops.

You are standing on Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street, which officially opened on January 9, 2016. This narrow, tree-lined lane stretches 100 meters from Hai Bà Trưng Street to Notre Dame Cathedral. Along the street, you see 20 book stalls, small bookstores, and areas for newspapers, magazines, and stationery. Many book publishers from across Vietnam gather here to showcase their work. The street offers spaces for old book exchanges, book exhibitions, and organized displays of newspapers and pictures by topic. You can find a variety of books here, including literature, politics, society, science, and culture. This street creates a connection between books, bookmakers, and readers in the heart of Ho Chi Minh City.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you'll find yourself at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical site

Specific Examples: The grand architecture, historical war rooms, and lush gardens.

Orientation: Pause just outside the main gates, where the broad driveway and the towering walls frame the palace’s vast silhouette.

You stand just outside the main gates of the Independence Palace, its broad driveway and towering walls framing the vast silhouette before you. This palace was completed on 31 October 1966, designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ in a Vietnamese modernist style to replace the old Norodom Palace. Construction began on 1 July 1962, ordered by President Ngô Đình Diệm after bombing damaged the previous palace, but he never saw its completion, having been assassinated in a coup in November 1963. The calm exterior of the palace hides the tension that once filled these halls, especially on 30 April 1975, when a tank from the Liberation Army of South Vietnam crashed through these gates. That moment marked the Fall of Saigon and the end of the Vietnam War, changing the fate of the city forever. You can still see traces on the ground where metal met stone, a silent reminder of that historic day. The Independence Palace stands as a threshold between conflict and peace, its gates once shattered now open to the rhythms of the city.

Directions: Head northwest for about 500 meters, and you’ll reach the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Powerful exhibits on the Vietnam War, military vehicles, and poignant photography.

The War Remnants Museum opened its doors in 1975, first known as the Exhibition House for U.S. and Puppet Crimes. The museum occupies the former United States Information Agency building, a detail that anchors it firmly in the complex post-war landscape of Saigon. Here, the government of Ho Chi Minh City took this symbolic space—once a projection of American influence—and transformed it into a repository of wartime testimony. The museum’s original mission was to display evidence of the conflicts that scarred Vietnam, particularly the Vietnam War and the earlier First Indochina War. It does not shy away from the brutal reality of conflict, presenting its subject matter with unflinching clarity. This lineage of public exposure shaped how the museum framed its collections, grounding its purpose in the documentation of specific acts and their consequences, rather than vague notions of war’s horror. The museum’s exhibits ripple outward from this narrative, anchoring the tour’s arc in the raw material of history’s turning points.

Directions: Head east for about 1.2 kilometers, and you'll arrive at the stunning Opera House. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural venue

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, live performances, and a vibrant atmosphere.

Orientation: Stand where the broad sidewalk opens onto Lam Sơn Square, facing the elegant facade of the Saigon Opera House. The low hum of traffic mixes with occasional bursts of conversation, while the intricate ironwork and delicate arches of the building draw the eye upward.

Its flamboyant style, with ornate balconies and sculpted details, echoes the grand opera houses that rose in Hanoi and Haiphong during the same period. For over a decade, the chamber where laws were debated and passed filled these ornate walls instead of opera arias. Later, when the legislature split, the Opera House hosted both the Lower House and the Senate, transforming this place of artistic grace into the political heart of a divided nation. Today, the weight of those gatherings lingers in the polished wood and gilded moldings, a sharp contrast to the performances echoing here now. The faint scent of aged wood and the distant murmurs of modern performances remind us that this place still lives between past and present. The Opera House was not used again as a theatre until 1976.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you'll find yourself at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government building

Specific Examples: Beautiful colonial architecture, well-maintained gardens, and photo opportunities.

Orientation: The wide steps lead up to a grand entrance where government officials come and go.

The building before you was completed in 1908 and opened in 1909, designed by Fernand Gardè during the French colonial era. After colonial rule ended, it was repurposed by South Vietnam’s government to serve as the city hall, anchoring local governance within its stately walls. Today, it holds the offices of the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee and Council, linking the pulse of government with the daily flow of the city in a place where history and daily life meet. In the garden courtyard, a statue of Ho Chi Minh teaching a child stands as a quiet symbol of transformation. It reflects the city’s journey from its days under French control to a new era of Vietnamese leadership and independence. The statue’s presence here turns the space into a living dialogue between the past and present, where the formal government functions inside meet the hopes of future generations represented in bronze.

Directions: Head southeast for about 400 meters, and you'll find yourself at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic waterfront

Specific Examples: Beautiful views of the Saigon River, boat tours, and a relaxing atmosphere.

Orientation: Stand on the wide concrete apron of Bạch Đằng Pier, facing east towards the Saigon River. Look for the patchwork of moored boats clustered near the riverbank alongside the smooth metal gangways.

Bạch Đằng Pier stands here as a place tied closely to movement and transit, once serving as a historical boat quay, its wooden docks whispering of countless arrivals and departures. Though this pier now greets modern vessels, it carries itself with a quiet purpose: it functions as the embarkation point for tours heading to the Củ Chi Tunnels. These tunnels embody a significant chapter in the Vietnam War, where complex underground pathways were dug to aid resistance efforts. Travelers who set out from here trace that physical and historical journey, connecting with the wartime strategies crafted long ago beneath the soil. The shore braces a pathway a short distance behind the pier, familiar in passing if you remember the bursting verticals of Café Apartments 42, where city life intensifies in layered, reused spaces. Here, Bạch Đằng Pier offers a calm contrast—a fleeting pause beside the water where the texture of waves contrasts with the solidity of urban change. This place’s role frames movement between land and water, city and history, tranquility and purpose. One story anchors this place unmistakably: travelers depart here for tours to the Củ Chi Tunnels. These underground networks, carved meticulously during the Vietnam War, were central to guerrilla strategy and survival. Bạch Đằng Pier is the literal gateway to that history, where visitors prepare to descend into the labyrinth that shaped resistance against far larger forces. Another historical dimension of the pier is its continuous function in river transport. Although subsequent urban development redefined its use, the pier remains a point marking the flow of people and commerce along the river’s edge. Here, the slow rhythm of the water and the pier’s low hum create a meeting place: a point where the city’s pulse slows briefly, held by the steady beat of history, travel, and the river’s steady pull.

Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: bar

Orientation: Thi Bar is a bar located at 183 Đề Thám, Bến Thành, Hồ Chí Minh 70000 in Ho Chi Minh City.

You are standing outside Thi Bar, which has been open since 2010 in the heart of Saigon's tourist area. The bar welcomes everyone and features live music every night from 10pm until late. You can enjoy local bands covering hit songs, and you can even request your favorite song from the friendly staff. Thi Bar is one of the few real gay bars in the city and has a vibrant atmosphere. It opens daily from 7pm but is closed on Tuesdays, except on holidays. The bar offers limited food options, so you might want to eat beforehand or ask if you can bring outside food.
