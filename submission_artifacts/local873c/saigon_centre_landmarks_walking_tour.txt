Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779785, 106.699020

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, beautiful stained glass windows, and a peaceful garden.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From the intersection of Lê Duẩn and Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, head west on Lê Duẩn for about 300 meters, then turn left onto Công xã Paris. The cathedral will be on your right. Standing in Paris Commune Square, the roundabout encircling the Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon, the cathedral’s twin bell towers rise conspicuously to 58 meters, framed by the bustle of Lê Duẩn Boulevard and Nguyễn Du Street. From this vantage point, the red bricks catch sunlight unevenly, their worn surfaces revealing the passage of time.

The cathedral here was established in 1863 and completed in 1880, designed by Jules Bourard. It was constructed by French colonists as a symbol of their religious influence and colonial presence in Vietnam. The red bricks that compose its walls were shipped all the way from Marseille, a detail that ties this building materially to France. In 1959, the space in front of the cathedral changed when a statue of the Virgin Mary was installed. This statue, dedicated as Our Lady of Peace, became a vital focus for Vietnamese Catholics. Over time, it attracted gatherings of pilgrims who came to pray and mark significant religious dates, weaving the rhythms of faith into the city’s daily life. The statue’s presence transformed the forecourt from a mere plaza into a living place of devotion. A striking episode occurred in 2005 when the Virgin Mary statue reportedly shed tears. This event drew thousands of believers and curious visitors alike, turning the cathedral’s courtyard into a temporary pilgrimage site. The tearful statue intensified the spiritual pulse of the square, connecting centuries of colonial architecture with contemporary religious experience. Encircling the cathedral in Paris Commune Square stands a bronze statue of Pierre Pigneau de Béhaine, a French Catholic priest, alongside Prince Nguyễn Phúc Cảnh. Erected in 1903 by the colonial government, this statue recalls a historical relationship: Pigneau de Béhaine’s role in advocating for the young prince’s mission to France, an episode that shaped early Franco-Vietnamese ties. This statue anchors the square’s identity, named Place Pigneau de Béhaine during colonial times, offering a tangible link between Saigon’s spiritual and political past. The cathedral’s presence here, facing down the square and its statues, reverberates with the layered identities of Ho Chi Minh City — a space where faith, history, and daily urban life converge beneath the soaring bell towers. Just ahead, the echoes of another French architectural landmark await, continuing this story of cultural intersections.

Stop 2: Saigon Central Post Office

Coordinates: 10.779852, 106.699844

Type/Specialty: Architectural Landmark

Specific Examples: Beautiful colonial architecture, a large map of Vietnam, and local postal services.

Orientation: The post office occupies the side of this small roundabout along Lê Duẩn Boulevard and Nguyễn Du Street, its broad arched windows and ironwork inviting you to enter.

The Saigon Central Post Office, completed between 1886 and 1891, was designed by architect Auguste Henri Vildieu with the assistance of Marie-Alfred Foulhoux. The building stands beside the cathedral in what is now known as Paris Commune Square, or Công trường Công xã Paris, which marks the city’s kilometre zero. This location has long been a convergence point for religious, administrative, and civic life, shaping the center of Saigon for over a century. More than just a postal hub, the post office serves as a repository of historical layers, linking colonial design, global communication, and the city’s evolving identity. One tangible episode anchors this place in the wider world of communication.

Directions: From the post office, head south on Công xã Paris for about 200 meters, then turn right onto Nguyễn Văn Bình. The book street will be ahead. Look out for the colorful book covers lining the stalls. Continue to Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street.

Stop 3: Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780564, 106.699820

Type/Specialty: Cultural Street

Specific Examples: Numerous bookstores, cozy reading spots, and outdoor book stalls.

Orientation: Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street is a cultural street in Ho Chi Minh City. It is known for numerous bookstores, cozy reading spots, and outdoor book stalls. Take a moment at the entrance to get your bearings before you go in.

You are standing on Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street, named on July 4, 2000, after the famous intellectual Nguyễn Văn Bình. This pedestrian-only street stretches 100 meters from Hai Ba Trung Street to the Notre Dame Cathedral, right in the heart of Ho Chi Minh City. Here, you find over 20 uniquely designed bookstores offering thousands of books in multiple languages and genres. The street blends traditional streetscapes with cafés and green spaces, creating a vibrant urban public space. Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street is a cultural hub that gathers book publishers from across the country, featuring bookstores, cafés, and exhibition areas. You are just opposite the Independence Palace and about 3 kilometers from Bui Vien Street, making this a central spot for book lovers.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.776994, 106.695305

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The grand main hall, war rooms, and beautiful gardens.

Orientation: Standing before the Independence Palace, your view is framed by its broad north gates, where the texture of aged metal meets the smoothness of stone. The air carries a faint hum from the surrounding city streets of Ho Chi Minh City, blending with birdsong from the gardens nearby. The palace’s clean, modernist lines rise against the sky, inviting a close look at the building where history pivoted sharply.

On 27 February 1962, the palace suffered severe damage during a bombing attack, a violent interruption that underscored the ongoing turmoil of the era. This event accelerated plans to rebuild and modernize the palace, culminating in the structure you see today. The palace’s spacious halls and gardens have since stood as the backdrop to pivotal moments that shaped the nation. The sight of a Liberation Army tank smashing into the palace grounds is etched into memory, signaling the close of decades of conflict and the reunification of Vietnam.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779511, 106.692090

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Exhibits on the Vietnam War, military vehicles, and impactful photographs.

Orientation: The low hum of city life filters through the trees outside, while the distant murmur of traffic blends with the occasional clatter of footsteps on pavement. Ahead, the museum’s entrance beckons quietly, framed by tanks and artillery pieces that anchor the building firmly in the realities of conflict.

The War Remnants Museum preserves the nation’s record of resistance and struggle, a story etched in images, objects, and testimonies that refuse to fade with time. Created immediately after 1975, the museum was a deliberate act by the Vietnamese government to shape historical memory. It was designed to preserve the heroic legacy of the nation’s resistance, detailing both the brutality inflicted and the resilience that followed. Walking through its halls, visitors confront photographs that capture individual suffering and resolve, bringing forward the voices of those who endured the conflict. Among the remnants of war machines and faded photographs, one senses the layers beneath Ho Chi Minh City’s bustling modernity—the scars beneath the skyline. The War Remnants Museum holds onto these traces, preserving memory amid the flow of daily life. Its founding was more than a gesture of commemoration; the museum became a site where history is laid bare, not as abstract fact but as lived experience, shaping how future generations understand the conflict and its impact on the nation. Here, the weight of decades rests visibly in the presence of artifacts and images that testify to a turbulent past.

Stop 6: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776613, 106.703170

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, ballet performances, and concerts.

Orientation: Stand facing the Saigon Opera House from Lam Sơn Square, where its façade commands attention with a blend of French colonial flair and intricate design. The ornate balconies and arched windows frame this 500-seat hall, inviting you to look up and trace the delicate stonework that whispers of its European roots.

Imagine, instead of music and ballet, the echoes of political debate filling these walls, where lawmakers shaped the policies of a nation in a turbulent era. The transformation from cultural venue to legislative chamber shifted its purpose dramatically, embedding it in Vietnam’s mid-20th-century history. After that intense period, the building returned to its original function only in 1976, reclaiming its stage for the arts once again. The stone and stucco you see now carry the weight of those political assemblies and artistic performances alike. It was opened in 1900.

Stop 7: Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building)

Coordinates: 10.776543, 106.700910

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Impressive façade, beautiful gardens, and historical significance.

Orientation: The ironwork of the balconies and window frames catch the sunlight, their delicate curves contrasting with the building’s imposing scale. The manicured gardens to the sides lend a quiet softness, while the distant murmur of traffic and hawkers blend with a faint scent of tropical blooms carried on the breeze.

The building was officially opened in 1909 by the French Indochina governors-general, who named it Hôtel de ville, or municipal hall. Its purpose was to serve as a center of administration and authority—a physical statement of governance in what was then a key colonial city. The structure’s ornate European design, carefully balanced with practical function, reflects the city’s layered identity, blending French architectural tradition with the bustling life of Saigon. Since 1975, after the communist conquest of Saigon, the building has housed the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee and People’s Council offices. The solidity of its stone steps and the faint echo of footsteps recall its ongoing role as a place where governance meets daily life. Beyond this historic seat of power, the city’s commerce continues, with river and canal routes weaving stories of trade and movement through Ho Chi Minh City’s dynamic heart.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.7752, 106.6941

Type/Specialty: Riverfront Area

Specific Examples: Scenic views of the Saigon River, boat tours, and evening lights.

Orientation: From the bustling energy of 42 Nguyễn Huệ and its café apartments, walk toward the Saigon River’s edge where Bạch Đằng Pier unfolds.

Bạch Đằng Pier was historically a boat quay—an essential stop where goods and passengers embarked and disembarked along the Saigon River, following routes that once connected the city to its wider region. Later, it became a ferry terminal, notably linking to Thủ Thiêm Pier across the river. Today, the pier’s role extends beyond transport, serving as a departure point for tours to the Củ Chi Tunnels, an extensive network of underground passages. Visitors who set off from here follow in the footsteps of soldiers and villagers who used these tunnels for shelter, supply routes, and surprise attacks. This connection grounds the pier not only in the rhythms of river traffic but also in the tangible history of conflict and resilience.
