Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779785, 106.699020

Type/Specialty: Historic Catholic Cathedral

Specific Examples: Stained glass windows, French colonial architecture, the iconic statue of the Virgin Mary.

Orientation: The Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon rises in striking contrast to the urban hum, its red bricks imported all the way from Marseille marking a tangible connection to French colonial ambition. Its two bell towers reach high, dominating the city skyline with a distinctly European silhouette amid tropical greenery. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From the main street, head east on Nguyễn Thị Minh Khai, turn left onto Lê Duẩn, and continue straight until you reach the cathedral. Standing just off the busy square that links Saigon’s central post office to the cathedral, the best vantage point is from the broad sidewalk facing the cathedral’s twin bell towers.

The cathedral’s name, Notre-Dame, only came into official use in 1959. The sound of bells ringing during mass echoes faintly even now, blending with the distant chatter of vendors and the occasional rumble of motorbikes, a reminder that this is a living place of faith amid the city’s daily rhythms. This cathedral stands as a vivid marker of layers of history: the wooden church of 1863 began a spiritual presence that the grand stone structure completed by 1880 transformed into a lasting symbol of French colonial influence. The imported bricks and soaring towers physically embody that era’s ambitions and its imprint on Ho Chi Minh City’s identity.

Directions: Exit the cathedral and walk back to Lê Duẩn, then turn right and head south until you reach the post office on your left. The intricate architecture of the post office will catch your eye. Continue to Saigon Central Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Saigon Central Post Office

Coordinates: 10.779852, 106.699844

Type/Specialty: Historic Post Office

Specific Examples: Beautiful vaulted ceilings, antique wooden counters, and a large map of Vietnam.

Orientation: Stand near its grand entrance, beneath the wide iron arches and beneath the curved glass roof, where the bustle of the city blends with the steady clatter of typewriters and the murmur of letters being sorted inside.

It was designed by architect Auguste Henri Villedieu with the assistance of Marie-Alfred Foulhoux. This duo created a building that combines Western architectural forms—namely Gothic and Renaissance influences—with delicate Eastern decorative elements. The result is an eclectic style that marks the aspirations and tensions of colonial Saigon, where French order and Vietnamese tradition met in stone and iron. Look up as you enter: the vaulted ceiling, painted in soft hues, curves above you, supported by ironwork that allows natural light to spread evenly through the space. The faint scent of old wood and ink lingers, a sensory echo of the countless letters dispatched here. A story anchors this building’s identity: the earlier post office, built from 1860 to 1863, was often mistaken as the work of Gustave Eiffel, whose name still clings to the place in popular imagination. Yet it was Villedieu and Foulhoux who shaped the current structure, a fact noted by the 1891 journal Architecte constructeur. The inauguration ceremony, held on July 14, was delayed until the return of the Governor General, underscoring the building’s role as an emblem of colonial governance and modern communication. Inside, a large portrait of Ho Chi Minh hangs, an unexpected presence amid the French architectural elegance. This juxtaposition reflects Saigon’s layered identity—a city where colonial imprints exist alongside the assertion of Vietnamese sovereignty.

Directions: From the post office, head north on Lê Duẩn, then take a left onto Nguyễn Huệ, and continue straight until you see the opera house on your right. The lively atmosphere of the street adds to the experience. Continue to Saigon Opera House — it is roughly 500 meters away.

Stop 3: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776613, 106.703170

Type/Specialty: Historic Opera House

Specific Examples: Baroque architecture, lavish interior decor, and performances of ballet and opera.

The Saigon Opera House opened on January 1, 1900, the work of French architect Félix Olivier. It was designed in the flamboyant style, a late phase architecture, evident in the delicate scrollwork, floral motifs, and the lavishly carved pediments that crown its windows and doors. Originally seating, it now holds 468, its interior trimmed with velvet and gilded plasterwork, though this is less visible from outside. The building marks Saigon’s status at the turn of the 20th century as a colonial hub, a cultural beacon intended to bring French theatrical and operatic traditions to what was then the capital. This opera house’s significance extends beyond performance. The chamber, once a place for opera arias, echoed instead with political debate and lawmaking. The restored façade, refreshed in 1998 to mark Saigon’s 300th anniversary, now stands as a layered symbol: a colonial architectural gem, a witness to legislative history, and a continuing venue for cultural performance.
