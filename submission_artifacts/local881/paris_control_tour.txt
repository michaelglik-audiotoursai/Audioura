Stop 1: Eiffel Tower

Coordinates: 48.858370, 2.294481

Type/Specialty: Iconic landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning views of Paris, intricate iron lattice design, and the sparkling light show at night.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Paris, France. Your first stop is Eiffel Tower. From the main street, head towards the Seine River, cross the bridge, and walk straight to the Eiffel Tower, where you will see its towering structure emerge above the trees. Standing on the Champ de Mars, the vast iron lattice soars above, with its four massive legs stretching outward, firmly rooted in the ground, framing the open space where visitors gather beneath the open sky.

The Eiffel Tower, designed by engineer Gustave Eiffel and constructed by his company between 1887 and 1889, was completed on March 31, 1889, and opened to the public on May 15 of that year. Its iron structure, stretching high, was a feat of engineering that marked the centennial anniversary of the French Revolution and served as the centerpiece of the 1889 World’s Fair.

In 2024, the tower became the stage for a significant episode. During a visit by the Hindu religious group BAPS, female employees were replaced by male colleagues, a decision that led to an official investigation and sparked a staff strike. The strike forced the tower’s temporary closure, disrupting the flow of visitors and drawing sharp condemnation from Ariel Weil, who leads the company managing the tower and serves as mayor of Paris Centre. Weil described the incident as "totally shocking and unacceptable" and criticized managers for overzealous compliance with the group’s request, revealing tensions beneath the tower’s public image. The closure was a rare interruption in the tower’s continuous operation, underscoring its role not only as an emblem of Paris but also as a workplace where evolving social dynamics play out visibly.

Here, the sound of footsteps on metal staircases mixes with the murmur of the crowd and the occasional announcement echoing through the structure, reminding us that even this iron giant is shaped by human decisions and conflicts.

Directions: Head north for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll arrive at the Arc de Triomphe. Enjoy the walk!

Stop 2: Arc de Triomphe

Coordinates: 48.873790, 2.295028

Type/Specialty: Historic monument

Specific Examples: The names of famous battles inscribed on the arch, the panoramic view from the top, and the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier.

Orientation: Standing at the center of Place Charles de Gaulle, you face the Arc de Triomphe, rising boldly above the twelve avenues that radiate like spokes in a star. The deep shadows under its arches and the steady murmur of traffic swirling around its base offer a moment to grasp the monument’s scale and meaning.

The names of French victories and generals are carved into its inner and outer surfaces, a record of battles that echoes the triumphs Napoleon sought to commemorate when he commissioned the monument in 1806. Designed by Jean-François Chalgrin with later work by Louis-Robert Goust, Jean-Nicolas Huyot, and Guillaume-Abel Blouet, the monument’s neoclassical style draws inspiration from the Arch of Titus in Rome, linking Paris to the ancient world’s celebration of conquest. Beneath the vault lies the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier from World War I, marked by an eternal flame that burns continuously, honoring those who fell unidentified. The flame’s flicker blends with the distant sounds of Paris life—car horns and footsteps—reminding visitors of the passage of time and memory’s persistence. This monument witnessed history’s turning points. During World War II, as Nazi forces occupied Paris, the swastika flag flew above the Arc de Triomphe, a stark symbol of oppression in the city’s heart. Another solemn moment passed here in 1840, when Napoleon’s remains were brought back from exile on Saint Helena. His funeral procession passed beneath the completed monument, affirming the Arc’s role as a gateway between France’s imperial past and its enduring national identity. The Arc de Triomphe stands not only as a tribute to military glory but also as a witness to the layers of history that have shaped Paris.

Directions: Head east for about 2.7 kilometers, and you'll arrive at the stunning Palais Garnier. Enjoy the walk!

Stop 3: Palais Garnier

Coordinates: 48.871970, 2.331601

Type/Specialty: Opera house

Specific Examples: Opulent grand staircase, stunning chandeliers, and the ceiling painted by Marc Chagall.

Orientation: Standing at the Place de l'Opéra, your view is drawn upward and inward by the vast, ornately decorated façade of the Palais Garnier. Its grand entrance, framed by columns and crowned with gilded sculptures, invites attention to the scale and detail that define this building.

The ambiance inside carries the weight and warmth of history—footsteps echo on polished stone, and the air holds a faint trace of the waxed velvet from the auditorium seats. On 20 May 1896, during a performance of the opera Hellé, a counterweight meant to hold the six-ton chandelier failed. The chandelier plunged into the audience below, killing one woman and injuring several others. Beneath the opera house, an unexpected feature remains: during construction, workers uncovered a hidden arm of the Seine river, creating an underground lake still present today. Its Grand Foyer, echoing the Hall at Versailles, often draws quieter admiration than the stage itself. Here, the layers of Parisian art and history intersect, just as they do along the historic Avenue de l’Opéra that leads back toward the Arc de Triomphe. It was opened in 1875.
