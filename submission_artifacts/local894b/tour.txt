Caty Mui Ne Resort to Ta Cu Mountain - Driving Tour
Tour-Category: driving

Stop 1: Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip

Coordinates: 10.981999, 108.251183

Type/Specialty: Coastal area with fishing culture

You are leaving Mui Ne, once a wild coastal area with red sand dunes resembling a desert and only a few poor fishing villages scattered along the shore. Since the mid-1990s, Mui Ne and the surrounding wards of Phan Thiet have transformed dramatically into a major tourist destination, now home to hundreds of resorts along its 50-kilometer coastline. Mui Ne ward itself features two main beaches, Ganh Beach and Suoi Nuoc Beach, both lined with resorts, shops, and restaurants.

Directions: Drive northwest along Nguyen Dinh Chieu Street, then turn left onto Vo Van Kiet Street, continuing until you reach the river. Look out for the bustling markets along the way. Continue to Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River.

Stop 2: Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River

Type/Specialty: Riverfront city area

You see the boats on the Ca Ty River, which many say are the lifeblood of Phan Thiet. The Ca Ty River is about 7.2 kilometers long. Originally, this river was called the Phan River when the town of Phan Thiet was first established. During the French colonial period, Phan Thiet was divided into two areas. The other area included the coastlines of Binh Hung, Lac Dao, Duc Thang, and Hung Long.

Directions: Continue on Vo Van Kiet Street, then take a right onto Nguyen Huu Tho Road, following the signs to the plantations. You might see colorful dragon fruit being harvested along the way. Continue to Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road.

Stop 3: Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road

Type/Specialty: Agricultural region known for dragon fruit

Specific Examples: local farmers

You are in Binh Thuan province, the largest dragon fruit growing region in Vietnam. Vietnam is the only country in Southeast Asia that cultivates dragon fruit on a commercial scale. The dragon fruit here is known for its vibrant appearance and naturally sweet flavor. Thousands of local farmers grow dragon fruit thanks to the sunny, dry climate. Binh Thuan is internationally recognized for its dragon fruit production. This area is one of the country's most famous regions for growing dragon fruit.

Directions: Continue driving southeast for about 15 minutes, and you'll be arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends.

Stop 4: Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends

Type/Specialty: Mountain with cultural significance

At the top, you can see two famous pagodas: Linh Son Truong Tho and Long Doan. Here at the peak, you find the longest reclining Buddha statue in Southeast Asia, one of the biggest and most unique statues in the region.

Directions: Continue driving for about 10 minutes to reach The cable car ascent. Just keep heading in the same direction, and you'll find it ahead.

Stop 5: The cable car ascent

Type/Specialty: Cable car experience

You are about to experience the cable car ascent that holds the world record for the highest vertical climb. This cable car was built in 1955 and carries up to 65 passengers at a time. It travels from 1,035 meters to the top, making it the greatest vertical ascent of 4,626 feet to the top of Fansipan Mountain, Vietnam's tallest peak in the north. During your ride, you view the Toc Tien waterfall directly from above. You also notice the climate changes rapidly as you leave the coastal heat behind. The cable car is renowned for its two-stage ascent. If you want to ride, you can go to the ticket office as early as 6:00 a.m. to get tickets for a later ascent the same day.

Directions: The air is filled with the scent of incense as you approach. Continue to Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda.

Stop 6: Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda

Type/Specialty: Buddhist temple

The sounds here are different from the bustling roads below: quiet footsteps on stone, the murmur of chanting monks blending with the distant rustle of trees. Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda is known far beyond this quiet corner of Ham Tien for one striking feature: a reclining Buddha statue stretching 49 meters in length. The pagoda’s calm presence complements the mountain’s stillness, echoing the quiet moments you spent riding the cable car up earlier. But the pagoda’s story reaches further back.

Directions: From the pagoda, take the short walking path leading to the statue. You may hear the soft sounds of nature as you approach the Reclining Buddha.

Stop 7: The Reclining Buddha

Type/Specialty: Cultural and religious site

You are standing at the Reclining Buddha on Ta Cu Mountain in Ham Tien, Mui Ne, Vietnam. It was completed in the 1960s and is one of the largest reclining Buddha statues in Vietnam. The mountain around you is covered in lush green forest. The Reclining Buddha Pagoda here has several worship halls, three entrances, and colorful paintings of deities. The site is open all year round for visitors like you.

Directions: Continue driving for about 15 minutes to the summit views and the drive home. Enjoy the scenic drive as you make your way there!

Stop 8: The summit views and the drive home

Type/Specialty: Scenic viewpoint

At this summit, the quiet views hold the weight of these transitions. The distant hum of the road mingles with the rustle of leaves, framing a moment where sea, land, and history converge. Today, this place is a threshold where the past’s tides meet the future’s unfolding course.

From Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip, The cable car ascent and The summit views and the drive home, this route is the one you chose through the city — your own path, in your own order. That's 8 stops in all.
