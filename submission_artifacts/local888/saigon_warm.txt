This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stained glass windows, French colonial architecture, and a serene courtyard.

The Cathedral before you was completed in 1880 and designed by architect Jules Bourard. Its neo-Romanesque style is most visible in the twin towers that rise symmetrically above the square, crafted from bricks imported from France. The square itself, now known as Paris Commune Square, has changed names and identities alongside Saigon’s evolving history. It once held a bronze statue of Pierre Pigneau de Béhaine, a French Catholic priest who supported the young Prince Nguyễn Phúc Cảnh during the late 18th century.

Directions: It's a lovely stroll! Continue to Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic Post Office

Specific Examples: Grand interior with wooden counters, maps of Vietnam, and vintage postal items.

Between 1886 and 1891, this building was completed under the direction of Alfred Foulhoux, the chief architect of the French colonial administration. Although Gustave Eiffel’s name is often associated with the post office, this is a misconception that arose from his earlier work in Saigon between 1860 and 1863. In reality, the eclectic blend of Gothic arches, Renaissance details, and French colonial motifs you see here is the work of Foulhoux, who was praised in an 1891 architectural journal for the building’s artistic façade and practical layout. The vaulted iron roof above creaks faintly with the city’s shifting humidity, a reminder of the building’s enduring presence amid Saigon’s evolving streets. The hum of voices and the rustling of letters carried the weight of hope and anxiety, linking people across uncertain distances. Among those who served here was Dương Văn Ngộ, known as Vietnam’s last public letter writer, who worked within these walls from 1990 until 2021. His role preserved the art of handwritten correspondence in an era increasingly dominated by digital exchange. As you step away, the street hums with life.

Directions: From the Post Office, head south on Công xã Paris, turn right onto Nguyễn Văn Bình, and walk straight until you reach Book Street. The atmosphere shifts to a vibrant literary scene filled with readers and vendors. It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Street

Specific Examples: Open-air bookstores, cozy reading nooks, and local author signings.

You are standing on Nguyen Van Binh Street, where Book Street stretches about 100 meters long in the center of Ho Chi Minh City. Book Street is located near famous landmarks like Notre Dame Cathedral and the Ho Chi Minh City Post Office, under the shadows of these urban heritage sites. The street was established to promote a reading culture and provide a gathering space for book lovers in District 1. You can find books on literature, politics, society, science, and culture here, along with postcards, artworks, and other small items. Book Street hosts regular events such as book fairs and author signings, making it lively especially in the evenings when visitors come to explore and enjoy the atmosphere. One of the entrances to this street is from Hai Ba Trung Street, close to other popular spots like Chợ Bến Thành and the Independence Palace.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you'll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The war room, presidential office, and beautiful gardens.

President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered the construction of the new Independence Palace in 1962, but he never saw the finished building. It was completed in 1966 and inaugurated by General Nguyễn Văn Thiệu, who made it his home and office. Thiệu stayed here until 21 April 1975, when he fled as North Vietnamese forces advanced. The palace conceals more than its modernist exterior suggests, with the sound of distant traffic and the scent of tropical gardens surrounding you. It was also the site of the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 that ended the Vietnam War, when a Liberation Army of South Vietnam tank crashed through its gates.

Directions: Head northwest for roughly 500 meters, and you'll arrive at the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the walk!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: War exhibits, historical photographs, and military artifacts.

You are standing at the War Remnants Museum, located at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. The museum opened on 4 September 1975 as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes. The building was once the US military's intelligence headquarters during the Vietnam War. Inside, you find exhibits about the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War, covering events from 1946 to 1975. Military equipment on display includes a Bell UH-1 Huey helicopter, an M48 Patton tank, and various attack bombers. Some aircraft bear non-standard US Air Force markings but actually belonged to the South Vietnamese Air Force. You can also see a photographic collection donated by Vietnam War photojournalist Bunyo Ishikawa in 1998, a guillotine used until 1960, and jars containing fetuses affected by Agent Orange.

Directions: Head east for about 1.2 kilometers, and you'll find the beautiful Opera House waiting for you. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning facade, classical performances, and elegant interiors.

This building opened on January 1, 1900, designed by Félix Olivier in the Flamboyant style favored during the French Third Republic. In Saigon, however, some of the original lavishness was pared back in 1943 after critics said it was too ornate. In 1976, the building returned to theatrical use, bridging past and present. Its restored façade from 1998 stands as a reminder of both preservation and change. The murmur of voices, footsteps on polished floors, and the occasional tuning of instruments inside connect the present with its layered past.

Directions: Head west for about 200 meters, and you'll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Illuminated at night, large clock tower, and surrounding gardens.

Originally known as the Hôtel de Ville de Saïgon, it served as the municipal hall during French colonial rule. Its Beaux-Arts style borrows from Renaissance and Baroque traditions, with symmetrical wings stretching out like arms embracing the city’s core. The clock tower, echoing northern France’s bell towers, stands tall, marking the hours for the busy streets below. Along with the city’s new name, the building took on a new role and title, becoming the seat of the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee and People’s Council. The garden’s faint scent of tropical flowers blends with the city’s warmth, a reminder that beneath this grand architecture, daily decisions shaping millions unfold.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you’ll arrive at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

Directions: Head east for roughly 350 meters, and you'll find yourself at Bạch Đằng Pier. Enjoy the stroll! Continue to Thi Bar.

You are standing in front of the Café Apartments at 42 Nguyen Hue in Ho Chi Minh City. This building was constructed in the 1960s. During the Vietnam War era, it was home to American military officers. Today, the building has been converted into a hub of cafes, workshops, and boutique stores.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Pier

Specific Examples: River views, boat tours, and sunset scenery.

Bạch Đằng Pier was established in the early 19th century, when this stretch of riverbank served as the primary boat quay for Saigon. Over time, the pier evolved into a ferry terminal, directly linking Bạch Đằng Pier to Thủ Thiêm Pier across the river. This ferry terminal supported riverborne commerce and passenger flow well into the 20th century. The ferry boats you might see moored here once carried countless commuters and traders, serving as a reminder that Saigon’s waterways have been as crucial as its streets. The solid stone and concrete edges of the pier bear the wear of decades of such traffic, their surfaces smoothed by water and footsteps alike. Today, Bạch Đằng Pier serves as a departure point for tours to the Củ Chi Tunnels, a network of underground passages used during the Vietnam War by Vietnamese fighters. From this pier, visitors set out on journeys that retrace the paths of wartime resilience and strategy, connecting the urban heart of Saigon to the broader historical landscape beyond. The steady clatter of water against hulls and the calls of boat operators punctuate the air, anchoring Bạch Đằng Pier’s identity as a place shaped by movement and connection. Here, the river’s edge is a place where past and present coexist, a threshold between the city and the wider world.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll arrive at Thi Bar. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: Gay bar

Orientation: Thi Bar is a gay bar located at 224 Đề Thám in Ho Chi Minh City.

You are standing outside Thi Bar, which has been open since 2010 at 224 Đề Thám Street in Ho Chi Minh City’s District 1. This bar welcomes everyone and features live music every night from 10pm until late, with local bands playing cover songs. You can request your favorite song from the friendly staff here. Thi Bar is one of the few real gay bars in the city and is located in the busy tourist area of Saigon. The bar opens daily from 7pm until late but is closed on Tuesdays, except on holidays. There is outdoor seating available, but food options inside are limited, so customers are advised to eat beforehand or ask about bringing outside food.
