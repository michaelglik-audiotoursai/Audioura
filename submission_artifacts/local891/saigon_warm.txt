This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Bishop Isidore Colombert laid the first stone on October 7, 1877, before an audience including the Deputy Commander of Cochinchina and other officials. The church was completed in 1880. The church’s two towers, added in 1895, rise 57.6 meters and support six large bronze bells, each carefully cast and hung to call worshippers across the growing city. In 1959, the Vatican officially elevated this church to the status of a basilica, a recognition that marked its importance to Catholic communities throughout Vietnam.

Directions: Head east for about a minute or two, and you'll find the Post Office waiting for you. Enjoy the stroll! It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historical Building

The building before you was constructed between 1886 and 1891, during the era when Vietnam was part of French Indochina. Although it is often attributed to Gustave Eiffel—the engineer behind the Eiffel Tower and the framework of the Statue of Liberty—the post office was actually designed by Alfred Foulhoux. Inside, two large painted maps catch the eye. From 1990 until 2021, Dương Văn Ngộ worked here as Vietnam’s last public letter writer, preserving the ancient craft of handwritten communication in an age rapidly turning digital.

Directions: From the Post Office, head southeast on Nguyễn Huệ Street, then turn left onto Nguyễn Văn Bình Street. Book Street will be on your right. Enjoy the scent of coffee wafting from nearby cafés. It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Spot

You are standing on Nguyen Van Binh Street, home to Book Street in District 1 of Ho Chi Minh City, a narrow pedestrian street lined with numerous book stalls and shops offering a wide variety of printed books, from literature and politics to science and culture. This street, nestled near iconic landmarks like Notre Dame Cathedral and the Ho Chi Minh City Post Office, was established to promote a reading culture and provide a gathering space for book lovers. Book Street is especially lively in the evenings when locals and tourists explore its book fairs, author signings, and cultural events. Just steps away from here, on 1 February 1968, during the Tet Offensive, South Vietnamese police chief Lt. Col. Nguyen Ngoc Loan executed a handcuffed Viet Cong prisoner, Nguyen Van Lem, on a Saigon street near the city center. One of the entrances to Book Street is from Hai Ba Trung Street, close to other central spots like Chợ Bến Thành market and the Independence Palace.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you'll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

You are standing in front of the Independence Palace, a historic landmark and government-owned public hall in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. The palace is also officially known as the Reunification Convention Hall or simply Reunification Hall. Construction of the current Independence Palace was ordered by President Ngô Đình Diệm in 1962 to replace the old palace, which was badly damaged due to being bombed by two dissident Republic of Vietnam Air Force pilots. The current palace was designed by Ngô Viết Thụ and completed in 1966. From October 1967 to April 1975, the palace served as the home and office of President Thiệu until he fled the country during the Ho Chi Minh Campaign. After the communist North Vietnamese and South Vietnamese negotiated in November 1975, the Provisional Revolutionary Government renamed the palace Reunification Hall, while it remains a historic site for tourism today. The Independence Palace was the site of the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 that ended the Vietnam War, when a Liberation Army of South Vietnam tank crashed through its gates.

Directions: Head northwest for roughly 500 meters, and you’ll arrive at the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Photographic exhibits

The War Remnants Museum was established on 4 September 1975 as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes. Inside, you will find exhibits spanning the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War, showing stark images and artifacts. Faced with the documented horrors, William Wilson expressed disbelief, saying he had prayed to God that this thing was fiction. Within the museum are permanent and temporary exhibitions focusing on events dating from when the First Indochina War began in 1946, up until the end of the Vietnam War in 1975. Other exhibits include graphic photography, accompanied by a short text in English, Vietnamese and Japanese, covering the effects of Agent Orange and other chemical defoliant sprays, the use of napalm and phosphorus bombs, and war atrocities such as the My Lai massacre.

Directions: Head east for roughly 1.2 kilometers, and you'll find the beautiful Opera House waiting for you. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: opera performances

You stand before the Saigon Opera House, a grand building completed in 1900 that once showcased French colonial artistry with its ornate façade and statues. The Municipal Theatre owes its specific characteristics to the work of architect Félix Olivier, while construction was under supervision of architects Ernest Guichard and Eugène Ferret in 1900. During World War II, when Saigon was occupied by the Japanese, the opera house suffered damage from Allied bombing in 1944. After the Fall of Saigon in 1975, this very theatre took on a new role as the meeting place for the People's Assembly under the Provisional Revolutionary Government. By 1976, the building was restored to its original function as a theatre, returning to its cultural roots after a turbulent period in history.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you'll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

The Ho Chi Minh City Hall was completed in 1908 after ten years of construction that began in 1898, directed by architect Fernand Gardè. The building was officially opened in 1909 by the French Indochina governors-general to mark fifty years of French presence in Saigon. At that time, it bore the name L'hôtel de ville, which means “Municipal Hall” in French. The building’s role evolved through decades of change. On April 29 and 30, 2023, marking Reunification Day, it opened its doors to the public for the first time since its inception.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you'll arrive at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

You are standing at Café Apartments 42, which was once a residential apartment block. During the Vietnam War, this building housed American military officers and their families. It is located at 42 Nguyễn Huệ, right in the heart of Saigon. Today, the building has been transformed into a commercial space. Inside, you find multiple cafes and boutiques. This place has become a popular cultural and social hub in Ho Chi Minh City.

Directions: Head east for roughly 350 meters, and you'll find yourself at Bạch Đằng Pier. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Spot

Once, the French managed the pier under the Compagnies des Messageries fluviales, a company handling river shipping. In 1881, Bạch Đằng Pier was transformed into the River port and entrusted to the management of the Compagnies des Messageries fluviales, while the replacement mercantile port was constructed in Khánh Hội. During the 1945 August Revolution, this pier was a flashpoint where Vietnamese forces engaged Japanese troops. Three decades later, in 1975, Bạch Đằng Pier assumed a different but equally significant role. In 1975, during the Fall of Saigon, Bạch Đằng Pier was a significant evacuation point for many South Vietnamese and foreign nationals fleeing the city as North Vietnamese forces approached. As the sun lowers beyond the skyline, the pier’s calm invites another kind of journey: from past to present, from movement to stillness.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll find Thi Bar waiting for you. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: Gay bar

Orientation: Thi Bar is a gay bar located in Ben Thanh, Ho Chi Minh City.

You are standing outside Thi Bar, located at 224 Đề Thám Street in the Phạm Ngũ Lão ward of Ho Chi Minh City. Thi Bar has been open since 2010 and welcomes you every day from 7pm until late, except on Tuesdays when it is closed. Live music starts here at 10pm each night, creating a vibrant atmosphere with bands performing regularly. The bar is known as one of the few real gay bars in the city, with friendly staff ready to greet you. You can enjoy outdoor seating here, but note that food options are limited, so you might want to eat before coming or ask about bringing outside food.
