This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, beautiful stained glass windows, and a peaceful garden.

Completed in 1880, the Saigon Notre Dame Cathedral was designed by architect Jules Bourard. The sounds of traffic and voices mingle with the distant tolling of bells, connecting the past and present in the city’s most populous and economically vital district. The square around the cathedral has carried different names, mirroring political shifts. Originally called Place de la Cathédrale during French colonial rule, it was renamed several times, including President John F. Kennedy Square in 1964, before receiving its current name, Paris Commune Square, after 1975. This layering of names echoes the rhythms of Saigon’s urban life, where tradition and transformation intersect daily.

Directions: Enjoy the stroll! Continue to Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic Postal Building

Specific Examples: Intricate French colonial architecture, a large central hall with a wooden ceiling, and vintage postal equipment.

This building, completed over five years from 1886 to 1891, was designed by Alfred Foulhoux, the Chief Architect of the French colonial administration in Indochina. Despite frequent claims to the contrary, Gustave Eiffel, known for his iron lattice work, was not responsible for this structure. The journal Architecte constructeur: Revue du monde architectural et artistique noted on 15 September 1891 that the inauguration had taken place on 14 July but was delayed until the Governor General’s return. The journal celebrated the post office as a monument “adorned with a most artistic façade,” highlighting Foulhoux’s expertise in blending Gothic, Renaissance, and French influences into a building that is both functional and elegant. The scent of ink and old wood lingers subtly in the air, anchoring you in its daily purpose. Known as Vietnam’s last public letter writer, a man here maintained the tradition of hand-writing letters for those who could not or would not write themselves.

Directions: From the Post Office, head south on Lê Duẩn, then turn right onto Nguyễn Văn Bình. Book Street will be ahead on your left. You may catch the scent of coffee wafting from nearby cafés. It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Literary Hub

Specific Examples: Outdoor book stalls, cozy reading corners, and occasional literary events.

Here, you find numerous book stalls and shops offering a wide variety of books, including literature, politics, society, science, and culture. The street is closed to motorized traffic, making it a safe and pleasant place for you to walk and browse. Book Street hosts cultural events like book fairs, author signings, and literary discussions, promoting a reading culture in the city. In the evening, the street is beautifully lit and lively, providing a nice way for you to escape the hustle and bustle of the city. Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: Impressive architecture, historical exhibits, and lush gardens.

Orientation: Standing just outside the main entrance of the Independence Palace, the broad gates stand before you, framed by the low, sprawling building and its gardens. The smooth concrete walls reflect the bright sunlight, while the rustle of leaves from the surrounding trees carries the city’s vibrant hum to this historic spot.

This palace, completed on 31 October 1966, was designed by the Vietnamese architect Ngô Viết Thụ, who had won the prestigious Grand Prix de Rome in 1955. It replaced an older presidential residence that had been damaged by bombing. The current building was commissioned on 1 July 1962 by President Ngô Đình Diệm, who sought a new seat of power for the Republic of Vietnam. However, Diệm never saw its completion; in November 1963, he and his brother Ngô Đình Nhu were assassinated during a coup led by General Dương Văn Minh. Today, the palace preserves these layers of history. It stands quietly amid the city’s rhythms, a place where political power was both exercised and upended.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Powerful exhibits on the Vietnam War, military artifacts, and photographs.

The clatter of city life momentarily softens here, replaced by a quiet solemnity that seeps from this place of memories and reckonings. Established in 1975, the museum’s grounds hold a yard where relics of war—like the Bell UH-1 “Huey” helicopter, a BLU-82 “Daisy Cutter” bomb, and an M48 Patton tank—stand silent, their metal surfaces dulled by time and exposure, yet insistent on the violence they once embodied. The air feels heavy with history, mingled with the faint scent of aged concrete and the low murmur of visitors’ footsteps on worn floors. Inside, photographic displays—some contributed by the war photojournalist Bunyo Ishikawa in 1998—depict the devastating impact of chemical warfare, including the use of Agent Orange and napalm, and the human cost etched into images and objects alike. Among the most harrowing are three jars containing preserved human fetuses, deformed by exposure to the dioxins in Agent Orange, a chemical defoliant sprayed extensively during the war.

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, live performances, and elegant interiors.

The Saigon Opera House was completed on January 1, 1900, and designed by the French architect Félix Olivier. Originally called the Opéra de Saïgon, its venue auditorium was intended as a cultural beacon for the city’s growing elite. In the mid-20th century, the building’s purpose shifted dramatically. When the legislature split into two chambers, the Opera House adapted to house both the Lower House and the Senate. After 1975, the Provisional Revolutionary Government convened the People’s Congress of Deputies here, marking a moment of political transition. In 1976, the building returned to its original theatrical function, bridging the city’s colonial past with its modern identity.

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Grand façade, beautiful gardens, and historical significance.

Locals once called this building Dinh xã Tây, meaning French municipal palace, or Dinh đốc lý, the mayor's palace. In the garden courtyard, you see a statue of Ho Chi Minh teaching a child, symbolizing Vietnam's transformation from a French colony to an independent nation. Ho Chi Minh, born Nguyễn Sinh Cung, was a secretive revolutionary who used between 50 and 200 pseudonyms during his life. He returned to Vietnam in 1941 and founded the Việt Minh independence movement to fight against the Japanese occupation. In 1945, Ho Chi Minh led the August Revolution against the Vietnamese monarchy and proclaimed the independence of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam, marking the birth of North Vietnam.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you'll reach Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

Orientation: Café Apartments 42 is a coffee shop located at 42 Nguyễn Huệ in Ho Chi Minh City.

You are standing in front of the Café Apartments at 42 Nguyễn Huệ, a nine-story building in Ho Chi Minh City. This building was constructed in the 1960s and originally served as a dormitory for officers and civil servants. Now, Apartment 42 has been transformed into a cultural hub with dozens of independent cafes, handmade shops, and nail salons. From the street, the building looks like a giant grid box with many small units. The Café Apartments is located near the busiest Nguyen Hue Walking Street in the city. You might also hear it called The Café Apartments or simply 42 Nguyen Hue.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Waterfront Attraction

Specific Examples: Scenic views of the Saigon River, boat tours, and a relaxing promenade.

Parties leaving from here set out to explore a place where the stakes of conflict were carved literally into the earth—narrow tunnels barely wide enough for a person’s shoulders, where ingenuity turned soil into a hidden battleground. From this shore, the river stretches out as both a transportation artery and a natural boundary. The pier’s aged metal girders, chipped paint from years of exposure, and the low hum of engines preparing for departure all speak quietly but clearly of its enduring presence. The river’s steady murmur intertwines with human movement, connecting this place to the city’s pulse. Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: Gay bar

You are standing outside Thi Bar, one of the only real gay bars in Ho Chi Minh City, located at 224 Đề Thám Street in District 1. Since opening in 2010, Thi Bar has welcomed visitors every day from 7 pm until late, except Tuesdays when it is closed, though it opens on holidays. Starting at 10 pm each night, live music fills the bar, creating a vibrant atmosphere enhanced by super friendly staff. With outdoor seating available and a welcoming vibe, Thi Bar stands out as a distinctive spot in Saigon’s nightlife. Food options inside are limited, so you might want to eat beforehand or ask if you can bring outside food.
