This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical landmark

Completed in 1880, the cathedral stands as a central landmark, its quiet strength contrasting with the dynamic life bustling through the streets. This square was once named Place de la Cathédrale during French rule, and later became Hòa Bình Square when a statue of Our Lady of Peace was placed here in 1959, reflecting the city’s complex layers of change. The site’s significance extends beyond architecture. Following the self-immolation of Thích Quảng Đức, Diệm promised reforms to ease Buddhist tensions but failed to deliver, prompting further protests and violent raids on Buddhist pagodas. During these raids, Thích Quảng Đức’s heart was seized, becoming a grim symbol of ongoing conflict. His sacrifice inspired other monks to follow his example, escalating unrest that eventually contributed to the US-backed coup that toppled Diệm on 2 November 1963.

Directions: Head east for about a minute or two, and you'll find the Post Office just ahead. Enjoy the stroll! It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic post office

It is often mistakenly credited to Gustave Eiffel, but in fact, Alfred Foulhoux was the architect who brought together Gothic, Renaissance, and French influences in its design. Known locally as the “stork” stamp for its depiction of the elegant bird, this stamp was the first Vietnamese stamp issued in 1864. The introduction of the stamp enabled ordinary citizens to send letters and parcels with official recognition, transforming how information and personal news traveled in this rapidly changing city. The Post Office was constructed between 1886 and 1891 and is now a tourist attraction. The large painted maps on the walls were created shortly after the building’s completion in 1891. These maps chart telegraphic lines and the layout of Saigon and its surroundings in 1892, showing how this hub connected people and places beyond the immediate streets.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural street

You are standing on Nguyen Van Binh Street in District 1, where Book Street is located. Here, you find numerous book stalls and shops selling a wide variety of printed books in different genres, including literature, politics, society, science, and culture. The street is lined with trees and offers a place to escape the city's hustle and bustle. Book Street hosts regular events like book fairs, author signings, and cultural activities. One entrance to this street is from Hai Ba Trung Street. The street was established to promote a reading culture and provide a space for book lovers to gather.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical site

You are looking at the Independence Palace, a government-owned public hall in Ho Chi Minh City, also called the Reunification Convention Hall or Reunification Hall. The current building was designed by Ngo Viet Thu and completed in 1966, replacing an older palace damaged by bombing. President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered its construction in 1962 after the previous palace was attacked by dissident pilots. From October 1967 until April 1975, this palace served as the home and office of President Thiệu. The Independence Palace was the site of the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 that ended the Vietnam War, when a Liberation Army of South Vietnam tank crashed through its gates. Later that year, the Provisional Revolutionary Government renamed the palace Reunification Hall following negotiations between North and South Vietnam.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Photographic exhibits, military equipment

This museum first opened its doors on 4 September 1975, under the name Exhibition House for U.S. and Puppet Crimes. The displays gathered here do not shy from the brutal consequences of conflict. Inside, a poignant tribute honors the photojournalists who risked and often lost their lives to capture these truths. The War Remnants Museum is a war museum at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street, Xuân Hòa, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural venue

This building opened its doors on January 1, 1900. It was designed by Félix Olivier, with construction supervised by Ernest Guichard and Eugène Ferret—a trio of French architects who shaped a distinctly French colonial presence here. The architecture borrows directly from Paris, echoing styles completed for the 1900 Paris Exposition, which gives the façade its flamboyant curves and sculpted reliefs. The decoration, including inscriptions and furnishings, was designed by a French artist and shipped here, so every detail carries a shade of Parisian origin. The Opera House became the venue for the People's Assembly, reflecting a pivotal moment as the city’s political life shifted dramatically. When the legislature split into two chambers, it became home to both the Lower House and the Senate, serving as a political center as well as a cultural one. After the Fall of Saigon in 1975, the Provisional Revolutionary Government started holding the People's Assembly at the theatre. The façade you see today was restored in 1998 for Saigon’s 300th anniversary, bringing back some of the original ornamentation that had been removed during wartime.

Directions: Head west for about 200 meters, and you’ll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government building

This building was completed in 1908 after a decade-long construction that began in 1898, designed by Fernand Gardè. It was officially opened in 1909 by the French Indochina governors-general to mark fifty years of French presence in Saigon. At that moment, it took the name L'hôtel de ville, or Municipal Hall. Locals simply called it Dinh xã Tây, meaning 'French municipal palace,' or Dinh đốc lý, 'mayor’s palace.' For over a century, this hall shaped the city’s political life. On April 29 and 30, 2023, the building opened its doors to the public for the first time during Reunification Day celebrations. From here, the tour can continue toward the lively charm of Café Apartments 42 nearby, where everyday Saigon pulses in a different rhythm.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Waterfront area

Bạch Đằng Pier was established at the beginning of the 19th century as a key boat quay along the Saigon River. In 1881, Bạch Đằng Pier was transformed into the River port and entrusted to the management of the Compagnies des Messageries fluviales, while the replacement mercantile port was constructed in Khánh Hội. It is located next to the Ba Son Shipyard site, now part of the Saigon – Ba Son complex, showing its long connection to river commerce and transport. The pier once served as a ferry terminal linking this side of the Saigon River with Thủ Thiêm Pier across the water. This ferry service connected districts separated by the river and carried daily commuters, traders, and travelers. Bạch Đằng Pier is also a departure point for tours to the Củ Chi Tunnels, an underground network used during the Vietnam War.
