This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: St. Mary's stained glass windows, the iconic red brick facade, and the peaceful garden area.

The cathedral before you was completed in 1880, designed by the architect Jules Bourard. The twin towers rise, framing stained glass windows that catch the light with a soft glow, filtering it into the quiet interior. The square directly facing the cathedral was originally named Place de la Cathédrale during the French colonial period. In 1903, a bronze statue of French Catholic priest Pierre Pigneau de Béhaine and juvenile Prince Nguyễn Phúc Cảnh was erected here, and the square was known as Place Pigneau de Béhaine. That statue was removed in 1945, leaving the space blank until the installation of the statue of Our Lady of Peace in 1959. In May 1964, the South Vietnamese government renamed it President John F. Kennedy Square honoring the same name assassinated U.S. President.

Directions: Enjoy the stroll! Continue to Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Architectural Landmark

Specific Examples: The grand interior, beautifully painted ceiling, and historical postal artifacts.

Infobox: former names: Sở dây thép Sài Gòn; years built: *1860-1863 *1886-1891; architect: *Gustave Eiffel * Auguste Henri Vildieu. You step inside the Saigon Central Post Office, built between 1886 and 1891 during the French colonial era, replacing an earlier structure from the 1860s. The soaring vaulted ceilings and wrought ironwork frame a space filled with history, where sunlight filters through arched windows onto tiled floors. Along the walls, you see two painted maps from 1892—one showing telegraphic lines across Southern Vietnam and Cambodia, the other detailing Saigon and its surroundings at the time. Many mistakenly credit the building’s design to Gustave Eiffel, famous for the Eiffel Tower and Statue of Liberty, but it was actually designed by Alfred Foulhoux. From 1990 to 2021, Dương Văn Ngộ worked here as Vietnam’s last public letter writer, connecting people through handwritten messages in an age of rapid change.

Directions: From the Post Office, walk northeast along Dong Khoi Street, then turn left onto Nguyen Van Binh Street. The Book Street will be on your right. You'll notice the lively atmosphere filled with readers and vendors. It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Hub

Specific Examples: Rows of book stalls, cozy reading nooks, and local author signings.

You are standing on Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street, a narrow, tree-lined pedestrian street about 100 meters long in the heart of Ho Chi Minh City’s District 1. Named after the famous Vietnamese intellectual Nguyễn Văn Bình, this street lies beside the historic Notre-Dame Cathedral and the Central Post Office. Book Street was established to promote a reading culture and to provide a welcoming space for book lovers to gather in the digital age. Here, you can browse a wide variety of printed books covering literature, politics, society, science, and culture, all offered by numerous book stalls and shops. The street comes alive especially in the evenings, bustling with visitors exploring the stalls and enjoying cultural events like book fairs, author signings, and literary discussions. This small but vibrant street is a cherished hub for nurturing the love of reading amidst the busy city center.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you'll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The Presidential Office, the war room, and the beautiful gardens.

The Independence Palace was completed on 31 October 1966, following a design by architect Ngô Viết Thụ, a Vietnamese laureate of the prestigious Prix de Rome awarded by the French government in 1955. The breach of the palace gates by a tank was not merely a physical rupture but a symbolic collapse of a regime, closing a chapter of conflict and ushering in a new era for the city and country. The echoes of that day still resonate in the empty shell of the gate’s ironwork and the scars visible on the palace’s exterior. Today, the palace serves as a museum and convention hall, with its spacious rooms and war rooms preserved for visitors. The quiet gardens and formal spaces stand in contrast to the tense history they witnessed, linking the rhythms of the everyday city with moments of rupture and change. This palace reflects the sharp intersection of governance, conflict, and history that shaped this place.

Directions: Head northwest for roughly 500 meters, and you'll arrive at the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy your stroll!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Exhibits on the Vietnam War, military equipment, and survivor stories.

You are standing at the War Remnants Museum, located at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Ho Chi Minh City, a place dedicated to preserving evidence of the war of aggression against Vietnam. This museum was originally established on 4 September 1975 as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes, housed in the former US military intelligence headquarters. Inside, you encounter photographs, artifacts, and personal stories that reveal the devastating consequences of the war and its lasting toll on the Vietnamese people. One section recreates the Con Dao prison, offering a glimpse into the harsh conditions, though the real prisons are located at Con Dao itself.

Directions: Head east for about 1.2 kilometers, and you'll arrive at the stunning Opera House. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: The grand exterior, lavish interior, and performances of traditional Vietnamese music.

In 1898, the construction of the new theatre commenced on the site of the old one, and it was completed by 1 January 1900. You stand before the Saigon Opera House, completed in 1900 and designed by French architect Félix Olivier in the flamboyant style of France’s Third Republic. Its elegant façade, reminiscent of Paris’s Petit Palais, still shows the detailed stonework and curves of that era. Originally a venue for performances, this building took on a new role after the Fall of Saigon in 1975. The Provisional Revolutionary Government began holding the People's Assembly here, turning the theatre into a place where laws were debated and decisions made.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you’ll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: The impressive facade, the statue of Ho Chi Minh, and the surrounding gardens.

Infobox: former names: L'hôtel de ville Tòa đô chánh Sài Gòn; completion date: 1908; architect: Fernand Gardè; architectural style: Beaux-Arts; height: 30 m ft Passages: After victories over conquests in Southern Vietnam, in 1870. You are standing in front of Ho Chi Minh City Hall, a grand yellow building built between 1898 and 1909 during the French colonial period. Originally, it was called L'hôtel de ville, or Municipal Hall, and served as an administrative building for the colonial government. The building was modeled as a simpler version of the Hotel de Ville in Paris. After World War II and the end of colonial rule, South Vietnam converted this building into the city hall. In the garden courtyard, you can see a statue of Ho Chi Minh teaching a child, symbolizing Vietnam's transformation from a French colony to an independent nation.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you'll arrive at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy your stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

You are standing at the Café Apartments at 42 Nguyen Hue in Ho Chi Minh City. This building was constructed in the 1960s. During the Vietnam War era, it was once home to American military officers. Today, the building has been converted into a hub of cafes, workshops, and boutique stores.

Directions: Head east for roughly 350 meters, and you'll find yourself at Bạch Đằng Pier. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Waterfront

Specific Examples: Views of the Saigon River, boat tours, and evening lights along the riverbank.

By allowing Saigon’s residents to cross the river by boat instead of traveling around its bends, the ferry terminal shaped local transit patterns and daily life. The renovation of the area was eventually carried out in 2021 and completed in 2022, which gave the park its current appearance with the grass areas and walkways forming the shape of lotus flowers. Visitors leaving from the pier embark on a journey to witness the resilience and ingenuity of those who lived and fought beneath the surface. The scent of river water mingles with the faint diesel of passing boats as you stand here at the water’s edge.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at Thi Bar. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: Gay bar

You are standing outside Thi Bar at 224 Đề Thám Street in the Phạm Ngũ Lão area of Ho Chi Minh City. Thi Bar has been open since 2010 and welcomes a mixed crowd every evening from 7pm until late, except on Tuesdays when it is closed. You can enjoy live music here every night starting at 10pm, with a focus on cocktails and beer. The bar offers outdoor seating and a friendly atmosphere with staff ready to serve you. Food options inside are limited, so you might want to eat before you come or ask if you can bring outside food. Thi Bar is known as one of the few real gay bars in the city, located just off Bùi Viện Street in the heart of Saigon's tourist area.
