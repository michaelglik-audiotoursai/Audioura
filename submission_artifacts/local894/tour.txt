This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ham Tien, Mui Ne.

Stop 1: Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip

Coordinates: 10.9492, 108.1991

Type/Specialty: Coastal area

You are standing on Ham Tien Beach in Mui Ne, Vietnam, known for its swaying coconut palms and blue waters. Mui Ne is a coastal fishing ward located in the Lâm Đồng Province of Vietnam. Mui Ne and other wards of Phan Thiet stretch along the coast for about 50 kilometers and have evolved into a resort destination since the mid-1990s. Nearby, Mũi Né ward features two beaches, Ganh Beach and Suoi Nuoc Beach, which have several resorts, shops, and restaurants.

Directions: From the fishing coast, head northwest along the beach road, then turn left onto Le Duan Street. Continue straight until you reach the riverbank, where you can see local fishermen at work. Continue to Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River.

Stop 2: Phan Thiet and the Ca Ty River

Type/Specialty: Riverside area

It derives from Po Thit, the Cham Prince who governed this region militarily in the 15th century. The fortification known as Camp Po Thit lent its name to the area—a name that, through Vietnamese pronunciation, became Phan Thiet. The Ca Ty River quietly courses through town, its waters having witnessed far more than daily life. Once known as the Phan River, it winds through urban wards such as Đức Thắng and Lạc Đạo on one bank, and Long Bình and Minh Long on the other, marking the city’s historical geography. Misled by guidebooks, which incorrectly labelled Rang Beach as "Mũi Né Beach", they were the initial tourists, along with many succeeding tourists mistakenly calling every region east of the Phan Thiết city center "Mũi Né." In 1697, Bình Thuận was changed from a fort to a district, then to a dinh.

Directions: From the river, head east on Nguyen Huu Tho Street, then take a right onto Vo Nguyen Giap Road. As you drive, notice the vibrant dragon fruit fields lining the roadside. Continue to Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road.

Stop 3: Dragon fruit country – the plantations along the road

Coordinates: 10.9290, 108.1745

Type/Specialty: Riverside area

You are in Binh Thuan, one of Vietnam's most famous dragon fruit-growing regions. Vietnam is the only country in Southeast Asia that cultivates dragon fruit on a commercial scale. Dragon fruit was introduced to Indochina in the mid-19th century by French priests. In 1995, Vietnam became the first country to sell dragon fruit on the world market. Today, dragon fruit is grown in more than 30 countries worldwide.

Directions: From the dragon fruit farms, continue south on Vo Nguyen Giap Road, then turn left onto Ta Cu Mountain Road. You might catch glimpses of the mountain peak rising above the landscape. Continue to Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends.

Stop 4: Arriving at Ta Cu – the mountain and its legends

Type/Specialty: Mountain attraction

You are now at Ta Cu Mountain, located in Ham Thuan Nam District, about 40 minutes from Mui Ne Centre and Phan Thiet City. Many visitors take a modern cable car to reach the top of Ta Cu Mountain for a scenic ride. At the summit, you can see two famous pagodas: Linh Son Truong Tho pagoda and Long Doan pagoda. Ta Cu Mountain is known as the most famous spiritual attraction in Binh Thuan. Here, you find the longest reclining Buddha statue in Southeast Asia. This mountain features one of the biggest and most unique statues in Southeast Asia.

Directions: From the base of Ta Cu Mountain, follow the signs to the cable car station, which is a short walk uphill. You may hear the sound of the cable car's machinery as you approach. Continue to The cable car ascent.

Stop 5: The cable car ascent

Type/Specialty: Cable car experience

Fansipan is the tallest peak in Vietnam, located in the north in Sa Pa. As you rise, you can see the Toc Tien waterfall directly from above. The climate changes quickly during your ascent. You leave behind the coastal heat as you go higher.

Directions: Continue straight ahead for about 15 minutes, heading east, and you'll reach Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda

Type/Specialty: Buddhist temple

As you stand here, the smooth stone steps beneath your feet contrast with the rough bark of the pines surrounding the complex. Inside, the pagoda has been carefully restored over time, giving it a fresh appearance that belies its deep roots.

Directions: Continue walking south for about 10 minutes, and you’ll reach The Reclining Buddha. Enjoy the peaceful surroundings as you stroll!

Stop 7: The Reclining Buddha

Type/Specialty: Cultural landmark

It was completed in the 1960s and stands about 7 meters high. You reach the Reclining Buddha at the summit of Ta Cu Mountain, either by cable car or trekking up the mountain. Nearby, you can also find Linh Son Truong Tho Pagoda.

Directions: From the Reclining Buddha, walk back towards the main viewpoint area at the summit. As you reach the edge, take a moment to enjoy the panoramic views of the surrounding landscape before heading back down. Continue to The summit views and the drive home.

Stop 8: The summit views and the drive home

Type/Specialty: Scenic viewpoint

From this platform, the panorama unfolds fully — the undulating hills, the patchwork of plantations, and the distant coastline meet beneath the open sky. The quiet now at this summit contrasts with that surge. The quiet hum of the present moment carries the weight of those decades, visible in the shifting uses of the terrain and the evolving horizon.

From Leaving Mui Ne – the fishing coast and Ham Tien strip, The cable car ascent and The summit views and the drive home, this route is the one you chose through the city — your own path, in your own order. That's 8 stops in all.
