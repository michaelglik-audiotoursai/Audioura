Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: Old Quarter walk, Hanoi, a walking tour

Stop 1: Hoan Kiem Lake

Address: Hoan Kiem, Hanoi, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.028889, 105.852500

Type/Specialty: Scenic Lake

Specific Examples: The Turtle Tower, the Ngoc Son Temple on an island, and beautiful walking paths.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through the Old Quarter walk, Hanoi. Your first stop is Hoan Kiem Lake. From the main street of Trang Tien, head east towards the lake. As you approach, you'll see the vibrant colors of local flora and the tranquil waters reflecting the city skyline. Across the water, the slender red bridge leads to a small island crowned by Ngoc Son Temple, inviting eyes to trace its curve.

Hoan Kiem Lake, whose name means “Returned Sword,” anchors one of Hanoi’s four original urban districts, the Hoàn Kiếm District, which housed 135,618 residents in 2019. This district forms the commercial and political center of Hanoi, with the Hanoi Metropolitan People’s Committee located just adjacent on Đinh Tiên Hoàng street. The lake itself divides the Old Quarter’s compact alleys to the north from the French Quarter’s villas and wide avenues to the south. This division marks how the city’s present life and layered past converge here. The lake was once known as Luc Thuy Lake but took the name Hoan Kiem Lake in the 18th century, reflecting a local legend that shaped the district’s identity. The story tells that King Lê Lợi, after leading a successful rebellion against Chinese occupiers in the 15th century, returned a sacred sword to a divine turtle residing in this lake. According to this tale, the turtle appeared to reclaim the sword that had helped Lê Lợi drive out the invaders. This event gave the lake its name — “Returned Sword” — marking the moment when earthly power handed back its mysterious weapon to a celestial guardian. Further adding to the lake’s living history, a real giant turtle, believed by some locals to be the incarnation of the divine turtle from the legend, lived in Hoan Kiem Lake until recent times. This creature’s presence tied contemporary life directly to the myth, bridging centuries of culture and memory. The turtle’s slow movements and occasional surfacing echoed the stillness and continuity of the place, even as the city thrummed around Here, the murmurs of daily life—street vendors’ calls, the clatter of bicycles, the rustle of leaves—blend with these stories, making Hoan Kiem Lake both a public square and a vessel of tradition. As you watch the sun shift on the water’s surface, the narratives of returned swords and sacred turtles remain embedded in the lake’s quiet depths.

Directions: Start your stroll by the serene waters of Hoan Kiem Lake, taking in the lovely views. Head towards the iconic red bridge that leads to Ngoc Son Temple, which you can see rising in the distance. Cross the bridge, and you'll find yourself at the entrance to the temple, nestled on an island in the lake. Enjoy exploring this beautiful and historic site! It is roughly 200 meters away.

Stop 2: Ngoc Son Temple

Address: Đinh Tiên Hoàng, Hàng Trống, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.030694, 105.852392

Type/Specialty: Historical Temple

Specific Examples: The iconic red bridge, intricate carvings, and serene atmosphere.

Orientation: Approaching Ngoc Son Temple, you cross the slender red-painted wooden bridge that arches over a small inlet of Hoan Kiem Lake. This vantage point allows a view of the temple’s layered rooflines and intricate carvings, poised as if floating above the water.

Ngoc Son Temple was begun in the autumn of 1841 during the Ly Dynasty to honor Tran Hung Dao, a 13th-century general famed for his leadership against Mongol invasions. Tran Hung Dao’s strategic brilliance halted the Mongol armies three times, protecting the Vietnamese kingdoms from conquest in the 1200s. The temple’s dedication to him anchors this place in a moment of Vietnam’s fierce resistance to foreign domination, continuing the spirit embodied by the nearby Hoan Kiem Lake.

Inside the temple grounds, a glass case holds the preserved shell of one of the four ancient turtles that once inhabited Hoan Kiem Lake. This turtle, having died in the lake, became a symbol of endurance and continuity, linking the natural world to the spiritual realm the temple embodies. Its shell, dark and weathered, carries the weight of centuries, marked by the slow passage of time in the waters it once swam.

Ngoc Son Temple also witnessed the August General Uprising, when Vietnamese forces rose up with fierce determination. This event marked a critical point in 20th-century history, as the people asserted their will against colonial rule. The temple’s walls, seeming silent now, once observed this upheaval and stand as a reminder of the resilience threaded through Hanoi’s past. The wooden beams creak softly with the breeze, mingling with the faint scent of incense rising from its altars, as the temple weaves together military valor, natural symbolism, and the pulse of rebellion into a single enduring presence.

Directions: From Ngoc Son Temple, stroll along the scenic Hoan Kiem Lake, enjoying the views as you make your way towards the bustling streets of the Old Quarter. Head towards Hàng Buồm Street, and as you walk, keep an eye out for the lively shops and street vendors that line the path. Continue until you reach Bach Ma Temple, which will be just off the street, nestled among the charming buildings of the area. It is roughly 600 meters away.

Stop 3: Bach Ma Temple

Address: Bạch Ma, Hàng Buồm, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.035752, 105.851032

Type/Specialty: Cultural Heritage Site

Specific Examples: The ancient architecture, traditional incense offerings, and vibrant murals.

Orientation: Stand with the temple’s entrance behind you, facing the narrow street where the hum of the Old Quarter’s commerce threads through the air.

Bach Ma Temple, dedicated to Princess Bach Hoa, stands as a testament to the Old Quarter’s long history. The Old Quarter itself developed over centuries east of the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long, eventually spreading across 76 streets within 10 wards. The temple you see today is not at its original location; it was moved from near Long Bien Bridge to this site to make way for the construction of a river dike. This relocation shows how the city’s growing infrastructure has reshaped even its sacred spaces.

One episode in the temple’s history sets it apart. During the reign of King Lê Cảnh Hưng, royal troops stopped at Princess Bach Hoa’s shrine before setting out on a campaign in Hung Hoa. According to the story, the campaign ended without a single drop of bloodshed.

The temple is cared for by Nguyen Tat Kim Hung, an artisan who maintains its intricate woodwork and lacquer finishes. Bach Ma Temple represents another layer of the city’s history, where royal gratitude and the practical needs of flood control and defense come together.

Directions: From Bach Ma Temple, head back to Bạch Ma Street, then turn left onto Hàng Buồm Street. Walk straight until you reach Đồng Xuân Market on your left. The lively sounds of vendors and the aroma of street food will be enticing as you approach. Continue to Dong Xuan Market — it is roughly 300 meters away.

Stop 4: Dong Xuan Market

Address: Đồng Xuân, Đông Xuân, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.038164, 105.850031

Type/Specialty: Market

Specific Examples: A variety of local goods, street food stalls, and vibrant market atmosphere.

Orientation: Standing at the main entrance, the broad expanse of the four-storey Dong Xuan Market building unfolds ahead, its iron frame and high ceilings sheltering a vast indoor maze.

Dong Xuan Market was erected in 1889 by the French colonial administration as part of a larger plan to reshape Hanoi’s urban landscape. Its construction replaced the two smaller markets that had occupied Hang Duong and Hang Ma streets at the close of the 19th century. This deliberate move centralized commerce in one large, covered space, offering the city’s merchants a new architectural container for trade. The market’s proximity to the Long Biên Bridge, completed in 1902, connected it to wider trade routes across the Red River, helping merchants bring goods from rural areas into the city.

One episode that marks Dong Xuan’s story is its role during the First Indochina War. On February 14, 1947, fierce fighting erupted here between Việt Minh forces and the French colonial troops. The market’s vicinity became a contested zone, its sturdy iron and brick structure bearing witness to the tension between colonial power and the burgeoning Vietnamese resistance. This moment anchors the market not only as a site of commerce but also as a stage of conflict reflecting the nation’s struggle for independence.

The market’s endurance was tested again in 1994 when a devastating fire nearly destroyed the building. Afterward, a thorough renovation restored Dong Xuan, preserving its role as Hanoi’s largest covered market. Today, the clamor of wholesale traders selling clothes, household goods, and foodstuffs fills the air. The texture of worn concrete floors underfoot, the metallic clang of stalls opening, and the mixed scents of spices and fresh produce speak to a place alive with adaptation and resilience.

Together, these stops reveal the enduring spirit of resistance and resilience that has shaped Hanoi’s identity through centuries of change. As you reflect on the stories of sacred spaces and vibrant markets, consider how the past continues to inform the present in this dynamic city. That's 4 stops in all.
