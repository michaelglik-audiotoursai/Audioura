Stop 1: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Orientation: This tour takes you directly to the Saigon Opera House, a singular stop where you will experience a striking example of French colonial architecture nestled within a bustling metropolis. The Saigon Opera House stands as a beacon of colonial grandeur and a testament to the city's resilience and transformation, embodying the juxtaposition between its historical significance and the modern life that surrounds it. The building’s green-shuttered windows and cream walls rise above the street noise, while the delicate ironwork balconies catch the sunlight. The sound of distant chatter and the occasional motorbike hum mingle with the faint echo of music that sometimes spills from inside, inviting you closer to this historic stage.

The building before you was designed under the watchful eyes of Eugène Ferret and Ernest Guichard, and constructed between 1898 and 1900. Its French colonial architecture reflects the era when Saigon was the capital of the State of Vietnam, known then as Đô thành Sài Gòn-Chợ Lớn. This Opera House has worn many faces: from a cultural venue to a seat of government. The solid walls that now host opera and ballet once held the weight of governance during a turbulent period in the city’s life.

During World War II, under Japanese occupation, the Opera House was damaged by Allied bombing in 1944. Inside, the rich golden chandeliers and sweeping staircases still hint at the elegance of a bygone era. Beneath the polished floors lies a secret: the basement served as a bomb shelter during the Vietnam War, a hidden refuge amid violence. The 1998 restoration of its façade, timed with Saigon’s 300th anniversary, brought back much of its original charm. This building pulses with the rhythms of a city that has reinvented itself again and again, its walls holding the music of art, law, and survival.
