Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Cathedral

Specific Examples: Stained glass windows, French colonial architecture, a serene garden area.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From the main street of Nguyễn Huệ, head east towards Công xã Paris street. You may notice the blend of colonial and modern architecture around you. Standing before the Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon, your gaze rises to the twin bell towers reaching 58 meters into the sky. The sound of distant traffic and street vendors blends with the occasional tolling of the cathedral’s bells. The red brick walls, weathered but steadfast, draw the eye as they absorb the tropical sunlight, their warmth contrasting with the cool shadow under the portico.

Originally named the Church of Saigon, this cathedral served the French Catholic community that settled here following the conquest of Cochinchina. The building’s two bell towers rise sharply, designed to call the faithful across the bustling city—their height a deliberate assertion of presence and permanence. In 1959, the cathedral’s forecourt became home to a statue of Our Lady of Peace. This addition marked a shift in the cathedral’s role for local Catholics, as the statue drew Vietnamese pilgrims who gathered to pray and seek comfort, especially during times of uncertainty. It became a focal point where ritual and faith intertwined with daily life, echoing the rhythms of the city beyond the church doors.

During the Vietnam War, the cathedral held a quieter but no less vital role. Amidst the chaos and conflict, locals sought refuge here. The cathedral’s interior, a cool sanctuary from the noisy streets, offered a space for prayer and solace. The tolling bells served as a reminder of continuity and hope amid upheaval. The cathedral’s steady presence, framed by the urban hum and the scent of incense from inside, links the colonial past with traditions that continue to shape Ho Chi Minh City’s everyday spiritual life.

Directions: Head southwest for about 600 meters, and you'll reach the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 2: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.7763, 106.6955

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: The war rooms, the grand reception hall, and the lush gardens.

Orientation: The wide driveway here stretches toward a fortress-like entrance, marked by a pair of heavy metal gates. Pause at this threshold, where history’s tide surged through in a moment that reshaped Vietnam forever.

This is the Independence Palace, designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ. It was once the official residence and workplace of the president of the Republic of Vietnam. The concrete façade and expansive windows reflect mid-20th century ambitions, built as a seat of power for a republic that lasted from 1967 to 1975. The palace replaced an earlier presidential residence that was damaged during the conflict, mirroring the nation’s turbulent quest for stability. The most defining moment in the palace’s history came on April 30, 1975, when armored vehicles broke through its gates, ending the Vietnam War and bringing down the regime that had governed here. This act led to the reunification of the country under communist rule. Even after reunification, the building continued to serve as a government office until 1976, when the capital moved to Hanoi. Today, the palace functions as a museum and convention hall. Its walls preserve the echoes of moments that altered the course of history. Where the cathedral spoke of colonial faith and French influence, the Independence Palace confronts you with the raw reality of war’s end and a nation’s rebirth.

Directions: Head southeast for about 500 meters, and you'll find yourself at Bến Thành Market. Enjoy the stroll!
