Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

The Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Sài Gòn, completed in 1880, was designed by the French architect Jules Bourard. The cathedral’s walls are built from red bricks imported all the way from Toulouse, France, grounding this Vietnamese landmark in a tangible thread of colonial-era craftsmanship. The twin spires, a defining feature, rise symmetrically, framing the statue of the Virgin Mary that presides at the center front, a quiet sentinel over the square. One significant episode reveals the cathedral’s architectural and cultural imprint: the French architect Jules Bourard’s work from 1877 to 1880 created a Neo-Romanesque façade with twin towers and the central statue of the Virgin Mary, giving the building its distinctive profile that remains a main attraction in the city.

Directions: Enjoy the stroll! Continue to Post Office — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Architectural Landmark

The Post Office was constructed between 1886 and 1891 and is now a tourist attraction. The Saigon Central Post Office, completed in 1891 after five years of construction, stands as a remarkable relic from the late 19th century when Vietnam was part of French Indochina. While many attribute its design to Gustave Eiffel, the engineer famed for the Eiffel Tower, it was in fact designed by Alfred Foulhoux, the Chief Architect of the Colony. Dương Văn Ngộ, known for being the last public letter writer in Vietnam, worked at the office from 1990 to 2021.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

You are standing in front of the Independence Palace, a historic landmark and government-owned public hall in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. This building is also officially known as the Reunification Convention Hall or simply Reunification Hall. The current palace was constructed after President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered its replacement in 1962, following damage to the old palace caused by bombing. The palace was designed by architect Ngo Viet Thu and completed in 1966 to replace the previous structure. From October 1967 to April 21, 1975, the palace served as the home and office of President Thiệu until he fled the country during the Ho Chi Minh Campaign. In November 1975, after negotiations between communist North Vietnam and South Vietnam, the Provisional Revolutionary Government renamed it Reunification Hall. The Independence Palace was the site of the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 that ended the Vietnam War, when a Liberation Army of South Vietnam tank crashed through its gates. Today, the Independence Palace is preserved with its authentic 1960s retro-modern interior and remains open as a historic site for visitors.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: photographs, military equipment

You are standing at the War Remnants Museum, located at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. The museum opened on 4 September 1975 as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes and is operated by the Ho Chi Minh City government. This museum focuses on researching, collecting, preserving, and displaying evidence of crimes and consequences of the war of aggression against Vietnam. Inside, you see photographs, artifacts, and personal stories that document the devastation of the war and its lasting impact on the Vietnamese people. The museum includes military equipment such as a Bell UH-1 Huey helicopter, a Northrop F-5A fighter, and an M48 Patton tank. You can also find exhibits on the effects of Agent Orange and other chemical defoliants, as well as war atrocities like the My Lai massacre.

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

The Municipal Theatre owes its specific characteristics to the work of architect Félix Olivier, while construction was under supervision of architects Ernest Guichard and Eugène Ferret in 1900. The intricate façade once featured lavish decorations and inscriptions, but many of these were removed in 1943 to simplify its appearance after criticism for being too ornate. In 1944, the theatre was damaged by the Allied aerial attacks against Imperial Japanese Army, and it stopped functioning. In 1998, for Saigon’s 300th anniversary, the city restored some of these decorative elements, so what you see now is a blend of original flair and careful preservation. When the government shifted to a bicameral system, the building housed both the Lower House and the Senate, serving as a political hub as well as a cultural one. Its role shifted from entertainment to governance, then back again in 1976 when it resumed its original function as a theatre. The Ho Chi Minh City Opera House was built during the French colonial period, from 1898 to 1900. Capacity 500 people. It is located in the center of town.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you'll reach City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Designed by the French architect Fernand Gardè, it draws directly from the classic town halls of Paris, blending Renaissance, Baroque, and Beaux-Arts styles into a striking ensemble. Its ornate facade, carved with symbols like wreaths and figures wearing Phrygian caps, reflects the ideals of the colonial power that shaped Saigon at the dawn of the 20th century. The clock tower at the center, topped with a flagpole, once announced the rhythms of colonial governance. When the building opened in 1909, the French Indochina governors-general gathered here to mark fifty years of French presence in Saigon. Locally, the building gained names like Dinh xã Tây—‘French municipal palace’—signaling its role as the seat of foreign administration.

You stand before the Café Apartments 42, a nine-story building on Nguyễn Huệ Walking Street in Ho Chi Minh City. Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Pier

You are standing at Bạch Đằng Pier, located at number 2 Tôn Đức Thắng street on the west bank of the Saigon River in Ho Chi Minh City. In 1881, Bạch Đằng Pier was transformed into the River port and entrusted to the management of the Compagnies des Messageries fluviales, while the replacement mercantile port was constructed in Khánh Hội. Bạch Đằng Pier is in District 1, near landmarks like the Bitexco Financial Tower and close to popular spots such as Nguyễn Huệ Walking Street and Bến Thành Market. The name Bạch Đằng recalls the historic Bạch Đằng River, famous for legendary battles where Vietnamese forces defeated invaders. Opposite the Liberty Central Saigon Riverside Hotel, this pier is also a bus stop on Tôn Đức Thắng street, making it a busy and accessible riverside spot.
