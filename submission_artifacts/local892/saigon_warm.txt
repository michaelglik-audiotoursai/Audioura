This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

The cathedral was completed in 1880, designed by architect Jules Bourard. It was built to replace a wooden church, finished in 1865, that had become too fragile for regular use due to severe termite damage. As a result, church services were temporarily moved to the guest chamber of the French Governor’s Palace. A key moment in the cathedral’s history came on October 7, 1877, when Bishop Isidore Colombert laid the foundation stone in a ceremony attended by the Deputy Commander of Cochinchina and other colonial officials. Construction took three years, and the official consecration was held on Easter Sunday, April 11, 1880. In front of the cathedral stands a 4.6-meter tall statue of the Blessed Virgin Mary, carved from white Carrara marble from Italy and weighing eight tons. The statue arrived in Saigon on February 15, 1959, after being shipped from Genoa, Italy, and was erected on a pedestal that had stood empty since 1945.

Directions: Head east for about a minute, and you'll find the Post Office waiting for you. Enjoy the stroll! It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historical Building

This building was constructed between 1886 and 1891 when Vietnam was part of French Indochina. It was designed by architect Alfred Foulhoux, whose work is often mistakenly credited to Gustave Eiffel. Eiffel's firm was involved in an earlier post office building in Saigon, built between 1860 and 1863, but the current structure is Foulhoux's design. A journal from 1891 praised Foulhoux's design for its artistic façade and practical layout. Inside, you can see a map from 1892 showing Saigon and its surroundings during the colonial era. From 1990 until 2021, Dương Văn Ngộ, known as the last public letter writer in Vietnam, worked here.

Directions: From the Post Office, head east on Nguyễn Thị Nghĩa Street for about 5 minutes. The lively atmosphere of street vendors will catch your attention. Continue to Book Street.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Spot

You are standing on Book Street, a narrow pedestrian street dedicated to books and reading in District 1 of Ho Chi Minh City. This street is about 100 meters long and features numerous book stalls and shops selling a wide variety of printed books, including literature, politics, society, science, and culture. Book Street is located on Nguyen Van Binh Street and is near famous landmarks like Notre Dame Cathedral and the Ho Chi Minh City Post Office. The street was established to promote a reading culture and provide a gathering space for book lovers. You can find entrances to Book Street from nearby Hai Ba Trung Street, and it is close to other popular city landmarks like Chợ Bến Thành and the Independence Palace. Regular events such as book fairs, author signings, and cultural activities take place here, attracting both locals and tourists.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you’ll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

This building replaced the old Norodom Palace, which had been badly damaged after two dissident Republic of Vietnam Air Force pilots bombed it. The Independence Palace was designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ and was the presidential palace, home and workplace of the president of the Republic of Vietnam. President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered its construction in 1962 but never saw its completion. The palace was completed in 1966 and later became the home and workplace of President Nguyễn Văn Thiệu, who inaugurated it and lived here from 1967 until April 1975. The Independence Palace was the site of the Fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975 that ended the Vietnam War, when a Liberation Army of South Vietnam tank crashed through its gates. Though the government offices moved to Hanoi in 1976, this palace remains a public space for state events and a museum open to visitors.

Directions: Head northwest for roughly 500 meters, and you'll find the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the walk!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

You are at the War Remnants Museum, located at 28 Võ Văn Tần Street in Ho Chi Minh City. An earlier version of this museum opened on 4 September 1975 as the Exhibition House for US and Puppet Crimes. This museum focuses on researching, collecting, preserving, and displaying evidence of the war of aggression against Vietnam. Through photographs, artifacts, and personal stories, it documents the devastation of the war and its lasting impact on the Vietnamese people. The museum attracts about half a million visitors every year, reflecting its significance in Vietnam.

Directions: Head east for about 1.2 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at the beautiful Opera House. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

This building, completed on January 1, 1900, was designed by the French architect Félix Olivier. Its façade, modeled after Paris’s Petit Palais built the same year, displays the flamboyant style typical of the French Third Republic. The finely detailed inscriptions and reliefs on the front were drawn by a French artist and shipped directly from France, marking the Opera House as a true import of European theatrical culture. In 1944, the theatre was damaged by the Allied aerial attacks against Imperial Japanese Army, and it stopped functioning. After the Fall of Saigon in 1975, the Opera House’s story took a sharp turn. It became the venue for the Provisional Revolutionary Government’s People’s Assembly, shifting from a center of entertainment to the seat of political authority during a crucial moment in Vietnam’s unification. When the legislature split into two houses, the building accommodated both the Lower House and the Senate. Today, the sounds inside are a blend of distant traffic and music drifting from rehearsals—reminders that this is still a living theater.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you'll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

You stand before the grand yellow facade of City Hall, built between 1898 and 1909 during the French colonial period as a less ornate version of Paris's Hotel de Ville. Locals called it Dinh xã Tây, meaning 'French municipal palace,' or Dinh đốc lý, 'mayor's palace.' After World War II, the South Vietnamese government converted this building into the city hall, and today it houses the Ho Chi Minh City People's Committee. In the garden courtyard, you can see a statue of Ho Chi Minh teaching a child, symbolizing the city's history and future. For the first time ever, on April 29 and 30, 2023, the building was opened to the public to celebrate Reunification Day, inviting everyone to connect with this historic landmark.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you'll arrive at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

You stand before the Café Apartments 42, a nine-story building on Nguyễn Huệ Walking Street in Ho Chi Minh City. This building was constructed in the 1960s and originally served as a dormitory for officers and civil servants. Today, the Café Apartments 42 has transformed into a cultural hub filled with dozens of independent cafés, handmade shops, and nail salons. From the street, the building looks like a giant grid box with many little cafés, hidden corners, and balconies spread across its nine floors. You might also hear it called The Café Apartments or simply 42 Nguyễn Huệ.

Directions: Head east for roughly 350 meters, and you'll arrive at Bạch Đằng Pier. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Spot

You stand at Bạch Đằng Pier, located at number 2 Ton Duc Thang street in the center of Ho Chi Minh City, on the west bank of the Saigon River in District 1. In 1881, Bạch Đằng Pier was transformed into the River port and entrusted to the management of the Compagnies des Messageries fluviales, while the replacement mercantile port was constructed in Khánh Hội. This pier includes a port and a park stretching 1.3 kilometers long and covering 23,400 square meters, with views where modern skyscrapers meet colonial heritage. According to scholar Pétrus Ký, the waterfront area at the end of rue Catinat here was once called Bến Ngự, meaning the royal landing stage, suggesting this site’s history dates back to the 17th century when Saigon was still the Cambodian settlement of Prey Nokor. From the late 18th century, near the former Ba Son Shipyard just along this riverbank, Vietnamese warships were built and gathered. The name Bạch Đằng itself recalls the historic river where Vietnamese forces won legendary battles against invaders, linking this place to a proud maritime heritage.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at Thi Bar. Enjoy the walk!

Thi Bar offers cocktails and beer, and while food options are limited, you can ask if bringing outside food is allowed. It sits in the busy backpacker area near Bùi Viện Street, making it a popular spot in Saigon's tourist district.
