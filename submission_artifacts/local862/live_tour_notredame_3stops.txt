Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779785, 106.699020

Type/Specialty: Historical Church

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, impressive stained glass windows, and a peaceful garden area.

Orientation: At the cathedral, you will witness the remarkable size and unique design of this architectural masterpiece. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From the main street, head southeast on Lê Duẩn, then turn left onto Công xã Paris. The cathedral will be on your right. You might notice the vibrant atmosphere as you approach the cathedral. The first thing to register here is the steady tolling of bells from the twin towers above, their deep resonance threading through the bustle of downtown Ho Chi Minh City. Standing before the cathedral’s red-brick walls, the cool shadow under its soaring arches offers a calm pause amid the city’s rhythm.

The bricks here tell of that colonial era — imported red bricks and tiles from Marseille set this cathedral apart, their original materials still intact on the roof, a physical trace of French ambition and religious aspiration. The cathedral’s construction between 1877 and 1880 reflects a deliberate effort by French colonial authorities to imprint a European religious landmark on the city’s identity. Its imposing presence and imported materials contrast with the tropical environment around it, signaling both a spiritual and political statement of the time. The smell of incense from inside mingles with the city’s humid air, a reminder of the ongoing life and ritual contained within these walls. As the bells fade into the city's flow, the cathedral stands as a monument to a particular moment when Saigon’s urban fabric embraced a European vision — a vision that still shapes how the city’s past and present converse around this sacred space.

Directions: Head southwest for about 500 meters, and you'll arrive at the Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 2: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.776994, 106.695305

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: The iconic war room, lush gardens, and the presidential reception areas.

Orientation: Standing before the grand façade of Independence Palace, the wide expanse of its forecourt opens up like a stage set for history. The entrance gates here mark the threshold where one of the most pivotal moments in Vietnam’s modern era unfolded. As you position yourself near these gates, the scale and solidity of the building become unmistakable, inviting a deeper look beyond its walls into the turbulent past embedded here.

This palace, designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ, served as the official residence and workplace of the president of the Republic of Vietnam. Its modernist design reflects an era when Vietnam sought to project stability and progress amid internal and external conflict. In 1975, the entry of a tank here symbolized reunification and marked the end of the war, making the site a physical marker of profound political transformation. After reunification, the palace continued in government use until 1976, when the capital and most administrative functions moved to Hanoi. Today, the palace endures as a public hall and museum, preserving the layers of its past. The concrete walls and interiors still hold echoes of those tense final days and the decisions once made in the president’s underground bunker—a space where the fate of a nation was debated in silence.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 600 meters, and you'll find yourself at Bến Thành Market. Enjoy the stroll!
