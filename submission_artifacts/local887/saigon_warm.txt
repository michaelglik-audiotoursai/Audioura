Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, stained glass windows, and a tranquil atmosphere.

The Saigon Notre Dame Cathedral was completed in 1880 and designed by the French architect Jules Bourard. The cathedral’s twin bell towers rise prominently, a feature that made it a visible landmark in the city long before the skyline grew taller. This statue was installed in 1959, replacing an earlier bronze statue removed after 1945. The statue’s presence marks a moment when the square’s meaning evolved alongside the city’s changing political landscape.

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic Post Office

Specific Examples: Impressive vaulted ceilings, intricate mosaics, and vintage postal equipment.

Orientation: Approach it from the bustling Paris Commune Square, where the sounds of city life—honking scooters, distant calls of vendors—blend with the steady tick of time held within these walls.

Between 1886 and 1891, this building took shape under the direction of architects Auguste Henri Vildieu and Alfred Foulhoux. Completed in the style known as eclecticism—a fusion of Gothic, Renaissance, and French colonial elements—the post office was inaugurated in 1891. Although Gustave Eiffel’s name is often associated with this site, it was Alfred Foulhoux whose design brought the building to life. That year marked not only the completion of the structure, but also the arrival of a monument praised in the architectural journal Architecte constructeur for its artistic façade and practical layout, designed to meet the colony’s communication needs. Among the human stories connected to this place is that of the last public letter writer in Vietnam, who served here from 1990 until 2021. His presence marked the final chapter of a centuries-old tradition in a city where handwritten words have gradually given way to digital communication.

Directions: From the Post Office, walk east on Công xã Paris Street, then turn left onto Nguyễn Văn Bình Street. The colorful book stalls will greet you as you enter. Continue to Book Street — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Book Street

Specific Examples: Stalls filled with books in various languages, cozy reading spots, and local author signings.

Orientation: Standing on the narrow Nguyen Van Binh Street, your eyes catch the rows of book stalls aligning this pedestrian-only lane, their shelves spilling over with volumes of all sizes.

You are on Nguyen Van Binh Street, known as Book Street, in District 1 of Ho Chi Minh City. This pedestrian-only street is about 100 meters long and closed to motorized traffic, making it safe and pleasant to walk here. You can browse numerous book stalls and shops that sell a wide variety of printed books in different genres, including literature, politics, society, science, and culture. Book Street is located near major landmarks like the Notre Dame Cathedral, Ho Chi Minh City Post Office, and the Saigon River waterfront. The street was established to promote a reading culture and hosts cultural events such as book fairs, author signings, and literary discussions. You can enter Book Street from Hai Ba Trung Street and enjoy the colorful and charming atmosphere created by the book stalls.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: Stately rooms, lush gardens, and historical exhibits about Vietnam's history.

This place was designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ, who began construction on 1 July 1962. President Ngô Đình Diệm commissioned the new building to replace the old palace, which had been damaged by bombing. The building officially opened on 31 October 1966 under General Nguyễn Văn Thiệu, who used it as his office and residence. These marks are a physical trace of the political violence that shaped the palace’s story. The palace is best known for the dramatic end of the Vietnam War here on 30 April 1975. The tank’s path through the gate remains a vivid reminder of how history can break through the everyday order of a place. After reunification, the building served briefly for government use until 1976, when the capital moved to Hanoi. Today, it hosts state events and welcomes visitors eager to witness the layers of history embedded in its walls.

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning architecture, performances ranging from ballet to traditional Vietnamese opera, and guided tours.

This building opened its doors on January 1, 1900, as the Opéra de Saïgon, a venue designed to bring French theatrical culture to the colonial city. Construction was overseen by Ernest Guichard and Eugène Ferret. Its façade, inspired by the Petit Palais in Paris, features the flamboyant style of the French Third Republic, with decorative reliefs and inscriptions that once drew criticism for their ornate excess. The very seats where audiences once applauded ballet and opera also witnessed political decisions that shaped the nation’s future. Today, the scent of aged stone mingles with the distant sound of rehearsals, reminding visitors that this venue has returned to its artistic roots after decades of political transformation.
