Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, intricate stained glass windows, and the iconic statue of the Virgin Mary.

Orientation: As you traverse this urban landscape, you will encounter a blend of French colonial architecture and the bustling modern life that defines the city. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From Nguyễn Thị Nghĩa Street, head southeast on Lê Duẩn Street, then turn left onto Công xã Paris. The cathedral will be on your left. Stand near the broad open plaza before the cathedral, where the twin bell towers rise sharply against the sky. From here, the building’s red brick walls and soaring arches invite a close look at the stately facade and the statue of the Virgin Mary that guards this place.

The cathedral you see was completed in 1880 after several years of construction initiated by French colonial administrators. Before this stone basilica took shape, a simple wooden church stood along the Charner Canal in 1863. Built to serve the growing Catholic community in Saigon following the French conquest, that early structure quickly became too small for the faithful. This need led to the decision to replace it with the grander, more permanent edifice that stands here today. The two bell towers reach 58 meters high, their spires piercing the sky above the busy streets.

The cathedral’s original name, the Church of Saigon, changed in 1959 when a statue of Our Lady of Peace was placed in the forecourt. This statue now watches over the square and serves as a focal point for many who gather here. The statue, added decades after the cathedral’s completion, transformed the site into a symbol of hope and calm amid the city’s pulse.

Each year, the cathedral hosts vibrant Catholic celebrations, drawing expats and locals alike. As the bells ring out from the twin towers, the cathedral stands as a witness to the layers of faith and community life woven through Ho Chi Minh City’s streets.

Stop 2: Saigon Central Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Architectural Landmark

Specific Examples: Beautiful colonial architecture, a large mural of Vietnam, and vintage postal services.

Orientation: Stand just outside the main entrance on Paris Commune Square, with the Saigon Notre-Dame Basilica visible nearby. This vantage reveals the post office’s long façade, its arched windows framing the bustling street sounds and the steady rhythm of footsteps on the stone pavement. The air carries a faint hint of ink and paper from inside, blending with the city’s warm breeze.

The Saigon Central Post Office opened its doors in 1891 after five years of construction, a fact recorded in the September 15 issue of the journal Architecte constructeur. The inauguration ceremony was originally scheduled for July 14 of that year but was postponed until the Governor General returned to the city, marking its official entry into service. Its design is often mistakenly attributed to Gustave Eiffel, whose engineering signature is found elsewhere in Saigon’s earlier 1860 Steel Wire Department. The vaulted iron roof, supported by intricate metalwork, lets light fall evenly across the desks where locals and tourists alike send postcards and parcels. Inside, the steady murmur of conversation mingles with the clatter of postal workers stamping envelopes and sorting letters. Both buildings stand as enduring markers of a time when the city’s identity was being reshaped through architecture and infrastructure. The Central Post Office remains a functioning gateway to the past, where the tactile experience of stamped letters persists amidst the passage of time.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Exhibits on the Vietnam War, military vehicles, and powerful photography.

Orientation: Position yourself to best view this location.

The War Remnants Museum opens onto a narrative deeply etched in Vietnam’s modern experience. Its building once housed the United States Information Agency’s offices before 1975. On September 4, 1975, the Ho Chi Minh City government opened it as the Exhibition House for U.S. and Puppet Crimes, a deliberate act to confront the recent past. Inside, the air is thick with the weight of history, and you can hear the faint echo of footsteps mingling with the occasional hum of conversation. The exhibits catalogue the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War with unflinching directness. Large military machines stand silently in the courtyards, their rough metal surfaces telling a story of war’s machinery now at rest. Walls lined with photographs and artifacts capture moments of pain and endurance, each image a witness to lives altered irrevocably. In 2024, it was ranked Asia’s No. 1 museum by Tripadvisor, a reflection not of simple popularity but of a sustained engagement with its difficult truths. Here, history is not a distant echo but an enduring presence etched into the city’s fabric.

Stop 6: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, ballet performances, and musical shows.

Orientation: The best place to pause is just across the street, where the entire frontage of this building can be scanned without interruption.

The Saigon Opera House was designed by Eugène Ferret in 1911 to host up to 500 guests, reflecting its original role as a municipal theatre—a place for performance and gathering rather than just architectural display. Its curved facade and intricate motifs were a deliberate choice by the French colonial administration, aiming to establish a cultural foothold in what was then known as Saigon. Today, the opera house stands as a physical reminder of that older identity, even as it continues to serve modern performances. Ferret’s decision to design a theatre of this scale set the tone for social gatherings that balanced exclusivity with accessibility. Here, the city’s cultural life unfolds in a building that quietly bridges its past as Saigon with its present as Ho Chi Minh City. Turning the corner from this spot, the commanding presence of Ho Chi Minh City Hall awaits, another marker of the city’s layered history.

Stop 7: Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building)

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Impressive French architecture, beautiful gardens, and illuminated at night.

Orientation: The broad, cream-colored stone steps rise gently before you, leading to a grand facade crowned by a clock tower. The building stretches horizontally, framed by manicured gardens that soften the formal geometry. From this vantage point, the intricate wrought iron balconies and the tall French windows catch the sunlight, their shadows casting delicate patterns on the walls.

The French designed this building as a symbol of their administrative power and intended it to represent their permanence in the city. Its ornate architecture, with classical columns and sculpted details, echoes city halls in France and establishes a European presence in the tropical landscape. After 1975, when communist forces took control of Saigon, the building’s role changed dramatically. It became the office of the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee, serving as a local government center under the new regime. Today, the quiet hum of city traffic and the distant chatter from the gardens remind us that this building remains a living part of Ho Chi Minh City’s civic life, connecting its colonial past with its present-day governance.
