Stop 1: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam. Your first stop is Independence Palace. Standing near the front gates, the wide expanse of the palace stretches before you, its clean lines and pale walls catching the sunlight. The broad lawn and orderly gardens frame the building, inviting a closer look at the austere yet distinctive architecture designed to command attention and function as a seat of power.

The Independence Palace you see today was commissioned by President Ngô Đình Diệm in 1962 to replace the old presidential residence, which had been heavily damaged by bombing from dissident pilots in the Republic of Vietnam Air Force. This destruction forced a fresh start, and Diệm called upon architect Ngô Viết Thụ to create a new symbol of governance that combined modernist design with touches of traditional Vietnamese style. One of the most defining moments for this palace came on April 30, 1975. The distinct fusion of 1960s architecture and the weight of these historic moments make this stop a vivid reminder of the rhythms of political power and upheaval that have shaped Ho Chi Minh City.
