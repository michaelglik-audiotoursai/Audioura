Stop 1: Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, intricate stained glass windows, and the iconic statue of the Virgin Mary.

Operational Details: Open daily from 8 AM to 5 PM; free entry.

Orientation: As you traverse this urban landscape, you will encounter a blend of French colonial architecture and the bustling modern life that defines the city. Your first stop is Notre-Dame Cathedral Basilica of Saigon. From Nguyễn Thị Nghĩa Street, head southeast on Lê Duẩn Street, then turn left onto Công xã Paris. The cathedral will be on your left. You might notice the bustling atmosphere of the surrounding area. Stand near the broad open plaza before the cathedral, where the twin bell towers rise sharply against the sky. From here, the building’s red brick walls and soaring arches unfold, inviting a close look at the stately facade and the statue of the Virgin Mary that guards this place.

The cathedral you see was completed in 1880 after several years of construction initiated by French colonial administrators. Before this stone basilica took shape, in 1863 a simple wooden church stood along the Charner Canal. That early structure, built to serve the growing Catholic community in Saigon following French conquest, quickly became too small for the faithful. This need drove the decision to replace it with the grander, more permanent edifice here. The two bell towers reach 58 meters high, their spires piercing the sky above the busy streets. The cathedral’s original name, the Church of Saigon, changed in 1959 when a statue of Our Lady of Peace was placed in the forecourt. This statue now watches over the square, a focal point for many who gather here. The weight of history presses softly in the air as the bells toll, threading the sound of daily life with the rhythm of this place. Each year, the cathedral hosts vibrant Catholic celebrations, drawing expats and locals alike. The wooden church of 1863 was a modest beginning that met an urgent need for worship space, but it was the vision of the colonial administrators to build something enduring. The statue of Our Lady of Peace, added decades later, transformed the site into a symbol of hope and calm amid the city’s pulse. As the bells ring out from the twin towers, the cathedral stands as a witness to the layers of faith and community life woven through Ho Chi Minh City’s streets.

Stop 2: Saigon Central Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Architectural Landmark

Specific Examples: Beautiful colonial architecture, a large mural of Vietnam, and vintage postal services.



Orientation: Stand just outside the main entrance on Paris Commune Square, with the Saigon Notre-Dame Basilica visible nearby. This vantage reveals the post office’s long façade, its arched windows framing the bustling street sounds and the steady rhythm of footsteps on the stone pavement. The air carries a faint hint of ink and paper from inside, blending with the city’s warm breeze.

The Saigon Central Post Office opened its doors in 1891 after five years of construction, a fact recorded in the September 15 issue of the journal Architecte constructeur. The inauguration ceremony, originally scheduled for July 14 of that year, was postponed until the Governor General returned to the city, marking its official entry into service. Its design is often mistakenly attributed to Gustave Eiffel, whose engineering signature is found elsewhere in Saigon’s earlier 1860 Steel Wire Department. Inside, the steady murmur of conversation mingles with the clatter of postal workers stamping envelopes and sorting letters. The vaulted iron roof, supported by intricate metalwork, lets light fall evenly across the desks where locals and tourists alike send postcards and parcels. Both buildings stand as enduring markers of a time when the city’s identity was being reshaped through architecture and infrastructure. The Central Post Office remains a functioning gateway to the past, where the tactile experience of stamped letters persists amidst the passage of time.

Stop 3: Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Attraction

Specific Examples: Numerous book stalls, cozy reading spots, and occasional literary events.



Orientation: The rustle of pages turning and muted conversations rise softly from open stalls lining both sides, surrounded by tree-shaded benches and small cafés, their tables crowded with books and local readers.

Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street is the deliberate product of Ho Chi Minh City’s recent cultural ambitions, created as a hub to promote reading and literature. Over the past decade, this narrow street has evolved into a gathering place for readers and writers, nurturing a community centered on book culture — a detail that marks a shift in urban life here. The development of Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street stands as a conscious cultural intervention in a city where the population has exceeded 14 million as of 2025, spilling over with commerce and industry. Literature claims its own domain near the crowd and clatter of Vietnam’s largest financial center, which contributes roughly a quarter of the country’s total GDP. The establishment of this street as a cultural hub fosters reading. In the decade since, this stretch has transformed into a popular rendezvous for reading enthusiasts, weaving new social fabric where books act as connectors in a rapidly urbanizing landscape. The quiet rustling of pages, the tactile weight of books stacked along shops, and the murmured discussions in the cafés all manifest this effort to carve out a literary refuge in a sprawling metropolis. As you stand here, the subtle sounds and smells form a deliberate counterpoint to the transportation hubs and financial towers nearby—reminders of a city balancing commerce and culture.

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The Presidential Office, war rooms, and beautiful gardens.

Operational Details: Open daily from 7:30 AM to 5 PM; entry fee is 40,000 VND.

Orientation: Stand near the wide front lawn and entrance of the Independence Palace, where the clean lines of the building frame the vivid courtyard beyond. The broad windows and sturdy doors hint at the layers of history held inside.

The Independence Palace, known locally as the Reunification Hall, is a place where the pulse of history caught its sharpest rhythm in the mid-20th century. Nearly every surface here reflects moments that shaped the city’s fate. The explosion did not kill Diệm, but it left marks, both physical and symbolic, on this seat of power. Their plan aimed to disrupt a government they opposed, but instead, the palace continued as the nerve center of leadership, revealing the simmering tensions of that era. The palace witnessed the closing of a chapter that reverberated far beyond these walls. The rooms you glimpse through the windows still hold the tables, war rooms, and the presidential office where history was made, frozen as if the clocks stopped in 1975. Outside, the gardens offer a subtle contrast—a calm that belies the thunderous events lived here. Close by is Nguyễn Văn Bình Book Street, a reminder of the city’s ongoing stories, now inked rather than bombed.

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Exhibits on the Vietnam War, military vehicles, and powerful photography.

Operational Details: Open daily from 7:30 AM to 6 PM; entry fee is 40,000 VND.

Orientation: Position yourself to best view this location.

The War Remnants Museum opens onto a narrative deeply etched in Vietnam’s modern experience, its very building once the United States Information Agency’s offices before 1975. This is no neutral archive; it began its life on September 4, 1975, as the Exhibition House for U.S. and Puppet Crimes, a deliberate act by the Ho Chi Minh City government to confront the recent past. Inside, the air is thick with the weight of history, and you can hear the faint echo of footsteps mingling with the occasional hum of conversation. The exhibits catalogue the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War with unflinching directness. Large military machines stand silently in the courtyards, their rough metal surfaces telling a story of war’s machinery now at rest. Walls lined with photographs and artifacts capture moments of pain and endurance, each image a witness to lives altered irrevocably. In 2024, it was ranked Asia’s No. 1 museum by Tripadvisor, a reflection not of simple popularity but of a sustained engagement with its difficult truths. Here, history is not a distant echo but an enduring presence etched into the city’s fabric.

Stop 6: Saigon Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780



Operational Details: Open for events; check schedule for performance times and ticket prices.

Orientation: The best place to pause is just across the street, where the entire frontage of this building can be scanned without interruption.

This building was designed by Eugène Ferret in 1911 to host up to 500 guests, a figure that reflects its original role as a municipal theatre—a place intended for performance and gathering rather than just architectural showmanship. Its curved facade and intricate motifs mark a deliberate choice by the French colonial administration to establish a cultural foothold in what was then Saigon, the historic and colloquial name for the central area now officially reorganized as Saigon ward following the 2025 nationwide administrative reform of Ho Chi Minh City. The opera house stands as a physical reminder of that older identity, even as it continues to serve modern performances. Eugène Ferret’s choice to design a theatre for 500 guests was not arbitrary; it set a clear scale for social gatherings that balanced exclusivity with accessibility. Here, the city’s cultural life unfolds in a building that quietly bridges its past as Saigon with its present as Ho Chi Minh City. Turning the corner from this spot, the commanding presence of Ho Chi Minh City Hall awaits, another marker of the city’s layered history.

Stop 7: Ho Chi Minh City Hall (People's Committee Building)

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970



Operational Details: Open to the public for photos; no entry fee.

Orientation: The broad, cream-colored stone steps rise gently before you, leading to a grand facade crowned by a clock tower. The building stretches horizontally, framed by manicured gardens that soften the formal geometry. From this vantage point, the intricate wrought iron balconies and the tall French windows catch the sunlight, their shadows casting delicate patterns on the walls.

This was no mere gesture of colonial pride; the French intended this building to be a symbol of their administrative power and permanence here. The ornate architecture, with its classical columns and sculpted details, speaks to that ambition, echoing city halls back in France while planting a European footprint in the tropical cityscape. The building’s role shifted dramatically after 1975, when the communist forces took control of Saigon. It became the office of the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee, a local government body under the new regime. Here, the quiet hum of city traffic blends with the distant chatter from the gardens, a reminder that this building remains a living part of Ho Chi Minh City’s civic pulse, entwined with its colonial past and its present-day governance.

This change reveals how the building’s function evolved — from a utilitarian housing block to a creative hub, where the city’s energy infuses every corner. Just as the People’s Committee Building, which you passed earlier, stands as a symbol of governance and civic authority, 42 Nguyễn Huệ encapsulates the intimate scale of state life before becoming a stage for private enterprise and cultural exchange. Though distant, this act of redefining space and purpose resonates here as well. The Café Apartments embody that same process on a human scale: a structure once designed for containment now opens outward to connection. Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Scenic Spot

Specific Examples: Beautiful river views, local boat tours, and evening lights.



Orientation: Standing near the edge of Bạch Đằng Pier, the wide Saigon River opens before you, its surface marked by the rhythm of boats departing and arriving. The air carries the faint mix of river water and diesel engines, while the chatter of boat crews and the clinking of ropes punctuate the space. Facing upriver, you see the steady flow of vessels crossing between this pier and the opposite bank, a line of motion that ties together the city’s past and present.

Bạch Đằng Pier has long anchored itself as a vital river crossing point in Ho Chi Minh City’s network of waterways. Historically serving as a quay, it connected this side of the Saigon River to Thủ Thiêm Pier, enabling the movement of people and goods before bridges and highways dominated the landscape. This connection across the river was the lifeblood of commerce and daily transit, linking neighborhoods like Bình Mỹ to the west and the emerging ward of Thủ Dầu Một to the north. The pier’s continued vitality is evident as it functions today as the launching point for tours to historically significant sites such as the Củ Chi Tunnels. Visitors boarding boats here trace a route that echoes the strategic importance of river transport in Vietnam’s complex 20th-century history, though the pier’s role is fundamentally grounded in its geography and function rather than abstract memory. The steady hum of engines and the lapping of water against the pier’s hulls add texture to this space, linking it to the river’s constant flow of life. This spot forms a tangible link to the themes explored earlier on your tour, particularly the Café Apartments at 42 Nguyễn Huệ, where communal urban life unfolds vertically, while here at Bạch Đằng Pier, the river shapes horizontal connectivity. As evening approaches, the scene promises transition: the river’s reflective surface will soon catch the glow of lights from the nearby Thi Bar Saigon, where the city’s nightlife pulses anew along the waterfront.
