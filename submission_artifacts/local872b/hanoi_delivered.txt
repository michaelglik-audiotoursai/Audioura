Stop 1: Hoan Kiem Lake

Address: Đinh Tiên Hoàng, Hàng Trống, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.028889, 105.852500

Type/Specialty: Scenic Lake

Specific Examples: The Turtle Tower, beautiful walking paths, and local vendors.

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Old Quarter walk, Hanoi. Your first stop is Hoan Kiem Lake. From Hoàn Kiếm Square, head southeast on Đinh Tiên Hoàng street for about 5 minutes until you reach the lake. You might notice the calmness of the water contrasting with the bustling city around. Standing on the eastern edge of Hoan Kiem Lake, you face the reflective surface that mirrors the sky and the crowded streets of Hoàn Kiếm district beyond. To your left, the Hanoi Metropolitan People's Committee building on Đinh Tiên Hoàng street anchors this blend of urban life, governance, and commerce.

Hoan Kiem Lake takes its name from a pivotal moment in 15th-century Vietnam, when Emperor Lê Lợi returned a magical sword to a divine turtle living in these waters. Before this legend shaped its identity, the lake was known as Luc Thuy Lake. The act of returning the sword marked the symbolic end of centuries of Chinese rule and transformed the lake into a living emblem of national liberation.

Beyond legend, the lake once harbored a real giant turtle, believed by locals to be the earthly incarnation of the divine creature from the tale. The rare appearance of this turtle was a moment of quiet awe for residents, sustaining the connection between myth and reality and anchoring the community’s reverence for the spot.

Hoan Kiem is one of Hanoi’s original four urban districts, and as of 2019, it had a population of 135,618. Its northern half, where you stand, is the Old Quarter—a maze of small street blocks and alleys that preserve the traditional Vietnamese way of life. The lake acts as the natural heart of the district, where daily rhythms—morning tai chi, vendors’ calls, and the flow of locals and visitors crossing the iconic red Huc Bridge—unfold against the backdrop of history. The waters of Hoan Kiem Lake link the urban pulse of commerce and governance to a deeper cultural rhythm, weaving together the everyday life of its residents with the legend that gave the lake its name. As you observe the gentle ripples on the surface, the story of the real turtle living here brings the past into the present, connecting myth, history, and the living city along its edges.

Directions: As you approach the iconic red bridge, head towards it, which leads you directly to Ngọc Sơn Temple nestled on the small island. Take your time to soak in the sights and sounds of the Old Quarter as you make your way to this charming temple. It is roughly 200 meters away.

Stop 2: Ngọc Sơn Temple

Address: Đảo Ngọc, Hàng Trống, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.030694, 105.852392

Type/Specialty: Cultural Temple

Specific Examples: Traditional architecture, beautiful statues, and serene surroundings.

Orientation: From the edge of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, where you paused earlier by the water’s edge, the Ngọc Sơn Temple appears across the narrow red-painted Thê Húc Bridge. Stand at the foot of this wooden bridge, its lacquered beams creaking faintly underfoot, and gaze toward the temple’s tiled roofs rising against the sky, set on a small islet that seems to float on the lake’s surface.

Ngọc Sơn Temple stands on a tranquil islet in Hoàn Kiếm Lake, its architecture blending Vietnamese traditional design with Confucian ideals. The temple’s origins trace back to the autumn of 1841, during the reign of the Lý Dynasty. It is dedicated to Văn Xương, also known as Wenchang Dijun, a Taoist deity revered as the patron of scholars and culture. This dedication highlights the temple’s role as a spiritual beacon for those seeking wisdom and literary success, anchoring it in Vietnam’s intellectual traditions.

Within these walls, the story of Trần Hưng Đạo also unfolds. This 13th-century general famously repelled Mongol invasions in 1285, 1287, and 1288, defending Đại Việt’s sovereignty against the Yuan dynasty. The temple houses a preserved giant turtle, which connects directly to the legend of Hoàn Kiếm Lake. The turtle symbolizes strength and wisdom, tying the natural world to the mythic narrative that shaped the lake’s identity. Local tradition holds that this turtle was the guardian of the lake’s sacred sword, a gift from the heavens that helped the emperor defeat invaders centuries ago. Though the exact details of the turtle’s presence remain open to interpretation, its symbolism permeates the temple’s atmosphere.

Ngọc Sơn Temple’s location and its homage to figures like Trần Hưng Đạo link it to the broader narrative of Vietnam’s resilience and cultural continuity. Just beyond this sacred site, the bustling streets of the Old Quarter await, where the weight of history meets the vitality of everyday life.

Directions: Start by leaving Ngọc Sơn Temple and stroll along the edge of Hoàn Kiếm Lake, taking in the views. As you walk, head towards Hàng Buồm Street, where you'll find vibrant shops and street vendors. Continue exploring the lively atmosphere of the Old Quarter, filled with bustling energy and charming architecture. It is roughly 450 meters away.

Stop 3: Old Quarter

Address: Hàng Buồm, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.034775, 105.851150

Type/Specialty: Historic District

Specific Examples: Narrow streets, traditional shops, and street food stalls.

Orientation: Stand near the edge of the wide street where the Old Quarter opens before you, its dense network of shops and narrow lanes stretching ahead. Look for signs marking the Hoàn Kiếm District — this quarter covers about 100 hectares and comprises 76 streets that unfold into 10 wards.

The Old Quarter has long been the historical civic center of Hanoi, situated just outside the walls of the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long. Its layout took shape centuries ago during the Lý and Trần dynasties, when the area grew into a hive of commerce and residence along the rivers feeding into the Red River. Today, the hum of motorbikes and lively chatter mixes with the occasional distant call from the Ngọc Sơn Temple, creating a living mosaic of Hanoi’s evolving urban core. The contrast between the layered, bustling fabric of the Old Quarter and precise historical markers abroad—such as the 1909 replacement of Liberty’s image with Abraham Lincoln on the U.S. penny, and later presidents appearing on the quarter and nickel—offers a sense of how both cities and currencies anchor identity and transformation.

Directions: From the Old Quarter, stroll down Hàng Buồm Street, soaking in the vibrant atmosphere and the local shops along the way. Keep an eye out for the colorful storefronts as you make your way to Bach Ma Temple, which will be on your right. The temple’s distinctive architecture will be hard to miss, so just enjoy the sights as you approach! It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 4: Bach Ma Temple

Address: 76 Hàng Buồm, Hoàn Kiếm, Hà Nội, Vietnam

Coordinates: 21.035752, 105.851032

Type/Specialty: Historic Temple

Specific Examples: Ancient architecture, incense offerings, and local worshippers.

Bach Ma Temple traces its roots back to the 11th century during the reign of Emperor Ly Thai To, who ordered its construction to honor Long Do, an ancient guardian deity believed to be linked with the early days of this city. Long Do’s role was significant enough that the temple became a key place for worshipers seeking protection and blessings at a time when Thăng Long—the old name for Hanoi—was still taking its shape. The dragon imagery associated with the city’s founding is whispered here in the stone and wood, though local stories diverge on the exact details of how Long Do came to be worshiped. This temple has long served not just as a religious site, but as a gathering place for the community during important festivals and ceremonies. The temple’s location outside the Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long places it within the historical civic core where the rhythms of trade and faith interwove. The air here carries the soft crackle of incense and muted conversations that continue these age-old rituals. One story passed down through the neighborhood is that Emperor Ly Thai To himself ordered the temple’s founding to honor Long Do, seeking divine guidance to establish the capital on the banks of the Red River. This act linked the temple directly to the very birth of Hanoi’s identity, bridging spiritual belief and political decision. These gatherings reinforced social bonds, layered over centuries in the fabric of Hanoi’s urban life. Here at Bach Ma Temple, the pulse of the Old Quarter continues in a quieter key, a place where the city’s founding spirit endures through the everyday devotion and the shared stories of its people.

Together, these stops reveal the deep interconnection between legend, spirituality, and the daily life of Hanoi’s Old Quarter. This rich tapestry of history and culture invites you to reflect on how the past continues to shape the identity and vitality of the living city today. That's 4 stops in all.
