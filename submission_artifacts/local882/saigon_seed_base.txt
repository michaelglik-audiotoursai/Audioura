This is a step-by-step audio-guided walking tour of Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam.

Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, stained glass windows, and a peaceful atmosphere.

Orientation: This tour spans approximately 5 kilometers, leading you from the iconic Cathedral to the lively Thi Bar, as you traverse a city landscape where French colonial architecture intersects with modern life. Your first stop is Cathedral. From the central square, head southeast on Công xã Paris for about 5 minutes. You'll notice the beautiful colonial buildings lining the street. The Cathedral stands within the small Paris Commune Square, a roundabout framed by Lê Duẩn Boulevard and Nguyễn Du Street. The best viewing position is just across the square, where the full height of its twin bell towers rises clearly against the sky. The red brick facade emerges in sharp relief, its imported French construction materials catching the midday light. The square’s gentle hum of city traffic and the rustle of leaves from nearby trees blend with the faint echo of church bells, placing you amid the living rhythm of Ho Chi Minh City.

The Saigon Notre Dame Cathedral was completed in 1880, designed by architect Jules Bourard. Its twin bell towers soar above the surrounding streets, commanding attention and standing as a reminder of Saigon’s layered identity, where European design meets Vietnamese daily life. Before 1959, it was simply called the Saigon Cathedral. That year, a statue of Mary was installed in front of the building. This statue, known as Đức Bà Hòa Bình (Our Lady of Peace), led to the widespread use of the name Nhà thờ Đức Bà for the cathedral. The statue’s presence in the square changed the way locals and visitors referred to this imposing landmark, embedding it in the city’s collective memory as a place of peace and contemplation. Walking near the cathedral now, the faint metallic resonance of the bells blends with the scent of incense from nearby street shrines, linking the sacred interior with the vibrant life outside. The cathedral is not just a relic of colonial architecture; it remains a living center for many, resonating with the spiritual rhythms of the city.

Directions: Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 2: Post Office

Coordinates: 10.780000, 106.700000

Type/Specialty: Historic Post Office

Specific Examples: Impressive interior with vaulted ceilings, ornate wooden counters, and vintage postal artifacts.

Orientation: This stop unfolds as you step off the busy thoroughfare and face the façade of the Saigon Central Post Office. The broad, vaulted arches and the ornate ironwork overhead draw the eye upward, while the sound of voices and the faint rustle of paper mingle with the city’s pulse just outside.

The Saigon Central Post Office was completed between 1886 and 1891, designed under the direction of architects Auguste Henri Vildieu and Marie-Alfred Foulhoux. Its structural concept draws on plans by Gustave Eiffel from an earlier period, between 1860 and 1863. This building exemplifies an eclectic architectural style, blending Western engineering with subtle Eastern decorative motifs. The iron arches above you bear the mark of Eiffel’s influence, serving as a reminder that this was not merely a civic building but also a symbol of colonial ambition and modern communication. The air here carries the faint scent of ink and paper, a tactile link to the centuries of messages passed through these halls. Just beyond this square, the city’s waterways and bustling ports continue to shape the flow of goods and ideas, with this building standing as a steadfast witness to the city’s evolving story.

Directions: From the Post Office, walk east on Công xã Paris, then turn left onto Nguyễn Văn Bình. The street is filled with colorful book stalls and cozy reading spots. Continue to Book Street — it is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Book Street

Coordinates: 10.780573, 106.699695

Type/Specialty: Cultural Street

Specific Examples: A variety of bookstores, outdoor reading areas, and local authors showcasing their works.

Orientation: Book Street is a cultural street in Ho Chi Minh City. It is known for a variety of bookstores, outdoor reading areas, and local authors showcasing their works.

You are standing on Nguyen Van Binh Street, known as Book Street, a cultural hub in the center of Ho Chi Minh City filled with bookstores and outdoor reading areas. This pedestrian street is dedicated to books and reading, offering a wide variety of printed works on literature, politics, society, science, and culture. Nearby, urban heritage sites like Notre Dame Cathedral and the Ho Chi Minh City Post Office overlook the street, adding to its historic atmosphere. On 1 February 1968, during the Tet Offensive, a dramatic and tragic event unfolded on a Saigon street not far from here. South Vietnamese police chief Lt. Col. Nguyen Ngoc Loan executed a handcuffed Viet Cong prisoner named Nguyen Van Lem, an act captured by Associated Press photographer Eddie Adams. The photo of this execution shocked Americans and became one of the defining images of the Vietnam War, winning Adams the Pulitzer Prize and symbolizing the war's brutality. Today, as you explore Book Street, you walk through a city that has witnessed both cultural vibrancy and profound moments of history.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 600 meters, and you'll reach Independence Palace. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 4: Independence Palace

Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370

Type/Specialty: Historical Site

Specific Examples: The grand entrance, lush gardens, and preserved rooms from the Vietnam War era.

You are standing at the Independence Palace, a historic landmark in Ho Chi Minh City owned by the government. This building is also officially called the Reunification Convention Hall or simply Reunification Hall. The current palace was built in 1962 after President Ngô Đình Diệm ordered its construction to replace the old palace, which was bombed by dissident pilots. From October 1967 until April 21, 1975, the palace served as the home and office of President Thiệu before he fled the country. Inside, you can see the well-preserved 1960s retro-modern interior that reflects its historical period. Today, the Independence Palace remains open as a historic site for visitors like you.

Directions: Head northwest for about 500 meters, and you'll arrive at the War Remnants Museum. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 5: War Remnants Museum

Coordinates: 10.779500, 106.692000

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Powerful exhibits on the Vietnam War, military vehicles, and photographs documenting the conflict.

The building itself once housed the United States Information Agency, repurposed by the Ho Chi Minh City government to house this collection of war’s brutal evidence. Within these walls, the harsh realities of the First Indochina War and the Vietnam War unfold through photographs, military equipment, and personal artifacts. One haunting story emerges from the testimony of Colonel William Wilson, a combat veteran who investigated the My Lai massacre. The museum follows a tradition of exhibitions started during the earlier wars against the French and escalated during the American involvement. It was part of a deliberate effort to offer the Vietnamese perspective on these conflicts by exposing war crimes. The clatter of the city outside fades as you stand here, caught between past and present. The images and artifacts remain, stark and unyielding, as the city moves forward. Just beyond these walls, the arts await, offering a different kind of expression shaped by the echoes of what has passed. It was established in 1975.

Directions: Head east for roughly 1.2 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at the beautiful Opera House. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning French colonial architecture, lavish interiors, and a schedule of performances.

Orientation: Standing on Lam Sơn Square, with the Opera House’s warm cream walls and intricate façade directly ahead, the best perspective is from the broad plaza where you can take in the building’s full front. The ornate balconies and large arched windows frame the view, while the murmur of nearby traffic and chatter from the square blend into the atmosphere around this grand structure.

The Opera House opened in 1900 with a seating capacity of 559, offering a lavish venue for performances that drew the colonial elite to its plush interiors. This capacity, smaller than the venue noted in earlier sources, suggests adjustments were made over time to balance comfort and acoustics. The delicate plasterwork and wrought-iron balconies echo French design, reflecting the influence of French colonial artistry on the cityscape. Later, when the legislature became bicameral, the Opera House served as the home for both the Lower House (Hạ Nghị Viện) and the Senate (Thượng Nghị Viện). The building’s survival owes much to a restoration in 1995. Today, the Opera House stands as a reminder of how culture and power have intertwined in Saigon, where Vietnamese leadership found a home framed by these historic walls.

Directions: Head west for roughly 200 meters, and you'll arrive at City Hall. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Beautifully lit at night, elegant architecture, and a prominent statue in front.

Orientation: Standing on the wide sidewalk of Lý Tự Trọng Street, the building’s pale stone walls rise steadily before you, their smooth surface catching the shifting daylight. The air carries a blend of city sounds—honking scooters, distant chatter—and a faint scent of tropical blooms from nearby trees lining the street.

This building, now the Ho Chi Minh City People’s Committee headquarters, was constructed over a decade from 1898 to 1908 under French colonial rule. Designed to serve as the administrative center for the ruling government, it became a place where decisions were made that shaped Saigon’s transformation, embedding layers of governance into its walls. The long construction period reflects the colonial intent to imprint French authority physically and symbolically on the city’s core. Unlike the nearby Opera House, which served a cultural purpose, this building’s function was administrative—a place where the machinery of power turned.

Throughout the turbulent years of the Vietnam War and beyond, the City Hall remained the seat of various governments, its role extending far beyond architecture. Its dignified presence stands in contrast to the lively street life outside, where vendors and commuters weave around its perimeter. The building endures as a quiet witness to the city’s shifts from colonial past to present governance, marking it as a landmark of continuity amid Saigon’s evolving identity.

Directions: Head southeast for roughly 400 meters, and you’ll arrive at Café Apartments 42. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 8: Café Apartments 42

Coordinates: 10.774089, 106.704056

Type/Specialty: Coffee shop

Orientation: Café Apartments 42 is a coffee shop located at 42 Nguyễn Huệ in Ho Chi Minh City.

You stand before the Café Apartments at 42 Nguyễn Huệ, a nine-story building in the heart of District 1, Ho Chi Minh City. This building was once a residence but now hosts a lively complex of independent cafés and boutiques. The Café Apartments is located right on Nguyễn Huệ Walking Street, a popular pedestrian area. Its central location makes it easily accessible from various parts of the city. People also refer to this place simply as The Café Apartments or 42 Nguyễn Huệ.

Stop 9: Bạch Đằng Pier

Coordinates: 10.775240, 106.707002

Type/Specialty: Waterfront Area

Specific Examples: Scenic river views, boat tours, and a relaxing promenade.

Orientation: The pier stretches out beside a broad public park, where the Saigon River flows steadily to your left.

Bạch Đằng Pier was established at the beginning of the 19th century as one of the city’s earliest formal river quays. Its original purpose was to serve as a boat quay, anchoring the city’s river traffic when waterways were the main arteries of commerce and communication. Over time, the pier evolved within Ho Chi Minh City’s transport network, with a ferry link that facilitated movement between adjacent urban zones and underscored its ongoing function as a vital node in the expanding metropolis.

Today, Bạch Đằng Pier is also known as a departure point for tours heading to the Củ Chi Tunnels. These tunnels, located to the northwest of the city, formed a vital network during the Vietnam War and were used extensively by Viet Cong forces. The pier’s role in launching these tours connects it directly to the conflict’s legacy, transforming it from a simple transit point into a place of remembrance and historical inquiry. Visitors boarding here connect with the city’s complex wartime history through a physical journey into the tunnels’ subterranean passages.

The sounds of water slapping the pier’s supports and boat horns echoing across the river anchor Bạch Đằng Pier in its living role as a crossing point and meeting place. As night falls, the riverfront near Bạch Đằng Pier becomes a threshold to the vibrant heart of Saigon, where the city’s continual change is mirrored in the currents of the Saigon River itself.

Directions: Head southwest for roughly 1.7 kilometers, and you'll find yourself at Thi Bar. Enjoy the stroll!

Stop 10: Thi Bar

Coordinates: 10.767660, 106.693960

Type/Specialty: Gay bar

Orientation: Thi Bar is a gay bar located in downtown Ho Chi Minh City.

You are standing outside Thi Bar, located at 224 Đề Thám Street in the Phạm Ngũ Lão Ward of Ho Chi Minh City. Thi Bar has been open since 2010 and welcomes guests every day from 7pm until late, except on Tuesdays when it is closed. From 10pm each night, you can enjoy live music performed by local bands. This bar is known for its friendly staff and is one of the few genuine gay bars in the city. There is outdoor seating available, but food options are limited, so you might want to eat before you come or ask if you can bring outside food. Thi Bar is situated in the busy tourist area of Saigon, making it a popular spot for visitors looking for a vibrant atmosphere.
