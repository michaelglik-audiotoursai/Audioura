Stop 1: Cathedral

Coordinates: 10.779722, 106.699167

Type/Specialty: Historical Landmark

Specific Examples: Stunning neo-Romanesque architecture, stained glass windows, and a peaceful courtyard.

The Saigon Notre Dame Cathedral, completed in 1880 and designed by architect Jules Bourard, stands as a unique architectural landmark in Ho Chi Minh City. Its red brick walls, imported from Marseille, France, give the building a distinctive warm hue that sets it apart from the surrounding concrete and glass. The cathedral serves as the seat of the Saigon Archdiocese and draws visitors from around the world, standing as a reminder of the French colonial era’s impact on the city’s landscape. The arrival of the statue also led to the local adoption of the name “Notre Dame,” connecting the cathedral’s identity more closely with Catholic devotion to the Immaculate Conception. Here, the rhythm of daily prayer and ceremony meets the city’s relentless energy, reflecting the living pulse of Saigon’s traditions amid modern life.

Look up to see vaulted ceilings and arched windows that frame this historic space, where Western classical forms blend with Eastern decorative elements. Imagine the soft clang of telegraph equipment mixing with conversations in multiple languages, as this post office was once a bustling hub of communication. The scent of aged paper and ink still lingers here, a quiet reminder of the many messages sent and received within these walls. This place is more than a post office; it is a living symbol of history and connection in the heart of Saigon.

Stop 4: Independence Palace
Coordinates: 10.777050, 106.695370
Type/Specialty: Historical Site
Specific Examples: Grand halls, war memorabilia, and beautiful gardens.

The wide avenue stretches before you, framing the palace’s low, geometric silhouette. The gardens soften the edges with neatly trimmed hedges and tropical trees, their leaves barely rustling in the city's hum. From this vantage point, the palace’s role as a witness to Vietnam’s turbulent 20th century begins to unfold. The Independence Palace, completed on 31 October 1966 and designed by architect Ngô Viết Thụ, was conceived as the presidential residence and workplace of South Vietnam’s leader. Its solid concrete structure and austere lines reflect a Vietnamese modernist style, intended to combine functionality and authority. Yet beneath this calm exterior, the palace has been the stage for dramatic ruptures in the nation’s story. The immediate consequence was a hardened stance from Diệm, who grew hostile to American influence and tightened controls on the press and political freedoms, deepening the country’s divisions. More than a decade later, on 30 April 1975, the palace became the final symbol of a war ending. The sounds of engines and metal on stone still seem to echo faintly in the open space here.

Stop 6: Opera House

Coordinates: 10.776390, 106.702780

Type/Specialty: Cultural Venue

Specific Examples: Stunning façade, classical performances, and elegant interiors.

The Saigon Opera House opened its doors on January 1, 1900, as the Opéra de Saïgon. Its architectural style matches other colonial theaters in Hanoi and Haiphong, making it a rare surviving example of this period in Vietnam. Between 1956 and 1967, the Opera House was transformed from a venue for ballets and operas into a political arena. When the government shifted to a bicameral legislature, the building became home to both the Lower House and the Senate, where laws were debated and decided. Today, modern Vietnamese productions have reclaimed this elegant stage. The scent of polished wood and fresh paint now mingles with the faint traces of history embedded in the walls.

Stop 7: City Hall

Coordinates: 10.776470, 106.700970

Type/Specialty: Government Building

Specific Examples: Impressive architecture, illuminated at night, and a popular photo spot.

Orientation: Standing at the southern end of Nguyễn Huệ Street, your gaze meets the broad yellow facade of the Ho Chi Minh City Hall. The cool stone underfoot and the hum of the city envelop this spot, inviting a pause before the next landmark.

You stand before the grand yellow facade of Ho Chi Minh City Hall, officially the Ho Chi Minh City People's Committee Head Office, a striking French colonial building completed in 1909. Originally called Hôtel de Ville during the French colonial era, locals referred to it as Dinh xã Tây, or the French municipal palace. This building has witnessed the city's turbulent history, serving as an administrative center through colonial times and the Vietnam War. In front of the hall, a statue of Ho Chi Minh honors the revolutionary leader who founded the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945 and led the country until his death in 1969. Nearby, a commemorative stele marks August 25, 1945, the founding day of the Provisional Administrative Committee of Southern Vietnam, a key moment in the struggle for independence. Though you cannot enter, the building’s illuminated facade at night offers a stunning view of this symbol of Ho Chi Minh City's past and present.
