1. Stop 1: Harvard Square
Address: Harvard Square, Cambridge, MA 02138
Coordinates: 42.3744, -71.1190
Type/Specialty: Historic public square and cultural hub central to the novel's Cambridge setting
Specific Examples: The iconic Harvard Square T station where characters frequently meet, the bustling street performers and chess players that create the vibrant atmosphere described in the book, and the mix of bookstores and cafes where intellectual conversations unfold
Operational Details: Open 24/7 as a public space, street performers typically active 10:00 AM - 10:00 PM, nearby shops generally open 9:00 AM - 9:00 PM, free to explore, busiest during Harvard semester times
Walking Directions: From Harvard Square T station, head northeast on Massachusetts Avenue toward Porter Square. Walk for about 8 blocks (0.7 miles) passing through the residential area that serves as backdrop for many character interactions in the novel.

2. Stop 2: Porter Square
Address: Porter Square, Cambridge, MA 02140
Coordinates: 42.3884, -71.1190
Type/Specialty: Residential and commercial neighborhood featured as a key location in the characters' daily lives
Specific Examples: The Porter Square T station with its distinctive underground escalator system mentioned in the book, the diverse mix of restaurants and small businesses that reflect the multicultural Cambridge community, and the residential streets where characters live and work on their creative projects
Operational Details: Public square accessible 24/7, T station operates 5:00 AM - 12:30 AM weekdays, nearby businesses typically open 7:00 AM - 10:00 PM, free public access, moderate foot traffic throughout the day
Walking Directions: From Porter Square, head southeast on Massachusetts Avenue back toward Harvard, then turn right onto Brattle Street. Walk for about 6 blocks through the historic Brattle Street area, passing elegant colonial homes and tree-lined sidewalks.

3. Stop 3: Brattle Street (Tory Row)
Address: Brattle Street, Cambridge, MA 02138
Coordinates: 42.3736, -71.1270
Type/Specialty: Historic tree-lined street known as "Tory Row" representing the intellectual and cultural heritage of Cambridge
Specific Examples: The beautiful colonial mansions that housed Loyalist families during the Revolutionary War, the Longfellow House-Washington's Headquarters National Historic Site where the poet lived, and the tree-canopied walkways that provide a contemplative setting for the novel's more reflective moments
Operational Details: Public street accessible 24/7, Longfellow House tours available Wednesday-Sunday 9:30 AM - 5:00 PM, admission $5 for adults, self-guided walking free, quieter in early morning and evening hours
Walking Directions: This concludes your literary walking tour through Cambridge locations that inspired and appear in "Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow." The tour ends near Harvard Square for easy T station access.
