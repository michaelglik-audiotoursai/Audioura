In 1916-1917, L. Rosenberg envisioned a book titled "Au Soleil du Plafond," designed by the artist Juan Gris and complemented by the evocative poetry. Unfortunately, Gris could not see the project to completion, passing away in 1927. Reverdy, known for his surrealist and cubist poetry, collaborated closely with Gris during the planning stages, intending to combine their artistic visions. Thus, "Au Soleil du Plafond" advances the exhibition's narrative by exemplifying a revolutionary integration of image and text, altering the perception of what a book can achieve as an art form. With 11 lithographs printed by Mourlot Frères, the work embodies a pivotal moment in artistic collaboration, showcasing the potential and depth inherent in the livre d'artiste. Positioned within the broader exhibit, it joins other masterpieces that challenge deeply held beliefs and expand the boundaries of imagination. Art dealer Léonce Rosenberg originally initiated this collaborative project between painter Juan Gris and poet Pierre Reverdy during World War I, with sources dating its start to 1915, 1916, or 1917. The original plan paired one of Gris's plates with each of Reverdy's twenty poems, but work halted when Gris died of kidney failure in 1927 at age forty, leaving only eleven lithographs finished. Nearly thirty years after Gris's death, the Louis Broder Tériade revived the stalled undertaking alongside Reverdy.
