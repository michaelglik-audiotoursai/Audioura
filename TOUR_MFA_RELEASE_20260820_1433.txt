Orientation: To fully appreciate "Au Soleil du Plafond," position yourself so that the light from above gently illuminates the pages.

"Au Soleil du Plafond," a masterpiece of literary and visual synthesis, finds its genesis in the early 1950s when the Louis Broder Tériade resurrected a long-dormant project. This work, born from the creative partnership of artist Juan Gris and writer Pierre Reverdy, was initially conceived in the late 1910s but remained unfinished due to Gris's premature death in 1927. By 1955, Tériade successfully revived it, presenting 11 lithographs inspired by Gris's artistic vision, printed by Mourlot Frères, a renowned lithographic workshop in Paris. Visitors encounter lush, color-rich lithographs that pair Gris's Cubist-inspired imagery with Reverdy's poetic text. The lithographs, a product of meticulous collaboration, reflect the innovative spirit that defines the livre d'artiste — a form where image and text seamlessly coalesce. The pages, printed by Mourlot Frères, embody a tactile quality that speaks to the craftsmanship of its production. This work exemplifies the exhibition's thesis that artists like Gris revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative efforts. The fusion of literature and visual art showcases how light transforms perception, echoing ancient narratives that continue to shape our understanding.
