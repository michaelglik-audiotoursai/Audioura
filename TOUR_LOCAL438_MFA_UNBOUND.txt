Le Lézard aux plumes d’or, created by Joan Miró in 1971, is an illustrated book consisting of 40 vivid color lithographs. The publication was the brainchild of Louis Broder, a visionary publisher who was known for his dedication to the livre d'artiste — a type of art book where text and images are integrated into a single artistic expression. Broder's decision to engage Miró for this project was driven by his focus on limited editions that emphasized the artist's direct collaboration with the Mourlot Frères. The lithographs were meticulously printed by Mourlot Frères, a renowned printing house famous for its collaborations with artists like Picasso and Chagall.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3394, -71.0898

Orientation: The intimate space allows the layered collaboration to unfold before you, a synthesis of word and image.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí embarked on an ambitious project to illustrate Sigmund Freud’s *Moses and Monotheism*, infusing the psychoanalytic text with his unique surrealist vision. Dalí, known for his dreamlike imagery, transformed the philosophical work into a visual narrative on sheepskin—a nod to the historic gravitas of ancient texts. The decision to use sheepskin was not mere coincidence; it bridged the tangible past with the existential questions Freud posed. Meanwhile, Freud's authorship provided a profound substrate for Dalí to explore themes of spirituality and identity, woven into the surrealist aesthetic that pushes the boundaries of visual interpretation.By integrating Dalí's surrealism with Freud's psychoanalytic insights, the exhibition argues that such collaborations redefine the artist's book, creating immersive experiences of text and imagery. In naming the gallery after patron Torf, who champions showcasing rarely seen works, the exhibition gains an aura of rediscovery and reverence. Each page, each line, offers a glimpse into how celestial light—manifested in art—guides both spiritual and artistic pursuits, inviting a reflection on the continuum of human thought and creativity.

Orientation: This is not a mere collection of images but a deliberate, crafted dialogue between image, text, and the material form of the book itself.

Gris, a prominent figure in the Cubist movement, collaborated with Reverdy to produce this livre d'artiste, featuring 11 lithographs. Tériade, recognizing the innovative potential of combining Gris's bold Cubist compositions with Reverdy's poetry, provided a platform where art and text could exist in synergy. The integration of image and text in "Au Soleil du Plafond" exemplifies how the artists aimed to create a cohesive narrative experience, one that extends beyond the visual into the poetic. Reverdy’s presence was crucial, as his surreal and thoughtful poetry added a layer of depth to the visual imagery produced by Gris.
