This remarkable piece by Joan Miró, "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," is an illustrated book showcasing 40 color lithographs bound in publisher's vellum. Published and gifted to the Museum of Fine Arts by Boris Fridman, this artwork had no precedent and revolutionized the book as an art form. Miró's imaginative exploration of myth and the fantastical is evident in the whimsical interplay of golden feathers and a lizard, inviting viewers to ponder the theme of transformation in human beliefs and history. The collaboration between Miró, Louis Broder, and the printer, Mourlot Frères, resulted in a groundbreaking artistic endeavor that continues to inspire awe and wonder in viewers to this day.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Orientation: Position yourself at the center of the room at an arm's length from the exhibit titled "Moses and Monotheism" by Salvador Dalí. From this vantage point, you'll be able to fully appreciate the intricate details and symbolism embedded within the illustrations.

The exhibit of "Moses and Monotheism" showcases Salvador Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Sigmund Freud's book of the same name. The illustrations, rendered in Dalí's unmistakable surrealist style, depict key scenes and symbols from the story of Moses and the concept of monotheism. Dalí's choice to illustrate this work in a surrealistic manner adds layers of complexity to the narrative, inviting viewers to delve deeper into the psychoanalytic theories put forth by Freud. Within these illustrations, Dalí seamlessly merges images, text, and typography, creating a cohesive and thought-provoking visual narrative that challenges traditional artistic boundaries. Each illustration serves as a visual commentary on Freud's exploration of religious and historical themes, offering viewers a unique perspective on the origins of monotheism through the lens of surrealism. The collaboration between Salvador Dalí and Sigmund Freud in this exhibition exemplifies the convergence of art and psychoanalysis, inviting viewers to contemplate the evolution of religious thought and its artistic interpretation. The illustrations reveal deep psychological undertones and the interplay between image and text, challenging perceptions of traditional religious iconography through a surrealist lens.

In the broader context of the exhibition, which focuses on the book as the artwork itself, "Au Soleil du Plafond" stands out as a testament to the creative possibilities that emerge when artists push the boundaries of traditional mediums.
