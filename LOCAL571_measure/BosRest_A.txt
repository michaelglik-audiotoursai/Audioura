Your first stop is Union Oyster House. From the Haymarket T station, walk south on Blackstone Street, turn left onto Union Street, and you will find Union Oyster House on your right. You might notice the historic charm of the buildings around you. As you step onto Union Street, the rich, savory aroma of fresh oysters greets you, drawing you toward the storied entrance of the Union Oyster House at numbers 41–43.

Established in 1826, the Union Oyster House holds the title of America's oldest continuously operating restaurant. Nestled in a building originally constructed for Capen's Dry Goods in the early 1700s, this landmark retains an unmistakable sense of history. As you walk inside, imagine John F. Kennedy himself once sliding into his favorite booth here, which now bears his name in dedication. The Union Oyster House is not just a relic but a living part of Boston culture where history and gastronomy converge. The restaurant's patrons have included esteemed figures such as Calvin Coolidge, Franklin D. Roosevelt, Bill Clinton, and Barack Obama—each perhaps drawn by the promise of classic New England seafood served with a side of tradition. In fact, Barack Obama's visit in 2010 caused quite a stir; the sitting president enjoyed the clam chowder, a decision that resonates with locals proud of their regional cuisine. Today, the Union Oyster House is a cornerstone of Boston's Blackstone Block Historic District. Its legacy endures through a commitment to simple, authentic flavors that have stood the test of time. Open Sunday through Thursday from 11:00 am to 9:00 pm, and until 10:00 pm on Fridays and Saturdays, this iconic establishment invites you to savor classic dishes at prices under $30. Here, you're not only dining but also stepping into a narrative that continues to unfold, one shucked oyster at a time. And while you might not leave with a presidential seal of approval, you'll certainly find a taste of Boston's enduring seafood tradition. As you carry the flavor of this timeless experience with you, know that the next stop on our tour promises to take you further into the evolution of Boston's seafood delights.


Directions: As you leave Union Oyster House, walk north on Union Street until you reach Hanover Street. Turn right onto Hanover Street and stroll along until you spot Neptune Oyster on your left. Enjoy your walk through the historic streets of Boston on your way to Neptune Oyster for some delicious seafood!



Stop 2: Neptune Oyster

Address: 63 Salem Street, Boston, MA 02113

Coordinates: 42.3645, -71.0540

Type/Specialty: Seafood, Casual Dining

Specific Examples: Oysters on the half shell, lobster roll, clam chowder

Orientation: The restaurant's tight-knit, 37-seat interior mirrors the intimate oyster bars of Paris, a direct nod to its founders' inspiration.

Jeff, who spent twelve years as a beverage manager at Todd English's Olives, brought his expertise to this intimate spot, transforming it into a pioneering force in Boston's dining scene. The restaurant quickly gained a reputation for its dedication to high-quality seafood, particularly its iconic lobster rolls and fresh oysters. Such dishes have become staples in Boston's culinary landscape, with Neptune Oyster frequently cited as a must-visit for seafood enthusiasts. One notable story that highlights Neptune’s impact involves writer Korsha Wilson. In a personal narrative, Wilson shared that she visited Neptune Oyster during a challenging period when both she and her partner had lost their jobs. The comfort of the restaurant's warm, bustling atmosphere and its exquisite seafood provided a much-needed solace during those hard times. This story underscores Neptune's role beyond just a dining spot—it serves as a haven for those seeking comfort and connection through food. The restaurant's acclaim has only grown over the years. In 2022, Food & Wine magazine named Neptune Oyster one of America's Best Oyster Bars, acknowledging its significant contribution to the culinary arts. With its no-reservations policy, Neptune remains accessible to all who are willing to wait in line, eager to experience the flavors that have made it a beloved Boston institution. As you enjoy the fresh seafood, consider the stories—like Wilson's—that each dish might tell.


Directions: As you leave Neptune Oyster, head south on Salem Street. Continue walking until you reach the intersection with Cross Street, then turn left. Stroll along Cross Street until you spot the iconic Cheers Boston bar on your right – you've made it!



Stop 3: Cheers Boston

Orientation: As you stand on Beacon Street, look across to the iconic entrance of Cheers Boston, nestled in the charming Beacon Hill neighborhood. Feel the cool breeze from the Boston Public Garden, a stone's throw away, as you prepare to step into a piece of television history.

The story of Cheers Boston begins in 1969, when and a partner transformed a basement storage space in the Hampshire House into the Bull & Finch Pub. The Bull & Finch Pub officially embraced its pop culture legacy in 2002, renaming itself Cheers after a successful deal with CBS Studios, the show’s rights holder. Unlike the seafood feasts you enjoyed at Union Oyster House and Neptune Oyster earlier on this tour, Cheers Boston offers a hearty menu of American staples, best washed down with a cold beer or a carefully crafted cocktail. While you may not find Sam Malone pouring drinks behind the bar, the camaraderie and warmth that Cheers embodied on screen are palpable here. Cheers operates from 11:00 to 22:00 daily, with the kitchen closing at 21:00.
