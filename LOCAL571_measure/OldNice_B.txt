Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: walking tour of Old Nice, France
Tour-Category: walking

Stop 1: Cours Saleya Market

Address: Cours Saleya, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6955, 7.2656

Type/Specialty: Market

Specific Examples: Fresh produce, local flowers, artisanal products

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Old Nice, France. The route spans approximately 1 km from the bustling Cours Saleya Market to the historic Palais Lascaris. From the birth of the world's first wholesale cut flower market in 1897 to the founding of Fenocchio Ice-Cream Parlour in 1966, each corner holds a forgotten story waiting to be uncovered, offering a glimpse into the rich tapestry of Old Nice's past. Your first stop is Cours Saleya Market. From the Nice Ville train station, head south on Avenue Thiers towards Rue de France. Turn left onto Rue de France and continue straight until you reach Cours Saleya. You will be greeted by the vibrant colors and sounds of the market. The air fills with the mingled perfumes of fresh-cut lilies, spicy basil, and citrus as you step onto the broad stone promenade of Cours Saleya Market. The ochre facades and the sea breeze draw a line between the old city rising behind, and the Mediterranean light that falls over the stalls. Position yourself where the market opens onto Place Charles Félix, looking west down the avenue of flowers and produce.

The Cours Saleya Market in Nice is a bustling hub that has been at the center of the city's daily life for centuries. Located at the foot of the French Alps on the southeastern edge of the Mediterranean, this market has a rich history dating back to the 17th century when it first gained fame for its vibrant flower market. Over time, the market expanded to include a fruit and vegetable section, attracting growers from the hills who brought their colorful blooms to sell. In 1897, Nice established the world's first wholesale cut flower market here, solidifying the city's reputation for its vibrant colors and fragrances that still define the market today. The layout you see now is the result of a transformation in 1873, when the Cours Saleya Market was redesigned as the central hub for trade in Old Nice. Stalls spread out beneath the open sky, and the promenade that once hosted aristocratic gatherings became a democratic ground for local growers and townsfolk. Since the 1920s, this market has also been home to the ‘Thérésa’ stall, known for serving socca—a crisp, golden chickpea pancake distinctive to Nice. Each spatula scrape and each serving handed over the counter connects today’s visitors to nearly a century of everyday ritual. From 1921 to 1938, Henri Matisse lived at 1 Place Charles Félix, at the market’s eastern edge. The story goes that Matisse painted the view from his window, capturing the shifting patterns of striped awnings and the play of sunlight on the crowd below—a painter’s record of the city’s living rhythm, shaped by the market’s daily pageant. The Cours Saleya Market, recognized as part of Nice UNESCO World Heritage designation in 2021, stands as a living stage where the traditions —“Nice the Beautiful,” as Menica Rondelly named the city anthem—are renewed each morning. As you move westward through the stalls, the scents and sounds braid together, guiding toward the promise of a sweet treat at the next turn.

Directions: From Cours Saleya Market, walk south on Rue de la Poissonnerie until you reach Promenade des Anglais. Turn left and continue walking east along the promenade until you arrive at Fenocchio, a famous gelato shop known for its wide variety of flavors.

Stop 2: Fenocchio

Address: 2 Place Rossetti, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6954, 7.2634

Type/Specialty: Ice Cream Parlor

Specific Examples: Unique flavors like lavender, and rose, and classic favorites like chocolate and vanilla.

Orientation: Pause where the narrow lanes of old Nice spill into the square—the chatter of visitors and the distant bell of the cathedral tell you you have arrived.

The year is 1966. The world beyond these streets is in the midst of change—cities growing, traditions shifting—but here, in the center of Vieux Nice, the Fenocchio family opens their doors at Place Rossetti. This first shop begins with a promise: to offer not just ice cream, but flavors rooted in the land and memory of this Mediterranean city. The very air is heavy with sweetness—today mingled with the sharp herbal note of basil and the faint, powdery trace of violet. The story that locals recall is not of grand gestures, but of persistence: each day, the family turns out new batches, and by the decade’s end, both neighbors and travelers know Fenocchio by name. The evidence of time’s passage hangs in the list of offerings. Today, Fenocchio serves 94 flavors—59 ice creams and 35 sorbets—each one an archive of regional ingredients and forgotten recipes. Thyme, rosemary, cactus, tomato & basil, black olive, lavender, and rose: flavors that echo the Cours Saleya Market you stopped at a moment ago, but here preserved in the cold silk of cream and sugar. The sorbet of beer or olive might seem whimsical, but it is a record—each scoop a proof that this city’s palate runs deeper than the tourist’s sweet tooth. Step closer and catch the rhythm of a line that never seems to shorten. Generations have waited here, from children in Sunday best to the couples who now return with grandchildren. The second episode in Fenocchio’s story unfolds in 1988, when a second shop opens at 6 Rue de la Poissonnerie, still in the old town and near the market. This expansion is not just business—it is an answer to demand, a sign that the neighborhood’s flavor has become part of its identity. Here, the centuries pass slowly. What endures at Fenocchio is not novelty, but the steady layering of days—each flavor another page, each scoop another gesture in the long story of Nice. Beyond the square, the air stirs with music: your next stop, just around the corner, promises another echo from the past.

Directions: As you leave Fenocchio, walk down Rue de la Préfecture until you reach Place Rossetti. Continue straight on Rue Droite, passing charming cafes and boutiques. Palais Lascaris will be on your left, a grand historic building with a beautiful façade.

Stop 3: Palais Lascaris

Address: 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6961, 7.2761

Type/Specialty: Museum

Specific Examples: Baroque architecture, historic musical instruments, and beautiful courtyards.

Orientation: As you stand at 15 rue Droite, the ochre façade before you rises above the narrow stone street, its carved doorway set back in shadow. Overhead, pigeons shuffle along the ledges, their cooing filtered by the hush of the old town.

Step inside the Palais Lascaris, and the air thickens with the weight of centuries. This building was erected in the middle of the 17th century for the Lascaris-Vintimille family—whom Duke of Savoy, described as the “principalissima” of Nice noble families. The first to live here was Jean-Baptiste Lascaris, who began his residence as the palace was being constructed in 1648. The monumental staircase, still adorned with 18th-century frescoes, signals the ambition of its original owners. Each worn marble step and painted ceiling speaks of a family determined to project power in a city where prestige was measured in stone and pigment. The Lascaris-Vintimille line held ownership of the palace until the Revolution. In 1802, the palace was sold, and its walls and roof were left to weather decades of neglect. By 1942, the city of Nice purchased the crumbling building, finding much of its former grandeur faded. However, in 1946, its status as a Monument Historique was secured, saving it from further decay. Restoration efforts began in earnest in 1962, leading to the palace's reopening as a museum eight years later. In 2001, the city’s historic collection of musical instruments, totaling over 500, was moved here from the Musée Masséna, the city's historical musical instrument collection. The palace’s salons, once hosting private concerts and family life, now house France’s second most significant collection of instruments. The faint scent of beeswax from polished wood mixes with the quiet, the strings and brass at rest, awaiting a player. The palace offers a different richness after the flavors at Fenocchio, a layered silence heavy with the memory of music and power.
