Stop 1: Cours Saleya Market

Address: Cours Saleya, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6951, 7.2676

Type/Specialty: Market

Specific Examples: Fresh produce, local flowers, artisanal goods

Operational Details: Open Tuesday to Sunday from 10 AM to 6 PM. entry fee €10

Orientation: You are about to embark on a walking journey through Old Nice, France. The tour spans from Cours Saleya Market to Vieux Nice, covering a mere 696 meters of historic cobblestone streets and baroque facades. Old Nice, a tapestry of vibrant markets and narrow alleys, whispers forgotten tales of power and artistry. From the birth of Cours Saleya Market in 1861 to the UNESCO World Heritage designation in 2021, this journey weaves the visible vibrancy of the past with the invisible threads of community history, connecting the resilience of the present to its colorful past. At Cours Saleya Market, you'll discover its historical and cultural significance. In Vieux Nice, explore how the old town's alleys provided refuge during World War II. Your first stop is Cours Saleya Market. From the Nice Ville train station, head southeast on Avenue Thiers toward Rue de la Buffa, then turn left onto Rue de la Buffa. Continue straight to reach the Promenade des Anglais, then turn right and follow it until you reach Cours Saleya. You’ll start to hear the vibrant sounds of vendors setting up their stalls as you approach. Position yourself to best view this location.

In 1873, the modern layout of the market was established, transforming it into a pivotal hub for commerce in Old Nice. This shift marked a new era for the market, allowing it to flourish as a central gathering place where locals and visitors alike could experience the essence of Nice through its fresh produce and vibrant flower displays. The Cours Saleya Market is more than just a place of commerce; it is a reflection of Nice's cultural tapestry. Situated on the southeastern coast of France on the Mediterranean Sea, at the foot of the French Alps, Nice is the second-largest city on the Mediterranean coast and holds a unique position in the Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur region. This geographical advantage has allowed Nice to develop a distinct identity, celebrated in the nickname "Nice la Belle," meaning "Nice the Beautiful."

In 2021, the city of Nice, along with its heritage sites including the Cours Saleya Market, was designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This recognition underscores the market's historical and cultural significance, highlighting its role as a living pulse of the community where traditions and everyday rhythms converge. As you wander through the colorful stalls, you are, in essence, participating in a tradition that has been cherished for centuries. The market embodies the spirit of Nice, where the past and present blend seamlessly in a vibrant tableau of sights, sounds, and scents.

Directions: As you leave the bustling Cours Saleya Market, stroll down Rue de la Poissonnerie until you reach Place Pierre Gautier. From there, continue walking along Rue Droite until you arrive at the impressive Palais Lascaris on your right. Enjoy the charming architecture and history along the way!

Stop 2: Palais Lascaris

Address: 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6964, 7.2753

Type/Specialty: Historic Museum

Specific Examples: Baroque architecture, musical instrument collection, historic artifacts

Operational Details: Open Tuesday to Sunday from 10 AM to 6 PM. entry fee €10

Orientation: Position yourself to best view this location.

The Palais Lascaris, constructed in the mid-17th century, was a symbol of affluence and power for the Lascaris-Vintimille family, one of Nice's most distinguished noble lineages. Its architecture, with a monumental staircase adorned with frescos, whispers of the opulence that defined the era. This residence remained under the family's ownership until 1802, when it was sold following the French Revolution, a period that brought significant changes and turmoil to the region. The building fell into disrepair until the city of Nice acquired it in 1942 to preserve its historical significance. Listed as a Historical Monument in 1946, the Palais Lascaris underwent extensive restorations that concluded in 1970. These efforts transformed it into a museum, preserving not only the architecture but also the cultural legacy of this noble house. Today, the Palais is home to a remarkable collection of over 500 historical musical instruments, making it France's second most important collection after the Musée de la Musique de la Philharmonie in Paris. In 2001, these instruments were transferred from the Musée Masséna to find a fitting home within these hallowed walls. As you explore the palace, the scent of aged wood and the faint echoes of past melodies create an atmosphere of timeless elegance. The collection’s diversity, from string instruments to wind and percussion, reflects centuries of musical evolution and innovation. Each instrument, with its intricate craftsmanship, tells a story of artistic endeavor and the cultural exchanges that shaped European music. As you step back onto the cobblestones, the narrow lanes beckon you to explore the heart of Vieux Nice, where history and daily life entwine, revealing stories of a city that has embraced change, yet cherishes its roots.

Directions: As you leave Palais Lascaris, head south on Rue Droite towards the Old Town. Continue walking until you reach the vibrant streets of Vieux Nice with its colorful buildings and bustling markets. Enjoy the charming atmosphere as you stroll through the historic heart of Nice.

Stop 3: Vieux Nice

Address: Vieux Nice, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6959, 7.2750

Type/Specialty: Historic District

Specific Examples: Narrow winding streets, colorful buildings, local shops and cafés

Operational Details: Open Tuesday to Sunday from 10 AM to 6 PM. entry fee €10

Orientation: The sound of echoes of footsteps against centuries-old stones and notice the vibrant facades of the buildings, painted in hues of ochre, terracotta, and pastel.

Its origins date back to the 14th century, but its narrow streets hold stories that stretch far beyond. This district became the focal point of the city in 1543, when the Turks and Franks besieged the Chateau. The siege transformed the ville haute into a citadel, prompting the civilian population to relocate to the old town, where you now stand. During World War II, the old town’s maze-like alleys provided refuge to those living clandestinely, offering safety from occupying forces. The area is also associated with Joseph-François Desmorest, the official city executioner, who conducted historical tours of the alleys, connecting the past to human experiences.
