This collective voice of dissent highlighted the community's commitment to accountability and change, making the nave not just a place of worship, but also a symbol of courage and resilience. The altar, designed and crafted by local artisans Eric Boeglin and Deacon Jim, is more than just a focal point of worship; it embodies the commitment of a community dedicated to preserving the spiritual and artistic legacy of their church. This altar, with its meticulously carved features, was the subject of a dedication ceremony where its presence was blessed, symbolizing continuity and renewal. The church has witnessed many stories, some of which have drawn national attention. In late 2002, Rev. Walter H. Cuenin took a bold step by delivering a homily that criticized the Catholic Church's handling of abuse scandals. His words challenged Cardinal Bernard Law and resonated across the country, highlighting the altar as a place not just for liturgical rituals but also for moral reflection. Another chapter unfolded in September 2005 when Father Cuenin resigned under pressure from the Archdiocese following an audit that revealed financial irregularities. This event underscored the altar’s role as a silent observer of trials and transitions within the church community. The church's namesake, Mary Help of Christians, is a title steeped in history, first described by John Chrysostom in AD 345. This devotion, later propagated by figures like Don Bosco and Vincent Pallotti, is linked to the defense of Christian Europe during the Middle Ages, adding a layer of historical significance to the church and its altar. As you turn to leave, the altar's story weaves seamlessly into the tapestry of the church’s history, preparing you for the next chapter of this sacred journey.

Orientation: As you step into the narthex of Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church in Newton, Massachusetts, pause near the historical plaques that grace the walls.

The narthex, historically a place of welcome for those not yet entering the main worship space, has been the backdrop for deeply significant events in this community. In December 2002, more than 850 parishioners filled this very entryway. They came to support Father Walter H. Cuenin, who courageously criticized Cardinal Bernard Law during the Boston Archdiocese clergy abuse scandal. This gathering was more than a show of solidarity; it was a defining moment for the parish, highlighting the narthex as a space where voices are amplified, and community bonds are strengthened. Fast forward to late June 2023, and the narthex once again became a focal point for the community. This time, it was filled with media and parishioners coming together for vigils following the tragic murder of three longtime congregants: Gilda “Jill” D’Amore, her husband Bruno D’Amore, and Jill’s mother, Lucia Arpino. Authorities arrested Christopher Ferguson, a local resident with no known ties to the family, charging him with murder and burglary after matching his barefoot print at the scene. The entryway transformed into a living canvas of candles and quiet conversations, offering the community a sanctuary for mourning and reflection. These events underscore the narthex’s role not just as an entrance, but as a gathering place where the communal heart beats strongest. The narthex in this church mirrors the larger theme of our tour, connecting the sacred and communal aspects of parish life. Just as the altar you visited earlier represents a spiritual focal point, the narthex serves as a bridge, where everyday life and significant moments converge. Here, the rhythms of the community pulse through shared stories, drawing together past, present, and future under the watchful gaze of its historical plaques.
