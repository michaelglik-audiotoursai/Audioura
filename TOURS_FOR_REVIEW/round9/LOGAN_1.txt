Orientation: As you leave the Terminal A Main Concourse you visited earlier, head toward the gates where the jetbridge awaits. These enclosed walkways stretch out like mechanical limbs, connecting the terminal to the aircraft.

Jetbridges are more than just practical connectors; they're symbolic of the transitions and connections that define air travel. Consider the engineering marvel that allows passengers to board and disembark without facing the elements—a glimpse into the complexity behind what seems a simple passage. This innovation is a vital component of modern airports, reflecting the evolution of passenger convenience and safety. Boston Logan International Airport, where you stand now, carries layers of history. The airport owes its existence to the urging of the U.S. Army Air Corps and the Massachusetts Air National Guard, who sought a permanent landing strip near Boston Harbor. Over time, the shallow waters and several harbor islands, like Governors Island and Bird Island, were transformed through dynamite and landfill into the extensive runways you see today. In 1943, a pivotal moment occurred when state legislators decided to name the airport after General Edward Lawrence Logan, commemorating local veterans and service members. This decision weaves a thread of honor and remembrance into the airport's identity, grounding it in the region's military heritage. Next time you traverse a jetbridge, think of it as more than just a passageway. It's part of an intricate choreography of arrivals and departures, each step echoing the remarkable history and engineering that make such transitions possible.

Orientation: As you stand here, look up and notice the imposing figure of Boston Logan International Airport's Control Tower. Its distinctive silhouette rises 285 feet into the sky, casting a long shadow over the bustling network of terminals and runways below, including the Jetbridge you paused at earlier.

Designed by the Boston architectural firms Kubitz & Papi, Inc. and Desmond & Lord, Inc., this tower is a marvel of brutalist architecture, an homage to both function and form. Its twin elliptical concrete pylons create a structure unlike any other in the U.S. These pylons are joined by a six-story trussed sky-bridge, which seems to hang in mid-air directly beneath the control cab where air traffic controllers guide over 400,000 flights annually through Boston's busy airspace. This architectural feat combines engineering innovation with a nautical nod, as the silhouette resembles a ship's mast and sails, a fitting tribute to Boston's maritime history. Trippe, the founder and later Pan American World Airways, helped connect Boston to New York, marking the city as a pivotal node in the burgeoning world of commercial aviation. Within this tower, the invisible choreography of arrivals and departures unfolds, a dynamic counterpart to the static structures of Terminal A's Main Concourse that you observed moments ago. Here, amidst the hum of technology and the quiet urgency of communication, the controllers ensure that millions of passengers reach their destinations safely. The architectural beacon plays a crucial role in shaping the journeys of countless travelers navigating through Boston Logan International Airport, which saw a record 43.5 million passengers in 2024.


Directions: As you leave the Control Tower, walk towards the airport terminals. Head in the direction of Terminal A and continue past the ticketing area. Follow the signs for Baggage Claim in Terminal A, and you'll soon arrive at your destination.
