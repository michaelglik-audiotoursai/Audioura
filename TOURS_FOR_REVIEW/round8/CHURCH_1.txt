Father Michael M. Green laid the groundwork for this church in the 1870s, in a village teeming with Irish immigrants. These windows were a product of sacrifice and community spirit, with laborers, domestic servants, and factory workers from Newton Corner and Nonantum contributing weeks of wages for their creation. Yet, the windows were more than a mere financial undertaking; they were a declaration of identity and faith amidst the prevailing Yankee Nativism of the time. The church was designed by James Murphy, a student of the notable architect Patrick Keely, as a counterstatement to cultural exclusion. It embraced Marian themes and traditional patron saints in its stained glass, echoing the church's dedication under Our Lady Help of Christians. The imagery in the windows reflects this dedication, with cycles that pay homage to Mary and other revered figures. This design choice was not arbitrary; it was a profound expression of defiance and unity, capturing the community's resilience through art.
