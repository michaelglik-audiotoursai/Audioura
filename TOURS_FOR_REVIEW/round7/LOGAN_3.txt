Orientation: As you step into the concourse at Boston Logan International Airport, take a moment to appreciate the bustle of travelers from all corners of the globe.

The concourse at Boston Logan is steeped in a rich history, having first opened as the East Boston Airport on September 8, 1923. Its establishment was a joint effort by the U.S. Army and the Commonwealth of Massachusetts, initially serving as a military and airmail airstrip. This venture transformed tidal flats and marshland into a vital hub for defense and communication along the Atlantic coast. The Commonwealth of Massachusetts re-assumed direct control in 1941, financing extensive landfill projects into Boston Harbor through state bond issues. This expansion not only solidified Boston Logan's role as a central player in aviation but also marked a significant shift in the airport's infrastructure and capacity. Among its many stories, the airport's concourse has witnessed events that shaped modern air travel security standards. This episode, deeply etched in the annals of aviation history, underscores the concourse's role as a stage for both innovation and somber reflection. As you continue your journey through Boston Logan, consider how these stories of transformation and resilience form the backdrop to every traveler’s personal journey, making each passage through the concourse a unique and layered experience.

Orientation: As you move from the concourse you visited earlier, pause at the jetbridge. This enclosed passageway forms a crucial link between the terminal and airplane, a threshold where the rhythms of travel converge.

The jetbridge at Boston Logan International Airport stands as a modern nexus of connection. Originally an airfield on reclaimed tidal flats at Jeffries Point, the site transitioned from military to commercial use, driven by the Boston Chamber of Commerce Committee on Aviation. This evolution reflects Logan's role in the broader narrative of air travel, where Massachusetts' National Guard 101st Observation Squadron once operated, now giving way to the bustling flow of international passengers. On February 17, 1959, operational control of the airport passed to the Massachusetts Port Authority (Massport), marking a pivotal moment in its development. This transition enabled Logan to expand and modernize, adapting to the demands of increasing global travel. Today, the jetbridge symbolizes the seamless journey from one world to another, sheltering passengers from New England's unpredictable weather as they embark on flights to distant lands. The atmosphere hums with anticipation — the faint scent of jet fuel, the murmur of announcements, and the soft thud of luggage wheels on the floor.
