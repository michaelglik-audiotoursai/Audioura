Orientation: As you step into the Upper Church, let your gaze ascend to the vaulted ceilings that reach heavenward. The intricate Gothic Revival detailing, a testament to the craftsmanship of the late 19th century, tells a story of devotion and perseverance.

This Upper Church, nestled within the parish of Our Lady Help of Christians in Newton, Massachusetts, bears the marks of both triumph and challenge. In the late 1800s, as Irish immigrants laid its foundation, they did so amidst a backdrop of social tension and anti-Catholic sentiment from local Protestant elites. The construction of this church was not merely a physical endeavor but a profound assertion of faith and community identity. The echoes of those struggles resonate through the hallowed space, a reminder of the community's enduring resilience. Amidst its storied walls, the church has borne witness to moments of profound consequence. In September 2005, an archdiocesan audit uncovered financial irregularities, leading to the resignation of Rev. Walter H. Cuenin, a figure central to the parish's narrative. This event marked a significant turning point, reflecting the complexities of faith and leadership within the church. The reverberations of this episode linger, reminding us of the human dimensions that accompany spiritual pursuits. More recently, in June 2023, the church became a beacon of solace in the wake of tragedy. The murder of longtime parishioners Gilda (Jill) D’Amore and Bruno D’Amore in a home invasion cast a shadow over the community. On June 25, 2023, seventy-four-year-old Bruno D’Amore was killed alongside his wife, Gilda D’Amore, and his mother-in-law, Lucia Arpino, after suffering blunt-force trauma and stab wounds during a break-in at their home on Broadway Street in Newton, Massachusetts. In response, a 'Mass of Peace' was held, uniting the parish in a collective embrace of grief and hope. This gathering underscored the church's role as a sanctuary for healing and reflection. Its history is etched in every corner, a living tapestry of faith woven through the decades. As you stand in this sacred space, consider the stories it has borne witness to, and the ones yet to unfold.

Stop 3: Stained Glass Windows

Address: 573 Washington St, Newton, MA 02458

Coordinates: 42.3525, -71.2071

Type/Specialty: Artistic Feature

Specific Examples: Vivid depictions of biblical scenes, intricate leadwork, colorful light patterns

Orientation: As you stand before the stained glass windows of Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church, look up and notice the intricate play of light and color.

The late 19th century marked a unique phase for Our Lady Help of Christians Parish. Due to structural limitations imposed by architect James Murphy's design, decorators employed a trompe-l'œil technique, painting simulated stained glass windows directly onto surfaces. This method, which means "deceive the eye" in French, was a creative response to the architectural constraints, showcasing the ingenuity and adaptability of the artisans of that era. Fast forward to the tumultuous year of 1938 when the New England Hurricane ravaged many parts of the region. The church wasn’t spared from nature's fury, and two stained-glass windows, depicting St. Louis and St. Anne, were destroyed. However, the central window portraying the Crucifixion remarkably survived the storm's wrath. This window stands as a symbol of endurance, representing the church's ability to withstand both literal and metaphorical storms through its history. Another significant moment etched into the fabric of these windows occurred decades later. In June 1995, Saint Mother Teresa of Calcutta graced Our Lady Help of Christians Parish with her presence. Addressing a packed assembly beneath these very windows, her visit left an indelible mark on the parish's history, drawing a profound connection between the sacred art and the community it serves. These windows are more than mere decorations; they are silent witnesses to the church's past, illustrating tales of creativity, survival, and spiritual significance.

Directions: As you leave the beautiful stained glass windows behind you, walk straight towards the altar area. Continue past the pews and head towards the center aisle. The pulpit is located near the front of the church, on the right side as you face the altar. You can't miss it with its intricately carved design.
