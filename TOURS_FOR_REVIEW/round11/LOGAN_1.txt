Stop 3: Jetbridge

Address: 1 Harborside Dr, Boston, MA 02128

Coordinates: 42.3654, -71.0245

Type/Specialty: Airport Boarding Area

Specific Examples: Boarding gates, waiting lounges, departure screens

Operational Details: Boarding typically starts 30 minutes before departure

Orientation: As you stand here, enveloped by the muted clamor of the airport, focus your attention on the jetbridge stretching out before you. This is your passageway to the skies, a bridge between the familiar ground and the great unknown above.

The jetbridge marks a pivotal threshold in your journey, echoing the adventures of legendary aviators like Charles Lindbergh and Amelia Earhart. Lindbergh, known for his transatlantic flight aboard the Spirit of St. Louis in 1927, landed at this very airfield during a celebrated tour. Similarly, Amelia Earhart, famed for her solo flight across the Atlantic in 1932, also graced these grounds, her presence a beacon for future generations of aviators. The jetbridge is not just a historical waypoint. It stands as a silent witness to the tragic morning of September 11, 2001, when five al-Qaeda hijackers boarded American Airlines Flight 11 and United Airlines Flight 175, both departing from Logan. These flights, bound for destiny, would alter the course of history as they were flown into the North and South Towers of the World Trade Center in New York City. This stop connects back to the security checkpoint you passed moments ago, a reminder of the precise choreography needed for safe travel. It mirrors the larger orchestration of international travel, reminiscent of JFK Airport in New York. JFK, the busiest in the New York airport system, covers 5,200 acres and hosts nearly 100 airlines. Logan and JFK serve as gateways in a global network, where each step onto a plane from a jetbridge is a leap into a world of intersecting paths and destinations. As you step onto the plane, appreciate how the airport orchestrates this symphony of departure.


Directions: As you exit the Jetbridge, head towards Terminal C. Walk past the airport shops and restaurants, then continue on foot towards the Control Tower. You'll see the tall tower ahead of you - just keep walking in that direction until you reach it.



Stop 4: Control Tower

Address: 1 Harborside Dr, Boston, MA 02128

Coordinates: 42.3661, -71.0245

Type/Specialty: Airport Control Tower

Specific Examples: Air traffic controllers, radar equipment, communication systems

Operational Details: Operates 24/7 to manage air traffic movements

Orientation: Here at the Control Tower of Boston Logan International Airport, you're standing at the nerve center of one of the busiest airports in the United States. From this vantage point, the view stretches across a landscape that was once a series of natural harbor islands, now transformed into an expansive aviation hub.

This transformation began with a joint initiative between the Massachusetts General Court and the United States Army, setting the stage for decades of growth. In the mid-20th century, the airport expanded by leveling and incorporating islands like Governors Island, Bird Island, and Apple Island into the airport's footprint. This ambitious undertaking laid the foundation for the airport's current expanse of 2,384 acres, accommodating six runways and four passenger terminals. Inside the Control Tower, air traffic controllers orchestrate the intricate ballet of flights, ensuring the seamless passage of millions of passengers. Although you've already experienced the bustle of Terminal A Ticketing and the meticulous process at the Security Checkpoint, it's here that the unseen maestros of the skies operate. With precision and calm, they manage the airport's operations as a large hub primary commercial service facility, as categorized by the Federal Aviation Administration. The architectural heritage of Logan Airport is also noteworthy. Kallmann McKinnell & Knowles, renowned for their Brutalist designs, contributed to this legacy with the Central Parking Garage. Meanwhile, the spiritual needs of travelers were addressed by Maginnis, Walsh and Kennedy, an esteemed ecclesiastical architectural firm known for the Our Lady of the Airways Chapel, dedicated in 1952. As you reflect on the complex orchestration of air travel, the Control Tower stands as a reminder of the network of dedicated professionals ensuring safe journeys from Boston to destinations around the globe.
