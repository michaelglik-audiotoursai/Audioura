In 1705, the commanding presence of the Citadel of Nice was reduced to ruins by the order of Louis XIV. The Sun King, asserting his dominance after capturing the fortification, left the site barren for more than a hundred years. What stands today is a testament not only to power but also to the relentless passage of time, with the remnants of defensive walls offering silent witness to a bygone era. In the 1860s, a different type of tradition took root here, one that continues to resonate through the city. He financed the firing of a cannon at noon every day, a practice that has evolved into a daily pyrotechnic display. This quirky historical nugget connects the hill to the everyday rhythms of Nice, merging past customs with present-day life. The hill also played a clandestine role during the tumult of World War II. The elevator shaft that now transports visitors was once a secretive arms cache and air-raid shelter. This piece of history, hidden in plain sight, reveals yet another layer of the hill's multifaceted narrative. Castle Hill stands as a living memory of the city's defiance, love, and survival, with each story adding depth to its enduring presence. Whether it's the echo of a cannon or the quiet rustle of the trees, Castle Hill speaks volumes about the resilience and character of Nice. Orientation: The vibrant red façades around you speak to the square's evolution from a riverbed to a bustling focal point of Nice's social life.

In the year 1956, a striking piece of art was unveiled here: crowned by the statue of Apollo sculpted by Alfred Janniot. The statue's nudity sparked outrage among locals, prompting modifications to its design. It was then removed and placed behind the Charles-Ehrmann stadium, only to find its rightful place back in the square by 2011, embodying resilience and the enduring spirit of Place Masséna. The square's history is not just one of art and aesthetics. It was here, in July 1976, that Albert Spaggiari orchestrated what became known as the 'Heist of the Century'. Spaggiari and his accomplices brazenly tunneled into the Société Générale bank vault from underneath the square, executing their audacious plan with a quiet precision that defied belief. Their actions left an indelible mark on the annals of criminal exploits, a reminder of the square's capacity for high drama beyond its serene surface. Place Masséna also showcases the cultural vibrancy of Nice by hosting the city's Carnival celebrations, which once featured plaster fights. Orientation: As you stand before the Colline du Château Waterfall, take a moment to appreciate the cascade of water that spills down moss-covered rocks.

In 1885, amidst a significant public health crisis in Nice, the creation of this waterfall marked an innovative solution. The city faced severe sanitation issues, and the Canal de la Vésubie was extended to Castle Hill to serve as an overflow relief basin. This engineering feat transformed the landscape, turning a practical necessity into an aesthetic triumph. The waterfall became a symbol of resilience and human ingenuity, blending functionality with beauty. The history of Castle Hill is steeped in dramatic events. The decision came after a French bomb struck the fortress's central powder magazine, leading to a devastating explosion. This act forever altered the landscape of Castle Hill, erasing its military might and paving the way for its rebirth as a public space. During World War II, the strategic importance of Castle Hill resurfaced. Italian and Nazi German forces drilled fortified tunnels and gun emplacements through its limestone core. These remnants, though not visible here, remind us of the turbulent history that lies beneath the tranquil surface of the park. The waterfall, with its gentle cascade, contrasts sharply with the hidden stories of conflict that Castle Hill has witnessed. As you leave the calming presence of the waterfall, remember the bustling Place Masséna you visited earlier. The narrow streets of Nice's ancient quarter await, promising more tales of transformation and endurance. Here, history and serenity coexist, offering a unique glimpse into the layered narrative of this remarkable city.

Address: Vieux Nice, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6968, 7.2724

Type/Specialty: Historic district

Specific Examples: Narrow cobblestone streets, colorful buildings, local markets

Orientation: As you step into Vieux Nice, the old town with its narrow streets and vibrant markets, imagine the noonday cannon firing, echoing through the alleys as it has since the 1860s.

In the labyrinth of Vieux Nice, history pulses through its cobblestones and pastel-painted facades. This neighborhood is more than a charming enclave; it has been at the heart of dramatic tales that defined its character. Picture Sir Thomas Coventry-More in the 1860s, orchestrating a daily ritual that echoes even today. To ensure his wife came home for lunch, he arranged for a cannon to be fired at noon. This peculiar custom, once a summons for family obligations, continues as a beloved tradition, now signaling midday in a uniquely Niçois fashion. Walking further, stories of resilience and drama unfold, such as the daring escape of Albert Spaggiari from the Old Nice courthouse on March 10, 1977. Having orchestrated what many call the "Heist of the Century" the previous year, Spaggiari found himself facing the consequences. In a scene that could rival any thriller, he leapt from the second-story courtroom window, evading capture on a waiting motorcycle. His exploits have become part of the tapestry of tales that Vieux Nice holds, each one adding to its rich narrative. Vieux Nice is not just about past escapades. It is a living reminder of bygone customs and societal quirks that persist. The bustling markets, where the scent of Provence’s herbs mingles with the sea breeze, paint a picture of everyday life as it intertwines with history. As you stand here, amidst the chatter of vendors and the rhythm of the cobblestones underfoot, the past isn’t just remembered; it resonates through the vibrant present.
