Orientation: As you ascend to the air traffic control tower cab at Boston Logan International Airport, the bustling activities of the apron and tarmac you visited earlier seem to shrink away. Here, high above the airport, is where the real choreography of flight takes place.

The air traffic control tower at Logan Airport is more than just a navigational hub; it is a feat of engineering and design. Completed in 1973, it was the tallest airport control tower in the world at that time, standing at 285 feet. This dual-pylon, elliptical concrete structure illustrates the bold architectural spirit of its era. Designed by John Carl Warnecke, known for creating President John F. Kennedy's Eternal Flame memorial, the tower is a striking example of Brutalist architecture. These forms were not just about aesthetics; they were about function and resilience, handling the complexities of international customs and wide-body jet processing. Inside the cab, air traffic controllers use state-of-the-art radar and communication systems to guide planes safely through one of the nation's busiest airspaces. This place is the nerve center, orchestrating the departures and arrivals that keep the airport alive. Yet, it’s not just the technology that defines this space; it’s also the human element. The controllers’ expertise and calm under pressure ensure that every flight is a safe journey. By taking over airport operations in 1959, Massport has overseen Logan's transformation into a modern hub, integrating sustainable design practices, such as those seen in the LEED-certified HOK terminal.
