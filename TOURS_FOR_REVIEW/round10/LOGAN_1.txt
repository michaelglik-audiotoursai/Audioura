At the bustling hub of Boston Logan International Airport, the Security Checkpoint is where anticipation fills the air. This checkpoint holds a heavy place in contemporary history. On September 11, 2001, ten hijackers passed through here to board American Flight 11 and United Flight 175, marking a turn in global security protocols. The repercussions of that day transformed travel security worldwide, leading to profound changes in airport operations and the introduction of stringent measures to ensure passenger safety. Beyond its historical significance, the checkpoint is part of a facility that has been integral to Boston’s growth as a major transportation hub. This connection to the broader globe is encapsulated by Terminal E, designed initially by Minoru Yamasaki and recently modernized by AECOM & luis vidal + architects. Here, modernity meets memory, forever shaping the journeys of those who pass through.

Stop 2: Concourse

Address: 1 Harborside Dr, Boston, MA 02128

Coordinates: 42.366444, -71.018444

Type/Specialty: Airport Concourse

Specific Examples: Gates, shops, restaurants

Operational Details: Various operating hours based on flight schedules

Orientation: As you step beyond the hustle of the Security Checkpoint, the Concourse at Boston Logan International Airport opens up like a vast avenue.

The Concourse at Boston Logan is more than just a passageway connecting travelers to their destinations. It's a living testament to the airport's rich history. On May 12, 1922, the Massachusetts General Court passed legislation authorizing the construction of an aircraft landing field. This pivotal moment came to life on September 8, 1923, with the opening of the Jeffries Point Glider/East Boston Airport, known today as Boston Logan International Airport. This was a strategic move, championed fervently by Governor Channing H. Cox and Boston's civic leaders who were keen to position the city as a leader in aviation, rather than let it fall behind other burgeoning East Coast hubs. The concourse isn't just a physical space—it's a bridge to the past, echoing the determination of Lieutenant General Edward Lawrence Logan, whose leadership and legacy as a South Boston military figure are deeply woven into the airport's story. Stand here and you can almost sense the bustling energy of an era when early patrons and aviation pioneers laid the groundwork for what would become a gateway to the world. With the skyline as your backdrop, it's easy to imagine the transformation from those early military hangars to the modern-day marvel you stand in. Here, history whispers through every corner, tying together past and present in a symphony of movement. As you make your way toward the gates, consider the legacy and ambition that continues to guide every journey from this iconic concourse.


Directions: As you exit the concourse, head towards the main terminal building. Once inside, continue walking towards the gates until you reach the jetbridge for your departing flight. Look for signs directing you to your gate along the way.

Type/Specialty: Airport Jetbridge

Specific Examples: Connecting passengers to airplanes, boarding area

Operational Details: Used during boarding and deplaning processes

Orientation: As you walk through the jetbridge here at Boston Logan International Airport, pause for a moment and notice the seamless connection between the terminal and the aircraft. The passageway, with its utilitarian design, might seem mundane, but it is a critical threshold in the world of aviation.

The jetbridge is where passengers officially transition from the concourse you visited earlier to the aircraft, shifting from grounded travelers to airborne adventurers. This structure plays a pivotal role in the choreography of air travel, enabling the smooth flow of thousands of passengers each day. The sounds of rolling luggage echo through the enclosed space, mingling with the distant hum of the airport's operations. This particular jetbridge holds a unique place in aviation history, having served as the gateway for historic arrivals and departures. Among those was Charles Lindbergh, known for his pioneering solo transatlantic flight in 1927. His journey marked a new era in aviation, opening the skies to possibilities previously unimagined. Architecturally, Boston Logan International Airport was influenced by the vision of Kallmann McKinnell & Knowles, who designed Terminal B, introducing innovative features like split-level drop-offs and modular ticketing pods. These design choices revolutionized airport layouts, emphasizing efficiency and passenger convenience. Massport's strategic management since 1959 ensured that Logan's expansions were funded through revenue bonds and fees, rather than public funds. These strategic decisions helped transform Logan into the bustling hub it is today. As you stand here, consider the jetbridge not just as a mere connector, but as a symbol of transition and possibility. It represents a portal to new experiences, uniting people and destinations across the globe.


Directions: As you exit the Jetbridge, head towards the main terminal building. Walk past the baggage claim area and continue towards the airport entrance. The Control Tower will be visible ahead of you, slightly to the left as you stroll towards it.



Stop 4: Control Tower

Address: 1 Harborside Dr, Boston, MA 02128

Coordinates: 42.367123, -71.019012

Type/Specialty: Airport Control Tower

Specific Examples: Air traffic controllers, radar equipment

Operational Details: Operational 24/7 for air traffic control

Orientation: As you step into view of the Control Tower at Boston Logan International Airport, look skyward. Perched 285 feet in the air, it is a striking presence against the cityscape, with its two massive cylindrical pylons flanking an elevated sky-bridge control cab.

The Control Tower, completed in 1973, is the brainchild of John Carl Warnecke, an architect known for notable buildings, & Associates in collaboration with Desmond & Lord. These design choices are evident in the tower's imposing structure, which overlooks the submerged Governors Island and the airport's busy runways. The tower is more than just an architectural marvel; it is the nerve center of the airport. Here, air traffic controllers orchestrate the complex ballet of departures and arrivals, ensuring that the 43.5 million passengers who passed through the airport in 2024 reach their destinations safely. The story of the airport itself is steeped in history. Named after General Edward Lawrence Logan, a local military hero, the airport was officially given its current name by the Massachusetts legislature in 1943. It serves as a critical hub within the Federal Aviation Administration's of Integrated Airport Systems, categorized as a large hub primary commercial service facility. Moreover, Logan Airport is not just a transit point; it is a community. Our Lady, a church serving the local community, of the Airways church, dedicated in 1951 by Boston Archbishop Richard Cardinal Cushing, serves the spiritual needs of airport workers and travelers alike, grounding the bustling facility in a sense of place and tradition. As you stand here, the hum of aircraft engines and the distant echoes of the terminal underscore the tower's vital role.
