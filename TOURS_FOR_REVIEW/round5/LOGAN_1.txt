The story of Boston Logan International Airport begins in the aftermath of World War I, when Boston's lack of a municipal airfield became apparent. This void led to the passage of Chapter 404 of the Acts of 1922, enacted by the Massachusetts General Court and signed by Governor Channing H. Cox, officially establishing the airport. Overseen by Duke, the original airfield, known as Boston Airport, was laid out on a 189-acre tidal flat with cinder runways, opening on September 8, 1923. Today, the airport has expanded significantly, now covering 2,384 acres and serving over 43.5 million passengers in 2024. The transformation from a humble airfield to a bustling international hub underscores the relentless march of progress and the vital role Boston Logan plays in connecting the city to the world.

Orientation: As you stand in Terminal A at Boston Logan International Airport, let your eyes wander upward to the elegantly sweeping arcs of the ceiling. This architectural design, a hallmark of the renowned firm HOK, sets the stage for a space that is both functional and innovative.

Terminal A is not merely a hub for travelers; it represents a pioneering moment in architectural history. Opened in 2005, it earned the distinction of being the world's first airport terminal to achieve LEED certification for new construction from the U.S. Green Building Council in 2006. This accolade recognized its dedication to sustainability, an ethos embedded in every corner of its design. From the energy-efficient lighting to the use of recycled materials, the terminal embodies an eco-conscious future, reflecting a growing global awareness of environmental responsibility. The terminal's design plays a silent yet crucial role in the traveler’s experience. As you navigate through its corridors, the natural light pouring through large windows reduces the need for artificial lighting and provides a calming ambiance amid the bustling activity. The efficient layout and open spaces are not merely aesthetic choices; they are intentional decisions aimed at reducing environmental impact while accommodating the needs of millions of passengers. Its creation marked a turning point in how airports could function — not just as transit points but as examples of sustainable innovation. The accomplishment of attaining LEED certification means that Terminal A stands as a model for other airports worldwide, showcasing how design can be both beautiful and responsible. Orientation: As you stand on this jetbridge at Boston Logan International Airport, look through the side windows to observe the sweeping panorama of Boston Harbor.

A place where passengers eagerly anticipate new destinations, the jetbridge is more than a conveyance. Its importance roots back to the military endeavors that first inspired the airport's creation. In 1923, the Massachusetts National Guard needed an operational base for their newly formed 101st Observation Squadron of the 26th Division Air Service. With strategic foresight, the United States Army Air Service constructed the initial airfield next to Boston Harbor by ingeniously reclaiming marshland with silt and cinder as fill material — a pioneering practice then, but a template for later expansions. The funding, a combined effort with $35,000 from the legislature and an added $10,000 generously contributed by the Boston Chamber of Commerce and private donors, not only transformed the flatlands into functional runways but also demonstrated community investment in advancing aviation technology and infrastructure. Over the years, this initial groundwork allowed Boston Logan International Airport to grow into a central hub, connecting events, people, and nations on its runways. Today, the jetbridge represents a unique convergence where history and progress coexist. As you connect from the familiar terminal — Terminal A, which you visited not long ago — to the possibilities that lie beyond, remember the foundational steps that transformed these reclaimed tidal flats into a bustling gateway to the world.
