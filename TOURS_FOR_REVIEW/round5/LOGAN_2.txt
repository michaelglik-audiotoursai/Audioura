Stop 3: Jetbridge

Address: Boston Logan International Airport, 1 Harborside Dr, Boston, MA 02128

Coordinates: 42.3644, -71.0177

Type/Specialty: Airport Jetbridge

Specific Examples: Bridge connecting terminal to aircraft, boarding point

Operational Details: Accessible only during boarding times

Orientation: As you stand here at the Jetbridge, notice the seamless transition from the bustling Terminal A Main Concourse you passed through to the aircraft waiting beyond.

The Jetbridge at Boston Logan International Airport serves as more than just a physical link between the terminal and the aircraft; it is a poignant reminder of the airport's historical significance. On September 11, 2001, Logan Airport was the departure point for Flights 11 and 175, both tragically hijacked that day. Among those onboard were Betty Ann Ong, a flight attendant who became one of the first to alert authorities, and Christine Hanson, the youngest victim of the attacks. Their stories are a solemn testament to the profound impact of that day, highlighting the importance of vigilance and security in modern air travel. Another chapter in Logan's history dates back to May 12, 1922, when Massachusetts Governor Channing Cox signed Chapter 404 into law. This legislation authorized the construction of an aircraft landing field at Jeffries Point, laying the groundwork for what would become Logan Airport. It marked a pivotal moment in aviation history, transforming East Boston into a hub of air travel and shaping the future of the region's connectivity. Just as Logan Airport is integral to Boston, JFK Airport is the linchpin of New York's air travel network. Located in Queens, JFK is the largest in the New York metropolitan area, serving nearly 100 airlines with flights to destinations across all six inhabited continents. This network of airports underscores the interconnectedness of modern travel, where each jetbridge serves as a gateway to the world, uniting cities and cultures in a web of global exchange.


Directions: As you exit the Jetbridge, walk towards the main terminal building. Continue on foot past the parking garage towards Terminal C. The Control Tower will be visible as you stroll towards the terminal entrance.

Orientation: As you step away from the jetbridge you visited earlier and gaze upward, the Control Tower at Boston Logan International Airport rises before you, a striking testament to the evolution of aviation. Located in East Boston, this tower stands amidst the sprawling complexity of Logan's 2,384 acres, where six runways and four terminals orchestrate the movement of over 43.5 million passengers annually.

The Control Tower isn't just an architectural focal point; it's the nerve center of one of the United States' largest airports. This place was once merely a muddy expanse in East Boston, transformed into a bustling aviation hub through a series of meticulously planned expansions. In the early days, the 101st Observation Squadron of the Massachusetts National Guard, led by Lt. Col. William A. Bishop, played an instrumental role in advocating for a permanent base here. Their efforts paved the way for the airport's military significance, setting the stage for its eventual transformation into a major commercial hub. Today, the Control Tower facilitates seamless coordination between flights, ensuring the safety and efficiency of air travel. The tower's design reflects decades of architectural and technological advancements. It harmonizes with the airport's role as a large hub primary commercial service facility, as classified by the FAA. Over the years, Logan has developed into a global gateway, with non-stop service connecting Boston to destinations across the United States and the world. Standing here, you can almost sense the pulse of this ever-changing community, where aviation history and modern innovation continue to shape its narrative.
