The altar at Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church serves as the spiritual heart of this space, a focal point where the community gathers in worship and reflection. This particular altar was crafted by local artisans Eric Boeglin and Deacon Jim, who combined their talents to create a piece that is both functional and deeply symbolic. The altar's design incorporates elements that reflect the church's broader mission and community ties, drawing inspiration from the local culture and the rich history of the Catholic faith. In 2002, Our Lady Help of Christians found itself at the center of significant events in the Boston Archdiocese. On December 8th of that year, Father Walter H. This bold act of speaking truth to power resonated deeply with the congregation, leading to standing ovations from over 850 parishioners. Another notable chapter in this altar's history occurred in September 2005, when the archdiocese demanded Father Cuenin's resignation over unauthorized expenses, which the parish’s lay finance council had approved. This incident highlighted the complexities of church governance and financial oversight, reinforcing the altar's position as a witness to the church’s evolving narrative. The altar's dedication in the early 20th century also reflects the cultural tapestry of Newton, as southern Italian immigrants from San Donato Val di Comino brought their devotion to the statue of Maria SS. di Pugliano here.
