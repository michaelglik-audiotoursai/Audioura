Let me share a story that roots this bustling modernity in its fascinating beginnings. Back in 1922, Boston faced a pivotal moment. Mayor James Michael Curley and Governor Channing H. Cox recognized a looming threat: the rapidly advancing aviation industry might bypass their beloved city without a municipal airfield. They persuaded the Massachusetts General Court to authorize the construction of an airfield on state-owned tidal fill in East Boston. Their foresight set the stage for the airport you see today, ensuring that Boston would not be left behind in the aviation age. Fast forward to 1928, the City of Boston took over the lease and management of the airfield from the military. Here's where it gets interesting: the city floated bonds to fund runway construction and civilian facilities, transforming a military outpost into a bustling municipal airport. This transition was crucial, aligning Boston with the new era of civilian air travel.

The story of Boston Logan International Airport begins with a crucial step in March 1922 when Boston Mayor James Michael Curley petitioned Massachusetts Governor Channing H. Cox for legislation to create an airfield in East Boston. This decisive move led to the official opening of Boston Airport on September 8, 1923, known early on as Jeffrey Field. This foundational moment was not just about creating an airfield but about positioning Boston as a key player in the burgeoning aviation sector.
