Stop 2: Au Soleil du Plafond by Juan Gris

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3406, -71.0879

Orientation: The best vantage point for "Au Soleil du Plafond" is directly in front of the display case.

In 1916, Léonce Rosenberg commissioned the Spanish artist Juan Gris to create illustrations for Pierre Reverdy's text "Au Soleil du Plafond." Gris, a prominent figure in the Cubist movement, took on the project with his distinct artistic vision, despite the challenges of the time, including the turmoil of World War I. Unfortunately, Gris passed away in 1927, having completed only half of the intended work.Pierre Reverdy later saw the project brought to life in a Parisian edition, ensuring Gris’s illustrations were paired with his words, thus preserving their shared vision. Tériade Éditeur, known for its commitment to publishing artistically significant books, realized the publication with Mourlot Frères printing the lithographs. This collaboration between Reverdy and Gris exemplifies the exhibition's argument that the livre d'artiste is a dynamic form of artistic expression, where image and text become inseparable.

In 1938, Salvador Dalí met Sigmund Freud in London and sketched his portrait. Years later, Dalí created illustrations for this very book, producing a set of drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin. This set of ten illustrations by Dalí, executed on sheepskin, is a tactile reminder of the collaborative spirit that defined the era's artistic ventures. The choice of sheepskin as a medium is more than an aesthetic preference; it echoes the ancient traditions of religious texts, bringing Freud's provocative ideas into a timeless context. The Hogarth Press, known for its literary contributions, published Freud's text, further cementing the collaborative nature of this work. This piece advances the exhibition's argument by demonstrating how Dalí and Freud, like many others, merged images, words, and typography into a unified artistic statement.
