Stand directly in front of "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers)", and you'll see how the interplay of text and image leaps off the pages. From this vantage point, Miró’s whimsical lines and vivid forms captivate, merging with poetry in a dance of color and meaning.

Joan Miró's "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" is an exquisite illustration of the power of collaboration in the art of book-making. The project was the brainchild of Louis Broder, a publisher renowned for his dedication to the livre d'artiste — a form that integrates literature and visual art into a single artistic endeavor. Broder's decision to engage Miró was crucial, as it exemplified the unique synergy between artist and publisher that the exhibition argues revolutionized the book as an art form. The printing was handled by Mourlot Frères, a celebrated atelier known for their expertise in lithography, ensuring that every line and hue was impeccably rendered. At its heart, Miró’s work reimagines nature with a surreal brilliance. The lithographs within this book do not merely illustrate; they interact with the text to challenge and expand the reader's perception of reality. The golden-hued lizard, a mythical creature, entwines itself among Miró’s iconic playful shapes and bold strokes, evoking a world where the boundaries between the real and the fantastical blur. Boris Fridman, a benefactor with a keen eye for the distinctive fusion of text and visual art, gifted this remarkable book to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. Fridman's donation enriches the museum’s collection, offering visitors insight into how artists like Miró transformed the printed book into a canvas of artistic exploration. The piece not only stands as a pivotal example of Miró's work but also illustrates the transformative potential of artistic alliances, prompting one to ponder how art can materialize intangible beliefs into forms that resonate on many levels.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Position yourself directly in front of the "Moses and Monotheism" exhibit to fully appreciate the intricate interplay of image and text that Salvador Dalí and Sigmund Freud have crafted. From this vantage point, you can observe how Dalí's illustrations dance around Freud's provocative prose, creating a dynamic dialogue between visual and written language.

Freud, the father of psychoanalysis, authored this provocative text, exploring the origins of religious belief and suggesting that Moses might have been Egyptian rather than Hebrew. Published by The Hogarth Press, this work exemplifies the collaborative spirit of the exhibition, where artists like Dalí worked intimately with authors to create a unified artistic and literary statement. The book's form, with Dalí's illustrations skillfully integrated with Freud's text, challenges traditional boundaries of art and literature, showcasing how visual art can encapsulate and expand upon complex ideas of identity and faith.

Stop 3: Au Soleil du Plafond

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: "Au Soleil du Plafond" is a striking work created in 1955 through the collaboration of Spanish artist Juan Gris and French poet Pierre Reverdy. This livre d'artiste, or artist's book, embodies the merging of visual art and poetry in a seamless union that elevates the book form itself.

The Louise Leiris Gallery in Paris was pivotal in the development of this innovative work, which was published by Tériade, a key advocate of the livre d'artiste. The integration of illustrations and text in this piece offers a unique experience, blending poems and lithographs to engage viewers visually and verbally. Within the expansive galleries of the MFA, Boston, this work prompts reflection on the collaborative nature evident throughout the "Picasso, Miró, Dalí: Unbound" exhibition. Through vibrant compositions, Gris' cubist innovations are vividly portrayed, transitioning his groundbreaking ideas from canvas to print. Reverdy's introspective and abstract poetry complements Gris' visual style, establishing connections that go beyond mere illustration. This work exemplifies the central theme of the exhibition: artists like Gris elevated the book to an art form by collaborating closely with poets like Reverdy, resulting in a creation that communicates on multiple levels. "Au Soleil du Plafond" showcases how such collaborations merge individual contributions into a cohesive aesthetic encounter. Similarly, like "Moses and Monotheism" elsewhere in the exhibition, this piece sparks a dialogue between text and image about perception and reality.
