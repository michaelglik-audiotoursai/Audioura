Your first stop is Tabitha Soren | An Artist Life. As you enter the Griffin Museum of Photography, the aptly named exhibition "Tabitha Soren | An Artist Life" invites you to explore the transition from journalism to fine art. Stand back a moment to take in the breadth of the exhibit, which reveals the evolution of Soren’s work from documentation to art.

Tabitha Soren’s career took a transformative turn in 1997 when she completed a fellowship at Stanford University, a private research university in California. During this period, Soren made the pivotal decision to leave her television journalism career behind and venture into fine art photography. The culmination of this project was realized when published it as a monograph in 2017, demonstrating how her work was no longer bound to the immediacy of news but instead embraced the depth of storytelling inherent in photography. The collection at the Griffin Museum offers a glimpse into the lives of athletes who aspired to fame and success. The photographs capture not just the pinnacle moments of their careers, but also the quieter, more telling instances of their everyday lives—moments that often go unnoticed in the fast-paced world of professional sports. This is where Soren’s gift as an artist truly shines. The images act as a window into the world of ambition, vulnerability, and the unpredictable nature of human endeavor.

Orientation: Stand near the center of the Main Gallery to appreciate the unique installation style of "TLC." From this vantage point, the method of display—using magnets and large vinyl—creates an inviting, informal atmosphere reminiscent of flipping through a family album.

The Griffin Museum of Photography's "TLC" exhibition, short for "Tender Photographers and Their People," serves as a poignant stop in the thematic series "State of Our Union," examining the United States' 250-year history. Scheduled from September 12 through October 25, 2026, the exhibition was curated by Dr. Delphine Sims, Assistant Curator at the San Francisco Museum of Modern Art. Sims crafted this exhibition to deviate from traditional art displays, employing an unconventional setup with magnets and vinyl to evoke the casual nature of family photo albums. This choice underscores her desire to connect the audience with the enduring yet transient nature of memory. In displaying works by contemporary artists Alanna Airitam, Harlan Bozeman, Charles H. Lee, and Star Montana, Sims assembled a diverse array of photographic narratives. Each artist navigates the fragile interplay between memory and identity, shedding light on personal and communal histories. By featuring these photographers, Sims not only challenges viewers to reflect on historical and cultural narratives but also complements the Griffin Museum's broader initiative to explore the past with an innovative lens. In crafting "TLC," the Griffin Museum welcomes visitors to ponder the memories that shape both individual identity and collective consciousness. As you leave the gallery, consider how these photographic stories resonate with Tabitha Soren's "An Artist Life," which you experienced earlier on this journey through the museum.

Address: 67 Shore Road, Winchester, MA 01890

Orientation: As you stand in front of "Lua Kobayashi | The Persistence of Memories," observe the seamless integration of traditional and digital art forms. This vantage point allows you to fully appreciate the immersive video projection and multimedia installation that define the exhibit.

2025, Lua Kobayashi embarked on a thoughtful journey through the greater Boston area, engaging intimately with Japanese-American families in their living spaces. This experience inspired her to create "The Persistence of Memories," which became the centerpiece of her exhibition at the Griffin Museum of Photography. The work highlights how memories persist through both personal narratives and collective experiences, blending tactile and digital mediums. This approach emphasizes the evolving nature of photography in contemporary art, offering fresh insight into our collective consciousness.

Orientation: Stand near the center of the gallery to fully appreciate BU Masters Show 2026 | Traces: Pursuing Process. From this vantage, you can see the intricate details and diverse textures of the photographic works.

Arthur Griffin, an influential American photographer known for his innovative techniques, inspired this exhibition. Harper reflected Griffin's pioneering spirit by choosing to capture the raw impact of tidal movements in sensitive environments, fostering conversations about sustainability. Pfannenstiel coordinated site-specific installations that merge visual storytelling with local narratives. This exhibition, therefore, not only celebrates Griffin’s legacy but also deepens the museum’s commitment to environmental consciousness through photography.

Orientation: Position yourself to face the sparse digital kiosk in the gallery's corner.

The Griffin Museum of Photography's decision to host "Intertidal: Field Notes" as an online exhibition marked a pivotal shift from physical displays to a virtual gallery. This innovative approach allowed the works of various artists to reach a global audience, offering a new level of accessibility and engagement. Arthur Griffin, known for his captivating coastal landscapes and seascapes, laid the groundwork for such endeavors by capturing the transient beauty of nature. Photography as an art form has long been associated with the ability to capture fleeting moments, a concept that resonates through this digital exhibition. This platform provides an inclusive space where artists and viewers, regardless of location, can engage with the collection in meaningful ways.
