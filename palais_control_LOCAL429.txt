Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: Palais Lascaris, Nice, France - Museum Tour
Tour-Category: museum

Stop 1: Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle)

Address: Palais Lascaris, 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6972, 7.2751

Orientation: Stand about three feet from the panel titled "Raquel (panneau, fin du XVIe siècle)" at Palais Lascaris, where the light hits the surface directly. This angle reveals the intricate details crafted in this late 16th-century work.

Raquel, a panel from the late 16th century, captivates with its depiction of the biblical story of Rachel, one of the matriarchs in the Book of Genesis. The panel showcases exquisite wood carving, with layers depicting Rachel's tale of love and motherhood. The artist's choice of walnut wood allows for fine detailing, lending depth to Rachel's expression. The work's historical context ties closely to the cultural focus on biblical figures during the Renaissance, a period marked by a revival of classical themes and religious narratives. This piece fits within the Palais Lascaris's collection, highlighting the region's transition from medieval to modern artistic influences.

Directions: Continue through Palais Lascaris — next is Basse de violon by Paolo Antonio Testore (Milan, 1696).

Stop 2: Basse de violon by Paolo Antonio Testore (Milan, 1696)

Address: Palais Lascaris

Coordinates: 43.6972, 7.2751

Orientation: Stand slightly to the left of the Basse de violon by Paolo Antonio Testore (Milan, 1696) to appreciate the graceful curve of its body, revealing the craftsmanship of this late 17th-century Italian maker.

The Basse de violon by Paolo Antonio Testore, created in Milan in 1696, represents an era when Italian luthiers were renowned for their exquisite string instruments. This bass violin, crafted from fine wood, highlights Testore's attention to detail, particularly in its sculpted scroll and elegant F-holes, which allow for rich acoustic resonance. During this period, Milan was a hub for musical innovation, with the likes of Testore contributing to the evolving landscape of baroque music. Positioned within esteemed collection, this piece complements other significant instruments like violas da gamba and violas d'amore, emphasizing the museum's dedication to preserving diverse musical heritage. This historical context enriches our understanding of Testore's work as part of a broader narrative of musical craftsmanship and innovation.

Directions: Next: Guitare baroque by Giovanni Tesler (Ancona, 1618).

Stop 3: Guitare baroque by Giovanni Tesler (Ancona, 1618)

Address: Palais Lascaris

Coordinates: 43.6972, 7.2751

Orientation: Stand directly in front of the display of the Guitare baroque by Giovanni Tesler (Ancona, 1618). This position allows you to appreciate the intricate craftsmanship and decorative elements that are unique to baroque instruments, highlighting their historical and artistic significance.

The Guitare baroque by Giovanni Tesler is a captivating example of early 17th-century musical craftsmanship. Crafted in Ancona in 1618, this instrument showcases the ornate design typical of the baroque period, characterized by elaborate decorations and attention to aesthetic detail. The guitar features intricate woodwork and ornate inlays, reflecting the period’s decorative style that often included floral and geometric motifs. Such designs were not merely ornamental; they conveyed the wealth and status of the owner. Baroque instruments like this guitar are key components of collection, illustrating the rich tradition of musical creation during this time. The guitar’s presence in this collection underscores its value in illustrating the era’s artisanal ingenuity and cultural richness.

Directions: Your final stop in Palais Lascaris: Guitare baroque by Jean Christophle (Avignon, 1645).

Stop 4: Guitare baroque by Jean Christophle (Avignon, 1645)

Address: Palais Lascaris

Coordinates: 43.6972, 7.2751

Orientation: Stand to the right side of the "Guitare baroque by Jean Christophle (Avignon, 1645)," where the carved rosette on its soundboard becomes clearly visible. This intricate detail showcases the craftsmanship that defined baroque instrument making, setting it apart in this music gallery.

The "Guitare baroque" by Jean Christophle, crafted in 1645, exemplifies the baroque era's artistic flair, characterized by ornate design and decadent materials. This guitar, originating from Avignon, features a finely carved soundboard rosette, a common feature in baroque guitars that not only provided decorative flourish but also played a role in sound projection. Instruments during this period served as status symbols, reflecting the wealth and cultural sophistication of their owners. This piece is a significant part of the Palais Lascaris collection, which includes various rare instruments, such as a tenor sackbut offering insights into the evolution of musical instrument craftsmanship.
