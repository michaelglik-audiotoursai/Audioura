The Chapelle de la Sainte Croix, the oldest building in the village is dating back to 1306, which stands as a testament to the enduring history of this place.

The area surrounding Èze was first settled around 200 BC near Mount Bastide, with the bay of Èze mentioned in the Antonine Itinerary, a historical document that dates back to ancient times. By 1388, Èze came under the control of the House of Savoy, who transformed the town into a fortified stronghold due to its strategic location near Nice. In a significant event in 1543, French and Ottoman forces led by Hayreddin Barbarossa captured the village, and in 1706, Louis XIV ordered the destruction of the castle and walls during the War of the Spanish Succession. The village of Èze was formally integrated into France in April 1860 through a unanimous vote by its residents. Walt Disney himself visited Èze Village in 1956 and dined at the Château de la Chèvre d'Or, thanks to a transformation suggested by him to hotelier Robert Wolf. Today, the château serves as a hotel, blending history with modern luxury. The sound of church bells echoes through the village, evoking a sense of bygone eras.
