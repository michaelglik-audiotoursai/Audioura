From this vantage point, you will be able to fully appreciate the vibrant colors and intricate details of this whimsical piece.

Nestled among the other artworks in the exhibition, "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" is a captivating illustrated book by Joan Miró, published in 1971. This unique piece features 40 color lithographs, including the wrapper front and cover, bound in publisher’s vellum. Miró's collaboration with Louis Broder and the precision of Mourlot Frères, the printer, resulted in a work that had no precedent and revolutionized the book as an art form. As you observe this masterpiece, you will enter a world where kinetic art intersects with surrealism. Miró's illustrations feature bold colors and whimsical shapes that encourage exploration of the fusion of image, text, and typography. The lithographs reflect the artist's playful yet profound exploration of form and movement. Miró's choice of medium and technique in this illustrated book not only showcases his artistic prowess but also reflects the cultural context of the time. The vibrant hues and dynamic compositions in "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" challenge traditional notions of storytelling and redefine the boundaries of art and literature. This work, gifted to the museum by Boris Fridman, serves as a testament to Miró's innovative spirit and enduring influence in the world of visual arts. As you continue to explore the exhibition, consider how this whimsical transformation connects to ancient beliefs and modern interpretations of art and creativity.
