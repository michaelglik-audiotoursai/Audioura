Coordinates: 42.3095, -71.1043

Orientation: As you stand on South Street, listen for the gentle whirr of an espresso grinder through the open door, and catch the scent of warm butter and caramelized sugar drifting out to the sidewalk. The sign for Fiore’s Bakery hangs above a single wide window, letting in sunlight that slips across a display of golden pastries. This is your spot: pause here, just inside the doorway, where you can see the bakers at work behind the counter.

Step inside Fiore’s and the first thing that greets you is the sound of steady conversation in several languages at the small tables, and the distinct aroma of fresh, yeasted dough. Those roots show in the laminated doughs and the traditional Turkish simit — a circular bread encrusted with sesame seeds that appears here on weekends, drawing a line out the door by midmorning. Local tradition holds that in 2010, during the winter blackout that left much of Jamaica Plain without power, Fiore’s remained open thanks to a small generator. Staff handed out coffee and slices of apple galette to neighbors who crowded in for warmth.

The bakery’s approach to coffee is slow and deliberate. The Turkish coffee pot, or cezve, sits in the open, its long handle catching the light beside stacks of tiny porcelain cups. On certain afternoons, Oya — one half of the couple now at the helm — prepares the coffee over a small sand heater, echoing a ritual from her childhood in Izmir. The aroma of cardamom drifts out as she pours, and customers are drawn to the counter, watching as the dark, thick brew settles. Here, the connection to the tour’s theme is in the way Fiore’s bridges technique and community: the old-world methods, the neighborhood stories, and the gentle pace of a bakery where patience is baked into every loaf. The taste of the simit, still warm, and the bitterness of strong coffee serve as a reminder of what endures — even as the neighborhood changes around it.
