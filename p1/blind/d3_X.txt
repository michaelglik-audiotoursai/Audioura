Step into Casa Muine—Sea & Food, where the daily rhythm of Mui Ne’s fishing heritage merges with the pulse of a family kitchen. The terrace hums with low conversation and laughter, overhead fans stirring the humid air, and the distant clatter of crab shells echoing the everyday rituals of a working shoreline. Casa Muine offers a unique dining experience shaped by the shore itself: the day’s catch arrives straight from the boats, and the menu is dictated by what the tide and local vendors bring in each dawn. Seafood is the mainstay here, and among the must-try offerings is the swimming crab—cracked, steamed, and served with a side of garlic butter or local salt and lime.

The kitchen is a family affair, with three generations working side by side: the grandmother overseeing pots of simmering broth, her son manning the grill, and grandchildren ferrying baskets of herbs to the prep table. Their hands have inherited recipes refined over decades, but each meal carries a subtle modern twist—chili oil made in-house, a squeeze of calamansi instead of lemon. This blend of tradition and innovation is what draws both locals and travelers, day after day. The convivial rhythm here is different from the bustle you experienced at Bờ kè Mr. Crab 112, where the energy was pure market.
