Stop 2: Sons of Liberty Bowl

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3376, -71.0942

Orientation: Stand just a few feet back from the Sons of Liberty Bowl and let your gaze capture the intricate craftsmanship of this silver masterpiece. Here, the details of Paul Revere's silversmithing come alive, revealing both artistry and a profound historical statement.

In 1768, fifteen members of the Sons of Liberty, a group of Massachusetts rebels, commissioned Paul Revere, the silversmith and midnight rider of 1768, Jr. to create this silver bowl. In 1949, the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston acquired the Sons of Liberty Bowl with the help of seven hundred small donations, securing its place as a true icon of their collection. Crafted from silver, a medium chosen for its resilience and reflective beauty, the bowl honors ninety-two members of Representatives who refused to rescind a letter opposing the Townshend Acts. These Acts imposed taxes on essential goods like tea and paper, fueling revolutionary sentiment. The bowl's surface glimmers with the names and symbols that echo the defiance of an emerging nation. The acquisition of this bowl highlights the profound role of community involvement in art preservation. Seven hundred donations from Boston schoolchildren and the public funded its purchase, demonstrating a collective commitment to maintaining cultural heritage. This commitment to art preservation continues in contemporary works, ensuring that the museum remains a vibrant testament to cultural endurance.

Stop 3: John Singer Sargent’s  The Daughters of Edward Darley Boit

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3376, -71.0942

Orientation: As you stand before John Singer Sargent’s *The Daughters of Edward Darley Boit*, you're in a spot where the painting fully reveals its emotional depth and spatial dynamics. By taking a step back, you can see how Sargent uses the vastness of the room to emphasize the solitude and introspective mirroring within the figures themselves.

In 1882, John Singer Sargent painted The Daughters of Edward Darley Boit, capturing the Boit sisters in a moment of their childhood. This work now resides in the Art of the Americas Wing, named for the Americas due to its dedication to showcasing the cultural and historical richness of the continent. Sargent defied traditional portrait norms with this piece. Unlike the formal compositions typical of that era, he scattered the sisters across the canvas, nestled in a shadowy room where they seem both part of their surroundings and detached. The focus on the negative space and the detailed depiction of the interior of the Boit family’s Parisian home highlights the artist's revolutionary approach to portraiture. This technique leaves room for the viewer to contemplate the emotional and psychological landscape of the subjects, offering a window into the evolving narrative of modern introspection. The painting is also part of a broader narrative within the museum, connecting to other works acquired through diverse acts of patronage and philanthropy, like the Sons of Liberty Bowl, which you encountered earlier. This acquisition, aided by donors and patrons like the Boit sisters, showcases the museum's mission to honor the complexities and breadth of American history through art.
