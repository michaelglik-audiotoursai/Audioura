Orientation: Each cup you enjoy here reflects the resilience and renewal of a community that continues to evolve amid changing urban landscapes. From the Forest Hills T station, walk along Washington St toward Centre St, then turn right onto Centre St; Jamaica Plain Coffee will be on your left. You might notice the vibrant murals adorning the nearby buildings. Position yourself to best view this location. Jamaica Plain Coffee offers a glimpse into the community’s vibrant tapestry. In 2022, just around the corner at City Feed and Supply, a significant labor movement unfolded. The workers formed City Feed Unite, demanding higher wages, healthcare, and COVID safety protections after their management cut pandemic hazard pay. David Warner and Kristine Cortese, the owners, refused to recognize the union voluntarily, which led to public protests and filings with the National Labor Relations Board (NLRB). This episode highlights the neighborhood's tradition of advocacy and resilience, where community voices have long fought for fairness and equity. A year later, in 2023, another chapter unfolded nearby at 182 Hyde Park Avenue with the opening of Public Coffee Initially welcomed as a new addition to the local coffee scene, it was soon discovered to be affiliated with a plant for the Wesleyan Church. The church's conservative views on homosexuality and gender non-conformity provoked a strong backlash from the community, leading to protests and a boycott. These episodes paint a picture of Jamaica Plain as a community deeply engaged in social issues, where coffee shops are not just places to enjoy a brew but also arenas for activism and change. As you sip your coffee here, you become part of a narrative that extends beyond the cup—one where every aroma and every corner holds a piece of the community’s ongoing story.
Directions: Keep an eye out for the colorful murals as you walk, and continue until you reach the intersection with Centre Street. Ula Cafe will be just a short walk ahead on your left, right next to the charming little shops along the way. Enjoy the vibe of the neighborhood as you go!

Stop 2: Ula Cafe

Address:

Coordinates: 42.3085, -71.1142

Type/Specialty: Café

Specific Examples: Freshly baked goods, breakfast sandwiches, community vibe.

Orientation: As you step into Ula Cafe, the scent of freshly brewed coffee wraps around you, mingling with the chatter and soft clinking of cups. Once inside, take a moment to appreciate the art adorning the walls, all crafted by local artists.

Ula Cafe is a vibrant gathering place in Boston’s Jamaica Plain, weaving together the community’s past and present. While modern in its offerings, the café is deeply rooted in the neighborhood’s history. The nearby Haffenreffer Brewery, established in 1870 by German immigrant Rudolph Frederick Haffenreffer, was a cornerstone of Jamaica Plain and remained in the Haffenreffer family for generations until the mid-20th century. This legacy of enterprise and community spirit continues at Ula Cafe, which reflects the neighborhood’s enduring values.

In November 2022, the café’s commitment to being a safe and inclusive space was tested when a transgender barista was targeted with a transphobic tirade. Patrons and staff quickly intervened, escorting the aggressor out and supporting the barista. In response, co-owners Marvin Mathelier and Beth Santos made a public commitment to protect their space, sending a community bulletin that announced immediate ejections for anyone threatening the safe environment they had cultivated. They also redirected café revenue to raise funds for GLASS, an organization supporting LGBTQ+ youth. This challenging event reinforced Ula Cafe’s role as a bastion of community values.

Another testament to this spirit is the 'Pay-It-Forward' wall, where customers can purchase meals or coffee in advance for those in need. This simple yet profound gesture of kindness echoes the neighborhood’s history of resilience and mutual support. Ula Cafe stands not just as a place to savor coffee, but as a living narrative of Jamaica Plain’s commitment to inclusivity and community.

Directions: As you stroll, you’ll pass by the beautiful green space of Jamaica Plain’s Arnold Arboretum on your left. Once you reach Centre Street, continue straight until you spot Tres Gatos on your right, just a bit past the colorful storefronts. Enjoy the charming vibe of this vibrant neighborhood as you make your way! It is about a minute's walk away.

Stop 3: Tres Gatos

Address:

Coordinates: 42.3091, -71.1151

Type/Specialty: Restaurant and Bar

Specific Examples: Spanish tapas, extensive wine selection, live music events.

Orientation: As you stand here at Tres Gatos in Jamaica Plain, let your senses guide you. Notice the eclectic mix of patrons, from book lovers to coffee aficionados, all drawn to this hybrid café and bookstore.

Tres Gatos is not just a café; it is a living narrative of Jamaica Plain's vibrant community spirit. In 2015, Tres Gatos became one of the first establishments in Massachusetts to introduce a mandatory 'Hospitality Administrative Fee' added directly to checks. This was a response to ongoing discussions about fair wages in the hospitality industry. By shifting part of the cost burden to patrons, Tres Gatos aimed to ensure equitable pay for its staff and sparked conversations across the local restaurant scene. This decision marked a pivotal moment in how restaurants engaged with their patrons about the true cost of dining out.
