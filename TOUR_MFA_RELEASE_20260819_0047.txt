From this vantage point, the interplay between Salvador Dalí’s visual interpretations and Sigmund Freud’s written word becomes truly apparent, inviting you to consider how image and narrative coalesce into a singular artistic vision.

Salvador Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism" unfold as a set of ten works, each printed on sheepskin. This medium, chosen for its tactile qualities, echoes the ancient origins of monotheistic thought that Freud explored in his text. Dalí's illustrations delve into Freud's radical theory that Moses was not Hebrew but rather an Egyptian nobleman. This notion challenges traditional religious narratives, mirroring the era's shifting perceptions of identity and belief. Salvador Dalí’s unique visual style brings Freud’s complex ideas to life, transforming the book into a dynamic dialogue between art and psychoanalysis. In this exhibition, the evolution of belief is visually represented through modern art, inviting contemplation on how such collaborations continue to shape our understanding of history and culture.

Stop 3: Au Soleil du Plafond by Juan Gris

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3387, -71.0879

Orientation: Look for Au Soleil du Plafond in the galleries.

In 1955, Éditions Verve, known for producing high-quality art books, published "Au Soleil du Plafond," a livre d'artiste featuring Gris's artwork and Reverdy's poetry. Gris, a Spanish artist who contributed substantially to the Cubist style, used 11 lithographs to complement Reverdy's manuscript. These lithographs were printed on Arches wove paper, known for its durability and texture, which added depth to the visual experience. The partnership between Gris and Reverdy was not merely an artistic endeavor but a merging of two creative visions. Reverdy's text, known for its sparse and evocative language, aligned with Gris's structured and geometric compositions. Together, they created a work that advanced the exhibition's thesis: that the book itself can be a complete artwork where image, word, and typography coexist harmoniously. The decision by Éditions Verve to publish this work was a deliberate choice to push the boundaries of what an art book could be. Collaborating with a poet like Reverdy emphasized the synergy between visual and literary arts. As you reflect on "Au Soleil du Plafond," consider the lasting impression such a transformation leaves on our understanding of art's role in shaping belief and perception.
