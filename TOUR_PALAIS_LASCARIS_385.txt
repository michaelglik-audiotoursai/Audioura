Palais Lascaris, a seventeenth-century aristocratic building, located in the heart of Nice, France, houses a significant piece of musical history. This masterpiece by Antonio de Torres, often hailed as the father of the modern guitar, represents a pivotal moment in the evolution of the instrument. In 1884, Torres proudly spoke of the excellence of his creations, referring to the guitars from his workshops in the plural form. The deep resonant tones emanating from this guitar echo the rich history of its maker and the legacy he left behind. Antonio de Torres' guitars are legendary, earning him the title of the Stradivari of guitar making. His innovations in design and construction set a new standard for classical guitars, blending harmonies with emerging polyphonic textures. This particular instrument, crafted in Almeria in 1884, showcases Torres' unparalleled skill and attention to detail. This bass violin by Paolo Antonio Testore is a remarkable example of Baroque instrument-making. Crafted in 1696, it showcases a rich, warm tone wood body with delicate F-holes, a decorative tailpiece, and a scroll at the headstock. The use of high-quality materials and precise craftsmanship is evident in every curve and line of this instrument, reflecting the artistry of its time. Historically, the Basse de violon by Testore offers a glimpse into the musical culture and craftsmanship of 17th-century Milan. It represents a time when string instruments were highly valued for their tonal qualities and intricate designs, catering to the needs of skilled musicians and composers of the era.
