In 1971, Joan Miró created "Le Lézard aux plumes d'or," an illustrated book featuring forty color lithographs. This project was published by Louis Broder, who had begun a partnership with Miró in 1956 alongside the renowned lithographic studio. Because of this collaboration, Miró's vivid lithographs were brought to life, blending poetry and visual art in a unique portfolio. The resulting work became one of Miró's most celebrated illustrated portfolios, marking a significant achievement in his artistic career. The medium of publisher’s vellum binds this edition of 100, providing a tactile experience that enhances the visual spectacle. This work exemplifies the exhibition's thesis that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative ventures. Miró’s integration of images and text in "Le Lézard aux plumes d'or" demonstrates the seamless fusion of multiple art forms, turning a book into a cohesive artwork. Boris Fridman's generous donation of this portfolio to the Museum of Fine Arts enriches the museum’s collection, allowing visitors to marvel at how art can transform the mundane into the magical. Stop 2: Au Soleil du Plafond by Juan Gris

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3391, -71.0879

Orientation: Stand directly beneath the installation of "Au Soleil du Plafond" to appreciate its transformation of light into a vibrant spectrum. This angle reveals the intricate interplay between Gris's lithographs and Reverdy's text, offering a comprehensive view of the collaboration.

In 1916-1917, L. Rosenberg conceived a plan for a book titled "Au Soleil du Plafond," involving the collaboration of Spanish artist Juan Gris and French poet Pierre Reverdy. However, Gris passed away in 1927, leaving the project incomplete with only half of the intended work finished. Despite this, the vision was realized posthumously in 1955 when the book was published by Éditions Verve in Paris, featuring lithographs based on Gris's work and Reverdy's text. The publication brought together the artist's visual style and the poet's words, creating a harmonious collaboration that spanned decades beyond Gris's lifetime. This book, a part of the exhibition's thesis, exemplifies the transformative power of the livre d'artiste. Image, text, and typography coexist as an integrated artwork. Printed by Mourlot Frères, a renowned printing workshop in Paris, it includes 11 lithographs echoing Gris’s cubist aesthetics. The interplay of Reverdy's lyrical prose with Gris's visual narratives speaks to a time when the boundaries between art forms were blurred, embodying a dialogue that extends beyond the canvas to reshape perceptions.

In 1942, Salvador Dalí published his autobiography, "The Secret Life of Salvador Dalí," which revealed his fascination with Sigmund Freud's theories. This interest in Freud's work laid the groundwork for Dalí's later artistic endeavors. In 1974, Dalí created illustrations for Freud's "Moses and Monotheism," embedding his surrealist style into the interpretation of Freud's text. This project marked a significant intersection of psychoanalysis and surrealism, showing how Dalí's earlier interests had matured into a new artistic expression. Dalí's illustrations are not merely visual accompaniments; they are pivotal in transforming Freud's analytical narrative into a multisensory experience. The use of intricate line work and unexpected juxtapositions of imagery invites viewers to engage with Freud's ideas on a deeper level. Dalí’s interpretations add another layer, enhancing the book's impact as an art object. This collaboration exemplifies how artists like Dalí and Freud revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative ventures, a central argument of the exhibition. As you ponder these illustrations, consider what enduring truths about perspective and narrative this artistic dialogue has left us.
