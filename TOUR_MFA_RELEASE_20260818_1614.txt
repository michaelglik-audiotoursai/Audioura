Joan Miró's "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" is a mesmerizing illustrated book from 1971, featuring 40 color lithographs on publisher’s vellum. Published by Louis Broder, this work exemplifies the profound collaboration that defined the Surrealist movement. Broder, known for his passion for limited-edition books, commissioned the work to revive the livre d'artiste tradition, ensuring artist, poet, and Mourlot Frères collaborated closely. Mourlot Frères, a renowned French lithographic studio, printed these vivid images, bringing Miró's surreal visions to life on the page. The vibrant interplay between color and text in the lithographs draws from the Surrealists' fascination with dreams and subconscious themes. This book is more than a collection of images; it's an intricate dance of image, word, and typography, pushing the boundaries of how art can be experienced. Stop 2: Au Soleil du Plafond

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3398, -71.0897

Orientation: Allow your gaze to settle on "Au Soleil du Plafond" from a slightly elevated angle.

Published by Éditions Verve, this piece consists of 11 lithographs that intertwine Gris's cubist sensibilities with Reverdy's poetic text. The lithographs were brought to life by Mourlot Frères, a Parisian printing house known for its meticulous craftsmanship. The choice of Arches paper, renowned for its quality and durability, added a tactile dimension to the work, ensuring that each lithograph's colors and lines were vividly preserved. This creation exemplifies the exhibition's thesis, which argues that the book itself becomes an artwork through collaborative ventures. Gris and Reverdy's partnership reflects this ideal, as they seamlessly integrated text and image into a unified whole. This work, rarely on public view, is part of a larger collection that includes "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," highlighting how shifting perspectives can unveil new truths. In the Torf Gallery, named after a generous patron, one can witness how "Au Soleil du Plafond" expands the boundaries of traditional art forms, making the mundane extraordinary.

Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3398, -71.0897

Orientation: Stand close to the display of Moses and Monotheism, allowing you to see the detailed texture of the sheepskin and the interplay of image and text that becomes most vivid from this perspective.

Salvador Dalí's illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism" are part of a set of ten drypoints and lithographs, created on sheepskin. Freud, the renowned psychoanalyst, authored the text in 1939, proposing the controversial theory that Moses was an Egyptian nobleman rather than a Hebrew, thereby challenging established religious beliefs. This work advances the exhibition's thesis by showcasing how the integration of Dalí's surreal illustrations with Freud's psychoanalytic text creates a profound dialogue between image and narrative. The result is an exploration of the origins of monotheism, revealing hidden truths about religious identity. In connecting these elements, the exhibition argues for the book as a deeply collaborative and revolutionary art form, where the intersection of images, words, and typography reimagines deeply held beliefs.
