Stop 1: Portret van Anna Wake (1605-voor 1669)

Address: Plein 29, 2511 CS Den Haag, Netherlands

Coordinates: 52.0805, 4.313

Museum Information: Monday, 13:00–18:00. €21

Orientation: You are about to explore the Mauritshuis in The Hague. Your first stop is Portret van Anna Wake (1605-voor 1669). To truly experience "Portret van Anna Wake (1605-voor 1669)," step back to appreciate the full effect of Van Dyck's masterful composition, which fills the canvas with light and rich detail that might otherwise elude a closer view.

This work captures Anna Wake in an elegant pose, her figure almost life-sized on the 112.5 x 99.5 cm canvas, against a subtly rendered background. Anthony van Dyck's use of oil on canvas here showcases his signature ability to convey both the dignity and the subtle emotional depth of his subjects. At the time of this painting, Van Dyck was at the height of his career, having recently returned to Antwerp from England, where his reputation as a leading portraitist was secured. Van Dyck's style, marked by fluid lines and a delicate interplay of light and shadow, set him apart as a true innovator of portraiture. Standing before this work, one can sense the quiet strength and poise of Anna Wake. As the eldest daughter of a prominent family and the wife of a successful cloth merchant, her portrait reflects the societal expectations of women in the 17th century. Van Dyck's rendering offers an intimate glimpse into Anna's personal world beneath her formal exterior. This painting captures not only her likeness but also the broader cultural and social milieu of its time, providing viewers with a timeless connection to the past.

Directions: Continue through Mauritshuis — next is Het loflied van Simeon.

Stop 2: Het loflied van Simeon

Address: Plein 29, 2511 CS Den Haag, Netherlands

Coordinates: 52.0805, 4.313

Orientation: From this vantage point, the cavernous temple interior becomes a stage, where light plays upon the figures, drawing your gaze toward their faces and gestures.

In 1631, an artist signed his work with the monogram 'RHL 1631', marking the completion of a panel depicting the biblical subject of Simeon's Song of Praise, an oil painting by rembrandt. This work, unlike Rembrandt's later unfinished depiction in 1669, placed the figures in a Gothic-style temple interior, drawing inspiration from the architecture of churches in Leiden. As a result, the completed panel offered a richly detailed and unique interpretation of the biblical scene, setting it apart from other portrayals and anchoring it in the architectural context of its time. This work, an oil on canvas measuring 94.5 by 107.5 cm, envelops the viewer in a dark, expansive space, reminiscent of the sackbut you stopped at a moment ago, where the interplay of light and shadow projects a sense of divine revelation. De Gelder's choice to depict Simeon in such a majestic setting reflects not only the biblical narrative but also an exploration of spiritual and personal understanding. Arent de Gelder, a student of Rembrandt, was deeply influenced by his master's techniques. By the time he painted Het loflied van Simeon, de Gelder was at a mature stage in his career, seeking to capture the emotional depth and complexity that characterized his mentor's work. The painting’s composition, with its focus on the interplay between light and dark, evokes a feeling of introspection and divine grace, drawing viewers into the intimate exchange between Simeon and the child.

Together, these stops explore the emotional depth and societal context of portraiture and biblical narratives in the 17th century. This connection invites us to reflect on the timeless human experience and the complexities of identity and spirituality. That's 2 stops in all.
