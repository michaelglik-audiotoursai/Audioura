Stop 1: Union Oyster House

Address: 41 Union St, Boston, MA 02108

Coordinates: 42.3612, -71.0568

Type/Specialty: Seafood

Specific Examples: Oldest operating restaurant in the US, Kennedy Booth, clam chowder

Orientation: Head northeast on Union St, notice the cobblestone streets. Position yourself to best view this location.

Inside the Union Oyster House, history and culinary tradition intertwine. Isaiah Thomas printed his revolutionary newspaper, The Massachusetts Spy, in this building in 1771. The exiled Duke of Orléans, Louis-Philippe, taught French lessons here in 1796 before ascending to the French throne in 1830. John F. Kennedy found solitude in Booth 18 during the 1940s and 1950s. The Union Oyster House is open from 11:00 am to 9:00 pm Sunday through Thursday and until 10:00 pm on weekends, welcoming both walk-ins and those seeking its storied walls.


Directions: Walk south on Tremont St, then turn right onto Stuart St, notice the Prudential Tower ahead.



Stop 2: The Capital Grille

Address: 900 Boylston St, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3477, -71.0792

Type/Specialty: Steakhouse

Specific Examples: Dry-aged steaks, extensive wine list, upscale ambiance

Orientation: Position yourself to best view this location.

In 2003, during the height of the storied ALCS rivalry, Yankees bench coach Don Zimmer found himself at the center of unexpected applause here. Following a heated brawl between the Boston Red Sox and the Yankees, Zimmer received a standing ovation from the Boston crowd, a rare moment of sportsmanship that marked the evening. In 2012, The Capital Grille was embroiled in a federal class-action lawsuit filed by Restaurant Opportunities Centers (ROC) United against Darden Restaurants, which spotlighted issues of wage theft and racial discrimination. The restaurant operates from 11:30 AM to 10:00 PM, with reservations recommended and main courses priced between 73 and 93 USD.


Directions: Head southwest on Boylston St, then turn left onto Dartmouth St, notice the South End architecture.



Stop 3: Toro

Address: 1704 Washington St, Boston, MA 02118

Coordinates: 42.3381, -71.0761

Type/Specialty: Spanish Tapas

Specific Examples: Lively atmosphere, grilled corn, paella, extensive sherry selection

Orientation: As you stand outside Toro, the lively hum of Washington Street fills the air, mingling with the enticing aromas of simmering paella and sizzling tapas. The warm, inviting glow from within, promising a vibrant dining experience can be found here.

From the start, the restaurant attracted attention—though not always for the right reasons. In its early days, diners often mistook Spanish tapas for Tex-Mex, expecting tortilla chips and nachos. The confusion didn't deter the chefs; instead, it fueled their determination to educate and delight. In 2006, The Boston Globe, a major daily newspaper in Boston, praised Toro's food but criticized its chaotic service—feedback that spurred improvements. Notably, Bissonnette once bet Oringer on making calf brains in pasta a hit, and succeeded, showcasing their bold culinary philosophy. Today, Toro stands open from 5 PM to 10 PM on weekdays, extending to 11 PM on weekends, with Sunday brunch from 11 AM to 3 PM. Booking is recommended for weekend evenings. Prices for pintxos and tapas range from $6 to $26, while their renowned paella can cost between $36 and $92.


Directions: Walk east on Washington St, then turn right onto Tileston St, notice the historic North End neighborhood.



Stop 4: Neptune Oyster

Address: 63 Salem St, Boston, MA 02113

Coordinates: 42.3647, -71.0556

Type/Specialty: Seafood

Specific Examples: Oyster bar, lobster rolls, limited seating

Orientation: From this vantage point, you can admire Neptune Oyster. This unassuming seafood haven might seem modest, but it has an impressive reputation.

Opened in 2004 and Kelli Nace, Neptune Oyster quickly became a sensation, challenging initial doubts about its potential success. The restaurant is renowned for its fresh seafood, particularly the warm, buttered Connecticut-style lobster roll it popularized in Boston. Despite its acclaim, Neptune Oyster remains humble, with no reservations and a kitchen that closes at 9:30 PM. Its limited seating often leads to long waits, creating friction with neighboring shopkeepers. Chef Michael Serpa, the chef known for his work, instrumental in its rise, eventually left to open his own restaurants, prompting speculation about Neptune's future.
