Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: Musee des Arts Asiatiques (Asian Art Museum), Nice, France - Museum Tour
Tour-Category: museum

Stop 1: La geste de Bouddha

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

The "Geste de Bouddha" — a grey schist relief panel from Gandhara, Pakistan, dating to the 2nd or 3rd century CE — depicts five scenes from the life of Siddhartha Gautama. The Gandharan artists combined Greek sculptural techniques, introduced by Alexander the Great's campaigns after 326 BCE, with Indian Buddhist iconography. The relief measures approximately 45 centimetres in height and shows the narrative episodes in a continuous frieze format. Each scene is separated by architectural elements — pillars and archways — that reflect both Hellenistic and Indian building traditions.

Stop 2: Armure de samouraï

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

This Edo-period samurai armour (yoroi), dating to approximately 1750, represents the highly codified martial tradition of Japan's warrior class. The ensemble comprises a kabuto (helmet) with kuwagata (horn-shaped crest), a do (cuirass) of lacquered iron plates laced with silk cord (odoshi), and matching kote (armoured sleeves) and suneate (shin guards). The iron plates are connected using the kebiki-odoshi technique — a dense, all-over lacing pattern that provides both flexibility and protection. The lacquer coating, applied in multiple layers over months, protects the metal from the humid Japanese climate.

Stop 3: Paravent aux grues

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

This six-panel folding screen (byōbu) depicts red-crowned cranes (Grus japonensis) against a gold-leaf background, a quintessential subject of Rinpa school painting. The Rinpa movement, founded by Hon'ami Kōetsu and Tawaraya Sōtatsu in early 17th-century Kyoto, emphasised bold decorative compositions using techniques like tarashikomi (pooling of wet pigments). The gold leaf — applied in sheets measuring approximately 15 centimetres square — creates the luminous background that characterises Momoyama and early Edo luxury screens. The cranes, painted in mineral pigments on the gold ground, symbolise longevity and fidelity in Japanese culture.

Stop 4: Bouddha khmer

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

This seated Buddha in the Khmer style dates to the Angkor period, approximately 12th century CE, during the reign of Jayavarman VII — the last great king of the Khmer Empire who ruled from 1181 to approximately 1218. The sandstone figure displays the dhyana mudra (meditation gesture) with both hands resting in the lap. The smooth, idealised facial features — downcast eyes, slight smile, elongated earlobes — follow conventions established during the Bayon period. The Buddha sits on a nāga throne, with the seven-headed serpent Mucalinda spreading its hood as a protective canopy.

Stop 5: Collection de thé

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

The tea ceremony (chanoyu) collection includes implements spanning the Momoyama period (1573-1603) to the present. Sen no Rikyū, who codified the wabi-cha aesthetic under Toyotomi Hideyoshi in the 1580s, established that simplicity and imperfection were the highest virtues in tea utensils. The collection includes a Raku-ware tea bowl (chawan) in the style of Chōjirō — Rikyū's preferred potter, active from approximately 1574 to 1592. Raku bowls are hand-shaped rather than thrown on a wheel, then fired at low temperatures and removed while still glowing, producing distinctive crackled glazes.

Stop 6: Estampes ukiyo-e

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

The ukiyo-e ("pictures of the floating world") woodblock prints represent Japan's most influential graphic art form, flourishing from the 1680s through the 1860s. Katsushika Hokusai published his iconic "Thirty-Six Views of Mount Fuji" series between 1830 and 1832, including "The Great Wave off Kanagawa" — possibly the most reproduced image in world art history. The printing process required a team: the artist (eshi) created the design, the carver (horishi) cut separate cherry-wood blocks for each colour, and the printer (surishi) hand-pressed each sheet using a baren. A single print could require 15 to 20 separate impressions.

Stop 7: Textiles indiens

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

The Indian textile collection includes examples of the mordant-dyeing and resist-printing techniques that made Indian cloth the most traded commodity in the pre-industrial world. The Coromandel Coast workshops produced chintz (from Hindi chhint, meaning "spotted") using a complex process requiring 14 to 20 separate stages over several months. Each colour demanded a different mordant — alum for red, iron for black, tin for orange — painted freehand onto the cloth before immersion in the dye bath. The French East India Company imported 100,000 pieces annually through Pondicherry by the 1680s.

Stop 8: Architecture du musée

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, Nice, France
Coordinates: 43.6717, 7.2154
Museum Information: Musée des Arts Asiatiques de Nice

Kenzo Tange designed the museum building, inaugurated on October 16, 1998, as a meditation on the four elements in Asian philosophy. The white marble structure — square, circle, and triangular forms floating above an artificial lake — deliberately echoes both Japanese Shinto shrine architecture and the Buddhist mandala. Tange, who won the Pritzker Prize in 1987, described the building as "a dialogue between tradition and technology." The museum sits within a 7-hectare Asian garden in the Parc Phoenix, itself designed as a landscape journey through Asian botanical traditions.
