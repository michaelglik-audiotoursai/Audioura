Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts, Vietnam
Tour-Category: museum

Stop 1: Vườn xuân Trung Nam Bắc

Coordinates: 10.7753, 106.6981

Orientation: You are about to explore the Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts in Vietnam. These diverse pieces showcase different mediums and periods of Vietnamese artistry. Your first stop is Vườn xuân Trung Nam Bắc. Stand a few feet back from "Vườn xuân Trung Nam Bắc" at the Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts to fully appreciate its expansive 540cm by 200cm canvas. From this vantage, the intricate details of Nguyễn Gia Trí's lacquer masterpiece, created over twenty years, manifest vividly, offering a window into Vietnam's diverse regions and cultural unity.

This work emerged not just as the largest lacquer painting by a Vietnamese artist but also as a cultural mosaic integrating the landscapes of Central, Southern, and Northern Vietnam. The choice of lacquer was intentional, a medium Nguyen revered for its depth and continuity, echoing the country's rich artistic heritage. Trí's decades-long commitment to this piece culminated in 1989, delivering a visual narrative that extends beyond mere representation—it embodies Vietnam’s unity in diversity. This designation highlights the painting's role as a vital cultural artifact, reminding viewers of Vietnam's intricate cultural tapestry. As you observe the subtle interplay of light and lacquer layers, consider the dedication and skill that Trí poured into each stroke, creating a timeless testament to a country's identity and artistic tradition. While working together, his student Nguyễn Xuân Việt wondered aloud where the immense painting would eventually end up, but the master dismissed the concern, maintaining that true art asks for nothing.

Directions: Continue through Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts — next is Thiếu nữ bên hoa huệ.

Stop 2: Thiếu nữ bên hoa huệ

Coordinates: 10.7753, 106.6981

Orientation: Stand close to "Thiếu nữ bên hoa huệ" in the soft-lit gallery where the painting resides, underlining the serene grace of its subject. From this vantage point, the interplay of light and shadow across the canvas comes alive, and the subtleties of expression become intimate and palpable.

In 1943, Tô Ngọc Vân completed "Thiếu nữ bên hoa huệ," an oil painting that stands as his most celebrated work. He skillfully layered oil paint to bring out the subtleties of the young woman's demeanor, choosing this medium amidst the backdrop of World War II and the Japanese occupation. The painting portrays a young woman next to lilies, symbolizing purity and the evolving modernity of Vietnamese identity. Her thoughtful and serene expression invites viewers into a moment of contemplation and reflection. Vân's meticulous attention to detail reveals his profound desire to preserve the quiet dignity and resilience of Vietnamese identity during a time of national upheaval.

Continue to Thanh niên thành đồng.

Stop 3: Thanh niên thành đồng

Coordinates: 10.7753, 106.6981

Orientation: As you stand before "Thanh niên thành đồng," find a vantage point where the light interacts with the lacquered surface, illuminating the vivid layers beneath. This perspective will reveal the depth and texture of Nguyễn Sáng's work, inviting you into the spirit and struggle it embodies.

In 1978, Nguyễn Sáng completed "Thanh niên thành đồng," using lacquer on wood, a medium deeply rooted in Vietnamese tradition. This work became an integral part of his artistic journey, as Sáng channeled the historic resilience and courage of Southern Vietnamese youth who resisted hardship. It was a challenging period for the artist, shaped by post-war recovery and the fresh scars of recent conflicts. In creating this piece, Sáng captured not only a moment of defiant bravery but also a tribute to cultural heritage. Throughout his career, Sáng's commitment to his themes and techniques never wavered. His motivation to honor Vietnam's cultural lineage through lacquer was deliberate, ensuring the work resonates with viewers and evokes a lasting emotional connection.

Directions: Proceed to Sơn nữ.

Stop 4: Sơn nữ

Coordinates: 10.7753, 106.6981

Orientation: Stand at the end of the room where Sơn nữ, or "Mountain Girl," is displayed. From here, the subtleties of Nguyễn Phan Chánh's silk painting become apparent, revealing the interplay of light and texture that speaks to its cultural significance.

Sơn nữ, a celebrated work by Nguyễn Phan Chánh, depicts a figure set within a Vietnamese landscape. This silk painting stands out among Chánh's works for its innovative use of silk as a medium, enabling gentle transitions among hues. The choice of silk reflects Chánh’s respect for Vietnamese traditions and sets a benchmark for later artists in the nation.

In 1925, Nguyễn Phan Chánh became the first Vietnamese artist to enroll at the Indochina Fine Arts College, a landmark moment that positioned him as a trailblazer in Vietnamese modern art. This allowed him to develop a distinctive style that blended traditional Vietnamese techniques with modern influences. Chánh was not working in isolation; his methods led to a broader movement within Vietnam's art scene, where silk painting gained prestige and expanded beyond its traditional boundaries. This transformation redefined the possibilities for Vietnamese artists and encouraged them to explore native materials in new, expressive ways.

The cultural resonance of Chánh's work is reflected in Sơn nữ, where he captures a scene that is both personal and universal, steeped in local authenticity yet accessible to a broader audience. Chánh’s integration of heritage and innovation continues to inspire the next generation of Vietnamese artists.

Directions: Your final stop in Ho Chi Minh City Museum of Fine Arts: Em Thúy.

Stop 5: Em Thúy

Coordinates: 10.7753, 106.6981

Orientation: Position yourself directly in front of "Em Thúy" and observe how the subdued hues frame the delicate expression of the young girl, drawing your attention to the tender capture of innocence and hope in her gaze—an emblematic vision of the era.

In 1943, "Em Thúy" was first unveiled to the public at the Foyer de l'Art Annamite (FARTA), during a period when Vietnam was striving to define its artistic identity under French colonial rule. Cẩn chose to portray his sister, capturing her innocence with muted tones that convey a yearning for brighter days. The artist found solace in familial bonds and the hope they represented for the future of his homeland during uncertain times. By creating such a deeply personal work, Cẩn did not merely paint a figure; he invoked a sense of resilience and ambition that reflected both his own aspirations and a collective national hope. During wartime, when her family had to evacuate, the painting was left behind and lost in the chaos. Eventually, the family tracked it down to a barber's house and bought it back from an art dealer.

Together, these stops highlight the resilience and evolving identity of Vietnam through the lens of its artists, showcasing their commitment to cultural heritage and personal narratives. This exploration invites us to reflect on the power of art as a means of connection and hope in times of uncertainty, as seen in works like "Em Thúy".
