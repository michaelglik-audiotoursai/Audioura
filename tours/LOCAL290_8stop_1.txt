Step-by-Step Audio Guided Tour: French Riviera Cycling Tour
Tour-Category: cycling

Stop 1: Old Town of Menton

Address: Vieille Ville, Menton, France
Coordinates: 43.7764, 7.5028

The old town of Menton rises steeply from the harbour in a cascade of ochre and terracotta facades dating from the 17th century. Jean Cocteau, who lived here intermittently from 1956, called it "the pearl of France" and decorated the Salle des Mariages in the town hall with mythological murals in 1957-1958. The Basilica of Saint-Michel-Archange, built in 1619 and expanded in 1675, dominates the skyline with its Baroque bell tower reaching 53 metres. The narrow streets — ruelles — follow medieval defensive patterns, with covered passageways allowing defenders to move unseen.

Stop 2: Eze Village

Address: Place du Centenaire, Eze, France
Coordinates: 43.7282, 7.3612

Perched 427 metres above the sea, Eze Village is a medieval fortification first documented in 1241 under the House of Anjou. Friedrich Nietzsche composed the third part of "Thus Spoke Zarathustra" while walking the steep path from the shore — now named Chemin de Nietzsche — in 1883-1884. The Jardin Exotique, established in 1949, occupies the ruins of a 12th-century castle destroyed by Louis XIV's forces in 1706. The garden contains over 400 species of succulents and cacti arranged across the castle foundations.

Stop 3: Cap Ferrat

Address: Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat, France
Coordinates: 43.6885, 7.3282

Cap Ferrat has attracted European aristocracy since King Leopold II of Belgium purchased land here in 1895, eventually owning most of the peninsula. Baroness Béatrice Ephrussi de Rothschild built her Villa Île-de-France between 1905 and 1912, filling it with 5,000 artworks including Sèvres porcelain and Aubusson tapestries. Somerset Maugham lived at Villa Mauresque from 1928 until his death in 1965, hosting writers including Noël Coward and Ian Fleming. The coastal path — Sentier du Littoral — circles the cape in approximately 2 hours.

Stop 4: Villefranche-sur-Mer

Address: Quai Courbet, Villefranche-sur-Mer, France
Coordinates: 43.7046, 7.3116

The deep natural harbour of Villefranche has served as a naval port since the Greeks established a trading post here around 300 BCE. Charles II of Anjou founded the "free town" (villa franca) in 1295, exempting it from taxes to encourage settlement. Jean Cocteau decorated the 14th-century Chapelle Saint-Pierre with scenes of fishermen and the life of Saint Peter in 1957. The Citadelle Saint-Elme, built in 1554 by Duke Emmanuel Philibert of Savoy, now houses the town hall and three museums.

Stop 5: Antibes

Address: Vieil Antibes, France
Coordinates: 43.5808, 7.1254

Antibes — ancient Antipolis, founded by Greeks from Massalia around 340 BCE — guards the western approach to the Bay of Angels. Picasso worked in the Château Grimaldi for six months in 1946, producing 23 paintings and 44 drawings that remain in what is now the Musée Picasso. Napoleon Bonaparte was briefly imprisoned in Fort Carré in 1794 during the political upheaval following Thermidor. The Marché Provençal in the covered hall on Cours Masséna has operated since 1927, selling local olives, socca, and flowers.

Stop 6: Corniche d'Or

Address: Route de la Corniche d'Or, Théoule-sur-Mer, France
Coordinates: 43.5090, 6.9380

The Corniche d'Or, completed in 1903 under the direction of the Touring Club de France, follows the red porphyry cliffs of the Esterel massif for 30 kilometres between Fréjus and Cannes. The volcanic rock, formed 250 million years ago during the Permian period, creates dramatic contrasts with the Mediterranean blue. Auguste Escoffier, born in nearby Villeneuve-Loubet in 1846, drew inspiration from this coastline for his Mediterranean cuisine at the Carlton in Cannes.

Stop 7: Saint-Paul-de-Vence

Address: Rue Grande, Saint-Paul-de-Vence, France
Coordinates: 43.6963, 7.1212

Saint-Paul-de-Vence has been a walled village since Francis I fortified it against Charles V in 1537. Marc Chagall lived here from 1966 until his death in 1985, and is buried in the communal cemetery. The Fondation Maeght, designed by Josep Lluís Sert and opened in 1964, was the first major private foundation for modern art in France. Its collection includes works by Miró, Giacometti, and Braque displayed in a building that integrates architecture with landscape through a series of interior courtyards.
