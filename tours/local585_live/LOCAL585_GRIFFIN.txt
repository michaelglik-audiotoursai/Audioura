Orientation: Stand in front of the photograph and let your eyes sweep across the gritty tableau Robert Frank captured. This positioning allows the full weight of Frank's transformative vision, a stark viewpoint that reshaped American photography, to wash over you.

In 1958, Robert Frank, armed with a camera and a Guggenheim fellowship, embarked on a cross-country journey across America. This journey gave birth to "The Americans," a photographic book that presented an unvarnished portrait of American society, capturing both its grandeur and humility. Despite initial controversy, the work became a linchpin in post-war photography. "Homage | Robert Frank: The Americans" at the Griffin Museum showcases Frank's enduring impact. Inspired by Frank, in 1993, Mark Erickson returned to Vietnam, applying a similar approach to document Vietnamese life. This demonstrated Frank's far-reaching influence, encouraging photographers globally to delve into societal narratives with honesty and depth.
