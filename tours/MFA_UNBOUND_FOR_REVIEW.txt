Sigmund Freud, the father of psychoanalysis, authored "Moses and Monotheism" in 1939, a text that delves into radical ideas about religious transformation and challenges prevailing notions of faith and identity. The Hogarth Press, known for its publication of Freud’s groundbreaking works, produced this edition, further propagating his controversial theories. Salvador Dalí, a prominent surrealist artist known for eccentricity, in 1974, brought his surrealist vision to Freud’s text through a series of illustrations. Dalí’s contributions offer a visual interpretation that pushes the boundaries of religious and psychoanalytic discourse. By integrating his vivid illustrations with Freud's text, Dalí helped transform the book into a cohesive artwork, embodying the exhibition’s thesis that artists revolutionized the book as a form of art through collaboration. This specific work, rarely on view outside archives, is displayed here in exemplifying the profound impact of two visionary minds meeting across time to create something enduring.

Orientation: This work is a striking example of how creativity can flourish within the confines of a book, a form known as a "livre d'artiste," where images, text, and typography converge.

Louis Broder, a publisher known for his interest in artist's books, commissioned this work from Gris. Broder's decision was driven by his belief that the livre d'artiste required the direct collaboration of artist and writer, resulting in a cohesive artistic vision. This collaboration with Pierre Reverdy, who provided the manuscript text, exemplifies this ideal. By working directly with artists like Gris, Broder advanced the collaborative book form, which the exhibition argues had no precedent. The intersection of Gris's lithographs and Reverdy's poetic text is crucial in understanding the work's impact. The lithographs, known for their bold and individualistic compositions, invite viewers to explore the dynamic interplay between image and word. The integration of typography with visual elements further enhances the narrative, making the book not just a collection of artworks but a unified artistic statement. Historically, this collaboration speaks to a vibrant era in the history of the livre d'artiste, where artists and writers sought to push the boundaries of what books could be.
