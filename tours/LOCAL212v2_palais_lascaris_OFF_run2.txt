Stop 1: Raquel

Address: Palais Lascaris, 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6975, 7.2744

Museum Information: 14:00–21:00

You are about to embark on a journey through the hallowed halls of Palais Lascaris, a building completed in the first half of the seventeenth century. Each step you take echoes with the harmonies of the past, where the power of music weaves tales of grandeur and grace. As you wander, you'll uncover the stories of those touched by the transformative force of music—like Raquel, whose presence symbolizes the artistic collaborations that once flourished here. The climax awaits with a masterpiece, the viol crafted by William Turner, showcasing the pinnacle of musical craftsmanship that graced these very halls. Let the melodies of the past guide you through this chapter of history, where each note resonates with the essence of Palais Lascaris's commitment to musical excellence and the enduring legacy of the Vintimille-Lascaris family.

Description:
In the heart of the Raquel exhibit at Palais Lascaris, a collection of exquisite artworks unfolds before you. One piece, in particular, commands attention — a stunning painting depicting a biblical scene of David and Goliath. The artist's meticulous brushwork brings to life the tension and drama of this iconic showdown, capturing the moment before the clash of titans. The choice of a dark, brooding color palette enhances the sense of impending conflict, drawing you into the narrative with its raw intensity. This painting not only showcases the artist's technical skill but also serves as a window into the cultural and religious beliefs of the time. The portrayal of David's bravery and Goliath's imposing figure speaks to the enduring themes of courage and triumph over adversity found in biblical lore. As you study the composition, take note of the subtle symbolism woven into each element, adding layers of meaning to the narrative. This artwork, nestled within the walls of Palais Lascaris, offers a glimpse into a world where art and faith intertwine, inviting viewers to ponder the timeless stories that continue to resonate across centuries. As you contemplate the significance of this piece, consider the pivotal role that biblical narratives played in shaping the beliefs and values of past civilizations, leaving a lasting imprint on the cultural landscape.

Stop 2: Violes gambe by William Turner (Londres, 1652)

Address: Palais Lascaris, 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

In the dimly lit gallery of Palais Lascaris, where the soft glow of antique chandeliers dances off the polished wood, the Violes gambe by William Turner commands attention. Crafted in London in the year 1652, this viola da gamba stands as a testament to the musical excellence that once graced the halls of this noble palace. The deep, rich tones of the wood seem to resonate with centuries of music that have passed through its strings. The Violes gambe itself is a masterful work of art, with intricate carvings adorning its neck and body. The scroll at the top is delicately fashioned, showcasing Turner's skill in wood carving. The strings, though aged, still seem to whisper the haunting melodies of long-forgotten compositions. This particular piece holds a unique historical significance, as it represents a time when music was not just entertainment but a symbol of refinement and sophistication among the aristocracy. In the seventeenth century, instruments like the Violes gambe were highly coveted for their craftsmanship and beauty, often displayed as symbols of wealth and cultural status. As you gaze upon this remarkable instrument, you can't help but wonder about the hands that once played it, the melodies that once filled these grand halls. The Palais Lascaris, with its extensive collection of over 500 instruments, including the bass violin by Paolo Antonio Testore from Milan, 1696, offers a glimpse into a world where music was revered as a true art form. How did such instruments inspire and transform the lives of the artists who played them? The Violes gambe by William Turner invites you to ponder the enduring legacy of music and craftsmanship throughout the ages.
