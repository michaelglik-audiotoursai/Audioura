Orientation: Midpoint of the display is where the interplay of text and image in Moses and Monotheism becomes apparent. This vantage reveals the intricate texture of sheepskin, allowing you to appreciate the depth at which illustrations connect to Freud's narrative.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí took on the monumental task of illustrating Sigmund Freud's Moses and Monotheism, bringing his surrealist lens to Freud's challenging exploration of religious identity. Freud, the father of psychoanalysis, authored the text in 1939, suggesting that Moses was originally an Egyptian priest — a theory that radically altered perceptions of monotheistic origins. Torf, a key patron, established this collection within the gallery that bears his name, ensuring the public could engage with pivotal collaborations between image and text. Artists like Dalí were instrumental in redefining the possibilities of illustrated books.

Stop 3: Au Soleil du Plafond

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3391, -71.0898

Orientation: This unique work, published by Éditions Verve and printed by Mourlot Frères, consists of 11 lithographs — an exemplary creation that redefines artistic collaboration through its blend of image, text, and typography.

In 1955, Éditions Verve tasked Gris, an innovator in the Cubist movement, with creating this extraordinary piece. The decision to team him with Reverdy, a pioneering figure in poetic expression, meant that the visuals and words were crafted to intertwine seamlessly on every page. Such synergy found under the umbrella of a livre d'artiste is precisely what the exhibition celebrates — artists revolutionizing the book form through collaborative ventures like these, where mutual influence guides the creative process. The palpable harmony between Gris and Reverdy reflects in the lithographic plates themselves. At their core, the illustrations communicate a vivid dialogue between light and shadow, reminiscent of sunlight dispersed over a surface—a metaphor for illuminating ideas. Reverdy's manuscript, lithographed to match Gris's designs, explores the porous boundaries between poetry and visual art, inviting viewers to engage in a dialogue where each element elevates the other. "Au Soleil du Plafond" continues to leave a profound impact on visitors, challenging perceptions and expanding the very nature of artistic expression.
