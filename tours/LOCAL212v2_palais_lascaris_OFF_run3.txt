You are about to embark on a journey through the hallowed halls of Palais Lascaris, a testament to the enduring legacy of music and craftsmanship. Completed in the first half of the seventeenth century and later restored in 1970, this palace holds hidden stories waiting to be uncovered. Each stop along the way reveals a different chapter of this captivating narrative. From Raquel's mysterious life in the 17th century to the exquisite Basse de violon crafted by Paolo Antonio Testore in Milan in 1696, the intricate web of music and artistry woven into the fabric of Palais Lascaris awaits your discovery. Join us as we delve into the rich tapestry of history and creativity that defines this remarkable space.

As you gaze upon the wooden cabinet, you are immediately struck by its grandeur and craftsmanship. The cabinet is adorned with delicate floral motifs, each petal meticulously carved to perfection. The choice of wood for this piece is significant, as the artist deliberately selected a rare variety known for its durability and natural beauty. This material not only adds to the visual appeal but also speaks to the artist's dedication to using the finest resources available. One particular technique that stands out is the inlay work on the cabinet doors. Intricate patterns of mother-of-pearl and ebony are skillfully integrated into the design, creating a striking contrast against the rich wood. This meticulous craftsmanship showcases the artist's attention to detail and mastery of the inlay technique, highlighting the fusion of different materials to achieve a harmonious whole. Adding a layer of cultural context, the floral motifs and inlay patterns on the cabinet are symbolic of the artist's heritage and traditions. These elements draw inspiration from the ornate designs prevalent in the 17th-century court of Nice, reflecting a blend of artistic influences from various regions. The cabinet not only serves as a functional piece of furniture but also as a visual representation of the cultural tapestry of the time. This exquisite cabinet in the Raquel exhibit not only stands as a testament to the artist's skill and creativity but also offers a glimpse into the opulent world of 17th-century Nice. As you explore the details of this piece, you'll uncover the hidden stories and symbols that Raquel carefully embedded, inviting you to unravel the mysteries she left behind.

Stop 2: Basse de violon by Paolo Antonio Testore (Milan, 1696)

Address: Palais Lascaris, 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

Description:
Crafted in 1696 by Milanese luthier Paolo Antonio Testore, the Basse de violon stands as a testament to the pinnacle of musical instrument making in Milan during the Baroque period. This exquisite instrument, part of Palais Lascaris's collection of over 500 instruments, embodies the artistry and precision of its maker. Testore, born in 1700 and part of the renowned Testore family of luthiers, learned his craft in Milan's Contrada Larga workshop under his father's guidance. What sets Paolo Antonio's work apart is his unique approach, often omitting purfling and occasionally utilizing wood of lower quality. Despite these deviations, his instruments, like the Basse de violon before you, display a remarkable level of detail and finesse. The historical transfer of Nice's musical instrument collections to Palais Lascaris in 2001 brought this exceptional piece to its current home, enriching the museum's offerings. As you gaze upon the Basse de violon, you can sense the weight of centuries in its elegant curves and meticulously carved details. Each element, from the scroll to the tailpiece, reflects the dedication and skill of its maker. This instrument not only serves as a functional piece but also as a work of art, resonating with the rich musical heritage of Milan and the broader Baroque period. Immerse yourself in the history and craftsmanship encapsulated in this remarkable instrument. Consider how the artistry of Paolo Antonio Testore influenced the evolution of music within the walls of Palais Lascaris. The Basse de violon invites you to explore the intersection of art, music, and history, offering a glimpse into a bygone era of musical excellence.
