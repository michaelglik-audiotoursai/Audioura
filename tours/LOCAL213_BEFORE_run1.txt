At Cap d'Antibes, you stand on the southern tip of the peninsula, where the ancient chapel of Notre Dame de Bon Port overlooks the azure waters. This geographical landmark is a testament to the natural beauty of the French Riviera, offering panoramic vistas of the Lérins Islands to the west and the Mercantour mountains to the east. In January 1888, the renowned artist Claude Monet sought inspiration on these shores, following in the footsteps of other creative luminaries who found solace in the tranquil surroundings. He stayed at the Château de la Pinède, where he depicted the interplay of light and color in his iconic Impressionist style. The coastal path known as "Tire-Poil" beckons visitors to wander along the rugged coastline, tracing a 2.7 km route from the Garoupe Beach parking lot to the historic Villa Eilenroc. This scenic trail meanders past imposing castle walls and rocky cliffs, offering a sensory feast of sights and sounds. The ancient structures of Château de la Garoupe and Château de la Croë stand as silent witnesses to the rich history of this coastal enclave. Imagine the artists and writers who once sought refuge in the idyllic surroundings of Cap d'Antibes, finding inspiration in the ever-changing hues of the sea and sky. The connection to Picasso and Monet infuses this stop with a creative energy that reverberates through the centuries, embodying the artistic legacy of this enchanting locale. Cycle further along the path, and envision the hidden coves where tales of intrigue and artistic brilliance await, echoing the whispers of the past in the timeless beauty of Cap d'Antibes.
