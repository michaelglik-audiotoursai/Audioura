Tour-Category: museum

Stop 1: L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6705, 7.2079

Museum Information: Closed on Tuesday. 10:00–17:00 (1 Sep–30 Jun); 10:00–18:00 (1 Jul–31 Aug). FREE

You are about to embark on a journey through the architectural vision of Kenzo Tange, shaping the Asian Arts Museum in Nice. Inaugurated on October 16, 1998, this cultural space weaves together the dedication of Pierre-Yves Trémois and the ancient artistry captured in each exhibit. Each stop in this narrative reveals a different facet of Tange's vision: from the compassionate gaze of Kannon to the symbolic dance of Ganesh. As you explore, you'll uncover the intersections of East and West that influenced both Trémois and Tange, all culminating in a harmonious blend of tradition and innovation. Join us as we delve into the unique blend of architectural styles and cultural intersections that define this museum, a testament to the enduring legacy of two visionaries.

As you gaze upon L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki, you are transported back to the mid-19th century in Japan, a time of exquisite artistry and skilled craftsmanship. This armor, known as Armure de type dô-maru, is a testament to the dedication and talent of Andô Naoyuki, who was just a teenager when he created this masterpiece. Crafted from steel, copper, leather, silk, lacquer, and gold leaf, this armor exemplifies the Edo period aesthetic with its attention to detail and precision. The combination of materials not only showcases the technical prowess of the artist but also reflects the cultural values and traditions of that era. The design of the armor features intricate motifs and patterns, symbolizing themes of honor, protection, and strength. Each element, from the elaborate helmet to the meticulously crafted chestplate, tells a story of tradition and heritage. The use of acier, a type of steel, highlights the importance of both functionality and artistry in samurai armor during the 19th century. This piece is a striking example of the fusion of art and history, offering a glimpse into the craftsmanship and cultural significance of armor during the Edo period in Japan. As you admire L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki, you can feel the spirit of the past come alive, resonating with the elegance and sophistication of 19th-century Japanese craftsmanship.

Stop 2: Statue de Bouddha

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

As you gaze upon the Statue de Bouddha, you are met with a profound fusion of Greek and Indian art styles, a testament to the cultural exchange during the conquests of Alexander the Great. This work, crafted in schiste, The statue, representing Buddha, stands tall and serene, embodying a sense of tranquility and spiritual enlightenment. Its grey schist material exudes a sense of timelessness, with each detail carefully crafted to convey a deeper meaning. Notable among these details are the lotus petals adorning the crown, symbolizing spiritual purity in Hindu iconography and reflecting the influence of Indian traditions. The four arms of the statue each hold a specific object — an axe, a rope, a broken tusk, and a sweetmeat — symbolizing detachment, rescue from illusion, the power of knowledge, and the rewards of a disciplined life, respectively. This ancient masterpiece not only showcases exceptional craftsmanship but also offers a window into the historical and cultural context of the 3rd century. It provides a rare glimpse into the artistry and beliefs of the era, connecting visitors to a time long past. The Statue de Bouddha invites contemplation on the enduring legacy of Buddhism and the artistic exchanges that shaped civilizations in the ancient world. As you absorb the profound presence of the Statue de Bouddha, consider how this ancient representation of Buddha resonates with Kenzo Tange's design principles, bridging the gap between ancient art and modern architectural vision. This remarkable statue serves as a reminder of the enduring influence of cultural intersections throughout history, encapsulated in a single timeless piece within the Asian Arts Museum.


Directions: Next: La danse cosmique de Ganesh.



Stop 3: La danse cosmique de Ganesh

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

Orientation: Stand at the center of the room, directly facing "La danse cosmique de Ganesh." From this vantage point, you'll witness the full glory of this sculpture and its intricate details that tell a captivating story.

As you stand here, your eyes are drawn to the majestic figure of Ganesh, the elephant-headed Hindu deity. This sculpture, dating back to the 10th century, is crafted from chlorite, a material that lends a unique texture and depth to the piece. Ganesh is depicted in a dynamic pose, captured mid-dance, symbolizing the eternal rhythm of the universe. Each aspect of the sculpture holds profound meaning. Notice Ganesh's four arms, each holding a significant object: an axe symbolizing detachment, a rope representing liberation from illusion, a tusk broken as a writing implement, and a sweetmeat signifying the rewards of a disciplined life. These objects offer insight into Ganesh's role as the remover of obstacles and the deity of new beginnings. This representation of Ganesh as a cosmic dancer reflects the artist's mastery in capturing movement and grace in stone. The intricate details on Ganesh's crown, adorned with lotus petals symbolizing spiritual purity, transport you to ancient Hindu iconography. This sculpture not only showcases artistic skill but also serves as a window into the spiritual beliefs and cultural practices of the 10th century. As you continue your journey through the museum, consider the cultural intersections embodied in Ganesh's dance, mirroring the architectural vision of Kenzo Tange, who designed this museum.


Directions: Proceed to Kannon, le bodhisattva de la compassion.



Stop 4: Kannon, le bodhisattva de la compassion

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

As you gaze upon this masterpiece from the second half of the 12th century, you are captivated by the serene presence of Kannon, the bodhisattva of compassion. This work, crafted in bois, Carved from cypress wood, this statue depicts Juichimen Kannon, known for its eleven heads symbolizing the varied ways in which Kannon aids those in need. Seated gracefully on a lotus and framed by a pierced mandorla, Kannon exudes a sense of divine tranquility and empathy. One striking aspect of this statue is the intricate gilding and lacquer work adorning Kannon's form, elevating the spiritual aura that emanates from the figure. The skillful use of these materials not only enhances the visual appeal but also adds a layer of sacredness to the representation of compassion embodied by Kannon. Embedded within the cultural and spiritual context of Japan, this statue resonates with the rich heritage of Buddhist traditions, where Kannon is revered as a symbol of mercy and salvation. The portrayal of Kannon with eleven heads speaks to the multifaceted nature of compassion and the boundless capacity for understanding and aiding those in distress. As you continue your journey through the museum, the compassionate gaze of Kannon serves as a poignant reminder of Trémois' dedication to preserving cultural legacies through art. Reflect on how this embodiment of compassion connects to the broader themes of empathy and cultural intersections within the museum's collection, weaving a narrative of spiritual depth and artistic excellence. Experience the timeless beauty and profound spirituality of Kannon, a testament to the enduring legacy of compassion in art and culture.


Directions: Continue to Ulysses Grant au Japon.



Stop 5: Ulysses Grant au Japon

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

In 1879, Toyohara Chikanobu masterfully depicted a pivotal moment in history through a xylogravure polychrome on papier. The print vividly portrays the reception at the imperial palace of the President of the United States, Ulysses Grant, and his wife during their visit to Japan. Chikanobu's attention to detail is evident in the elaborate attire of the figures, showcasing the fusion of traditional Japanese and Western influences. The scene not only captures the diplomatic encounter between the US and Japan but also serves as a testament to the East-West exchanges that influenced both Pierre-Yves Trémois and Kenzo Tange. This piece offers a glimpse into the cultural dialogue of the Meiji era, highlighting the evolving dynamics of global relations and artistic inspirations. As you study the print, observe the harmonious blend of cultural symbols and attire, reflecting the mutual respect and curiosity between nations during this period. The visual storytelling intricately weaves together historical context with artistic craftsmanship, inviting viewers to delve into a moment frozen in time — a moment that resonates with the weight of diplomatic significance and cross-cultural connections. 1879 is a year marked by the convergence of traditions and the dawn of a new era in international relations, encapsulated in this striking print that continues to bridge the past with the present within the walls of the Asian Arts Museum in Nice, France.

The intricate patterns not only serve decorative purposes but also convey deep spiritual meaning, reflecting the Taoist philosophy of seeking harmony and unity with the cosmos. Each symbol and motif on the robe tells a story of spiritual enlightenment and connection to the divine. This piece offers a glimpse into the rich cultural heritage of Taoism and the artistic traditions of the 18th century. It showcases the skill and artistry of the craftsmen who created it, highlighting the importance of textiles in conveying spiritual beliefs and cultural identity. The "Robe de prêtre taoïste" serves as a tangible link to the past, inviting viewers to immerse themselves in the spiritual world of Taoism and appreciate the artistry of the 18th century.


Directions: Proceed to Kannon à mille bras.



Stop 7: Kannon à mille bras

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

In the dimly lit gallery of the Asian Arts Museum in Nice, France, a masterpiece of spiritual grace and artistic finesse commands attention. "Kannon à mille bras" emerges before you, a bodhisattva of compassion seated upon a lotus pedestal, surrounded by a radiant mandorla. Eleven smaller heads crown the deity's main visage, each representing a different aspect of compassion and wisdom. But it's the multitude of arms that truly captivate the beholder — a visual testament to Kannon's boundless ability to aid and comfort all sentient beings. Crafted with meticulous precision, each of the thousand arms holds a sacred object, symbolizing various virtues and teachings. The axe severs attachment, the rope pulls devotees from illusion, the tusk serves as a writing implement, and the sweetmeat represents the rewards of a disciplined life. This intricate composition not only showcases the skill of the artisan but also offers a profound meditation on the complexities of existence and the path to enlightenment. Rooted in the rich tradition of Asian art, "Kannon à mille bras" carries deep cultural significance, embodying the ideals of compassion and selflessness revered in Buddhist teachings. The addition of this masterpiece to the museum's collection marks a significant enhancement, enriching the representation of Asian art within its hallowed halls. As you linger in contemplation before this awe-inspiring creation, you are invited to delve into the depths of spiritual devotion and artistic mastery that transcend time and boundaries.

Stop 8: Masque du vieillard kojô

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

As you gaze upon "Masque du vieillard kojô," you are immediately struck by the lifelike depiction of an elderly man, Kojô, a character from Noh theater. This work, crafted in bois, Carved from wood and adorned with delicate lacquer, this mask captures the wisdom and experience etched into the lines of the old man's face. The meticulous craftsmanship is evident in the subtle textures of the wood and the skillful application of the lacquer, creating a sense of depth and realism. What makes this mask truly fascinating is its cultural significance. In Noh theater, masks play a crucial role in conveying emotions and character traits. The portrayal of Kojô symbolizes the wisdom and foresight associated with old age, offering a window into the rich theatrical traditions of Japan. Each detail, from the wrinkles on the forehead to the expression in the eyes, tells a story of the character's journey and experiences. This mask not only showcases the artistic mastery of the craftsman but also serves as a portal to the world of Noh theater, where performances blend music, dance, and drama to create captivating narratives. "Masque du vieillard kojô" stands as a testament to the enduring legacy of traditional Japanese artistry, inviting you to delve into the cultural tapestry of Japan's theatrical heritage.
