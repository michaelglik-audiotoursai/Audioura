Stop 1: L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.678403, 7.221684

Museum Information: Closed on Tuesday. 10:00–17:00 (1 Sep–30 Jun); 10:00–18:00 (1 Jul–31 Aug). FREE

You are about to embark on a journey through the Asian Art Museum of Nice, a tale woven with threads of dedication and cultural exchange. Inaugurated in 1998, this architectural marvel by Kenzo Tange stands as a testament to the mutual respect between civilizations. Each chapter of this experience reveals a new facet of this vibrant collection: from the ancient representation of the Buddha to Ganesh's dance, embodying the essence of Asian civilizations within these walls. As you explore Kannon's compassionate gaze and historical moments of East-West interaction, you'll uncover the stories and secrets that make this museum a jewel by the Mediterranean. This armor, this statue of Vishnu, each piece whispers of the passion and commitment that birthed this treasure trove of art.

As you gaze upon L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki, you are immediately struck by the craftsmanship and artistry that went into its creation. This armor, known as Armure de type dô-maru, dates back to the 19th century and exemplifies the exquisite skill of Japanese armorers during the Edo period. The steel plates are meticulously crafted and adorned with intricate designs in copper, accented by delicate silk lacing and luxurious gold leaf embellishments. One particular aspect that stands out is the choice of materials, especially the use of acier, or steel. The strength and durability of steel not only provided protection to the wearer but also served as a canvas for artistic expression. The combination of steel with copper, leather, silk, and gold leaf showcases the multi-faceted nature of armor-making during this period. This armor not only serves a practical purpose but also holds deep cultural significance. It symbolizes the mutual respect between different cultures and hints at the dedication and craftsmanship that characterized the Edo period in Japan. L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki is a testament to the fusion of art and functionality, offering a glimpse into the rich history and traditions of Japanese armor craftsmanship in the 19th century.

Directions: Next: La danse cosmique de Ganesh.



Stop 3: La danse cosmique de Ganesh

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

In the heart of the gallery, the exhibit "La danse cosmique de Ganesh" mesmerizes viewers with its depiction of Ganesh in a dynamic dance pose. Crafted from chlorite, this stunning sculpture captures Ganesh, the son of Shiva and Parvati, engaging in a cosmic dance that symbolizes wisdom and intellect. Each element of the sculpture is meticulously carved, showcasing the skill of the 10th-century artisans who created this piece. Ganesh's four arms hold significant objects — an axe representing detachment, a rope symbolizing liberation from illusion, a tusk used as a writing tool, and a sweetmeat signifying the rewards of a disciplined life. These symbolic items offer a glimpse into the rich cultural and spiritual significance embedded within this artwork. As you observe Ganesh's dance, you are transported back in time to an era where art served as a conduit for expressing deep philosophical concepts and beliefs. In the broader context of the museum's collection, "La danse cosmique de Ganesh" stands as a testament to the artistic mastery of the Bengale region. Its presence within the museum's walls not only showcases the diversity of Asian art but also highlights the profound connections between artistic expression and spiritual beliefs.

Stop 4: Kannon, le bodhisattva de la compassion

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

In the second half of the 12th century, a masterful creation emerged from the hands of skilled artisans in Japan. This work, crafted in bois, Carved from cypress wood, this statue stands as a testament to the spiritual and artistic traditions of that era. Kannon, embodied with eleven heads, sits gracefully upon a lotus, surrounded by a pierced mandorla that adds an ethereal quality to the composition. Each head of Kannon symbolizes a different aspect of compassion and mercy, reflecting the bodhisattva's role in alleviating suffering and guiding souls towards enlightenment. The choice of cypress wood not only showcases the artist's reverence for nature but also highlights the durability and symbolic significance of this material in Japanese art. As you gaze upon this remarkable piece, consider the cultural context of the Pala dynasty tradition in Bengal, where intricate sculptures like this were commissioned by Buddhist monasteries between the 8th and 12th centuries. The attention to detail in the carving of the statue's features and the elaborate ornamentation speaks to a time when craftsmanship was revered as a form of spiritual devotion. In the broader collection of the Asian Arts Museum, "Kannon, le bodhisattva de la compassion" stands out as a poignant representation of the museum's dedication to showcasing the diverse expressions of compassion and spirituality across different cultures. Just as La danse cosmique de Ganesh embodies the joy of creation, Kannon embodies the spirit of compassion that transcends time and cultural boundaries.

Stop 5: Ulysses Grant au Japon

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

As you gaze upon the woodblock print from 1879, a vivid scene unfolds before your eyes. The print depicts the momentous reception of Ulysses Grant, the President of the United States, and his wife at the Imperial Palace in Japan. The figures are elegantly attired in traditional Japanese garments, surrounded by a flurry of activity that conveys a sense of diplomatic ceremony. Chikanobu's technique of xylogravure polychrome on papier showcases a masterful use of color and composition. The rich hues and delicate lines bring life to the figures and setting, inviting you to immerse yourself in the historical narrative depicted. This piece serves as a poignant reminder of the cultural exchange between East and West during the Meiji era. The meeting between Grant and Japanese dignitaries symbolizes a moment of diplomatic significance, highlighting the intricate relationships that shaped international politics in the late 19th century. As you continue your journey through the museum, consider how this artwork bridges the gap between different cultures and epochs. The woodblock print "Ulysses Grant au Japon" stands as a testament to the power of art in capturing moments of historical significance and fostering cross-cultural understanding. 1879.
