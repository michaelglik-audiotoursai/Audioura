Stop 1: L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki

Address: 405 Promenade des Anglais, 06200 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.6676, 7.2189

Museum Information: Closed on Tuesday. 10:00–17:00 (1 Sep–30 Jun); 10:00–18:00 (1 Jul–31 Aug). FREE

You are about to embark on a journey through the interconnected chapters of Kenzo Tange's Architectural Influence, unraveling the enigmatic blend of tradition and modernity within the Asian Arts Museum. Inaugurated on October 16, 1998, this architectural marvel stands as a beacon of Asian culture, a testament to Tange's vision. Each stop on this tour reveals a different facet of his design mastery: from the harmonious fusion of Japanese armor within his structure to the cosmic dance of Ganesh brought to life through spatial design. As you delve deeper, you'll witness the serene presence of Buddha and the compassionate gaze of Kannon, all within a space designed to bridge cultural gaps. This journey promises to unveil the profound impact of Tange's architectural ingenuity on the museum's aesthetic and thematic coherence, inviting you to explore the soul of Asian artistry through his visionary lens.

Description:
L’Armure d’Andô Naoyuki, a masterpiece from the Edo period in the mid-19th century, is a true marvel to behold. Crafted from acier, cuivre, cuir, soie, laque, and feuille d’or, this armor exemplifies the fusion of artistry and functionality. As you gaze upon it, you will notice the delicate gold leaf embellishments that adorn the surface, reflecting the opulence of the era. What sets this armor apart is its distinctive design, featuring elements that speak to both tradition and innovation. The intricate metalwork, coupled with the use of luxurious materials such as silk and gold, highlights the importance of aesthetics in Japanese armor-making during this period. Each detail, from the ornate helmet to the protective chest plate, serves a dual purpose of protection and visual impact. Contextualizing this piece within the broader collection, one can appreciate the influence of traditional Japanese craftsmanship on contemporary design. The museum's architectural layout, designed by Kenzo Tange, mirrors the blend of tradition and modernity seen in this armor. As you explore further, consider how Tange's vision shapes the presentation of the museum's diverse collection, hinting at the harmonious coexistence of past and present in Asian art. Immerse yourself in the historical richness of this armor, a testament to the skilled artisans of the 19th century who crafted it with precision and artistry.
