Stop 2: Violes d'amour by Joannes Florenus Guidanti (Bologne, 1717)

Address: Palais Lascaris, 15 Rue Droite, 06300 Nice, France

As you stand before the Violes d'amour, you are transported back to the romantic era of the early 18th century. The instrument, meticulously crafted by Joannes Florenus Guidanti in Bologne in 1717, exudes a sense of passion and artistry that defined the musical landscape of that time. The viola d'amore itself is a work of art, with its delicate curves and ornate embellishments catching the soft light in the museum. Crafted from the finest materials available during that period, the Viola d'amour showcases Guidanti's mastery of woodworking and design. The intricate carvings on the body of the instrument depict scenes from mythology and nature, each detail carefully etched to enhance both the visual and auditory experience for the player and listener alike. This particular Viola d'amour is a testament to the skill and artistry of Guidanti, a renowned instrument maker of his time. The historical significance of this piece is heightened by its connection to the broader collection at Palais Lascaris, where multiple violas d'amore by Guidanti, Gagliano, Schorn, and Ott are displayed, showcasing the evolution of musical instrument craftsmanship over the centuries. The historical musical instrument collections of Nice were consolidated at Palais Lascaris in 2001, adding to the rich tapestry of musical history housed within these walls. As you gaze upon the Violes d'amour by Joannes Florenus Guidanti, you can't help but wonder how the melodies of the past continue to resonate through the corridors of this historic museum, keeping alive the artistry and emotions of bygone eras.
