Orientation: To fully appreciate "Au Soleil du Plafond," stand in front of the lithographs, allowing your gaze to sweep across the eleven vivid images. From this vantage point, the interplay between text and image becomes a palpable dance, revealing the subtleties of collaboration between artist and poet.

Gris, a key figure in the development of Cubism, worked closely with Reverdy, a poet known for his evocative and concise language. Together, they endeavored to create a livre d'artiste—a book that integrated visual art and poetry into a singular artistic expression. Passed away in 1927, Reverdy carried forward their unfinished vision, eventually publishing this scaled-back version, which features 11 lithographs printed on Arches paper. The publishing house Éditions Verve, known for its ambitious art projects, collaborated with Mourlot Frères, a distinguished Parisian printing firm. Reverdy's decision to complete and publish the work despite Gris's early death ensured that their shared vision reached the public. The vibrant palette and dynamic forms within each lithograph underscore Gris's mastery of Cubism, while Reverdy's text provides a lyrical counterpoint, deepening the thematic resonance of the work. The decision by the Museum of Fine Arts to include this rare piece, typically housed in archives, highlights the significance of such collaborative works in art history.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí crafted these illustrations to visually interpret Sigmund Freud’s provocative text "Moses and Monotheism," a detailed exploration of the origins of monotheism. Dalí's choice of sheepskin as a medium provides a tactile connection to the ancient world that Freud explored, elevating this set of ten drypoints and lithographs into artifacts of historical reverence. Freud's text was initially published by The Hogarth Press, a decision that highlights the collaborative nature of this endeavor—a cornerstone of the exhibition's thesis. Such works redefine how books can serve as art forms, integrating image and text in unified expression.
