Orientation: Standing beneath "Au Soleil du Plafond," you will find that the best vantage point is just to the left of the exhibit. From this position, the interplay between the lithographs and the poems becomes strikingly apparent, allowing the seamless integration of Juan Gris's visionary art with Pierre Reverdy's poetry to unfold.

In 1955, "Au Soleil du Plafond" emerged as the collaborative brainchild of the renowned Cubist artist Juan Gris and the influential French poet Pierre Reverdy. Gris and Reverdy crafted this livre d'artiste, exemplifying a harmonious fusion of visual and literary arts. Gris, celebrated for his innovative style, produced 11 lithographs that visually encapsulate the abstract essence of Reverdy's poems, thus transforming the book into a cohesive art form. Printed on Arches paper, known for its durability and fine texture, the lithographs captured the poetic nuances with precision. This endeavor aligns with the exhibition's thesis that the book can be an artwork in its own right, where every element—from the images to the typography—becomes integral to the artistic expression. Displayed in a space named for Torf, a Linde Family who understood the significance of artistic collaborations, this rare work enriches the museum's history of interwoven art forms. The legacy of "Au Soleil du Plafond" underscores the revolutionary spirit of its creators, as they championed the transformative potential of collaborative artistry.

Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism

Orientation: As you stand here, position yourself towards the display case containing Moses and Monotheism, where the intricate details of the illustrations by Salvador Dalí are best appreciated. Notice how the sheepskin captures both text and image, drawing you into a narrative that challenges conventional interpretations.

Sigmund Freud authored Moses and Monotheism, presenting a radical thesis that Moses was an Egyptian nobleman, perhaps a follower of Akhenaten. This assertion reshaped prevailing narratives of religious history, proposing a psychological exploration of monotheism. In 1974, Salvador Dalí crafted a set of 10 drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin to visualize Freud's controversial ideas. Dalí, known for his surreal imagery, transformed Freud's dense text into a visual odyssey. The choice of sheepskin material adds an ancient and tactile dimension, echoing the historical weight of Freud’s claims. This collaboration exemplifies the exhibition's thesis, highlighting how Dalí and Freud's interaction revolutionized the art form of the book. Such visionary transformations continue to provoke thought about how past narratives shape our current world.
