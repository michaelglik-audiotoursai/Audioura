Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3391, -71.0879

Orientation: As you stand here, face the delicate sheepskin pages of "Moses and Monotheism" to fully appreciate the convergence of image and text.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí illustrated a set of 10 drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin for Freud’s "Moses and Monotheism," giving his surrealist touch to Freud’s exploration of monotheistic origins. Freud's text proposed that Moses was not a Hebrew but an Egyptian, reimagining monotheistic roots and stirring intellectual debates. This set exemplifies the exhibition's argument that artists like Dalí were revolutionizing the book as an art form. By intertwining Dalí's visual interpretations with Freud's text, they crafted a piece that invites the audience to reconsider the cultural and psychological foundations of faith. The medium of sheepskin not only adds an ancient, tactile quality but also echoes the enduring complexities of monotheism and authority.
