Father Dalla Via, recognizing the growing needs of his congregation, invited Aldo to assist in parish activities. This partnership was more than practical; it signified a deep commitment to nurturing the parish’s mission of service. Under their leadership, the parish expanded its outreach programs, strengthening the bonds within this diverse community. The parish's history is further enriched by the architectural beauty visible around you. As you step away from Sacred Heart Parish, the echoes of its founding and the partnerships it fostered linger in the air. Stop 3: Mary Immaculate of Lourdes Parish

Address: 270 Elliot St, Newton, MA 02464

Coordinates: 42.3222, -71.2073

Type/Specialty: Catholic Church

Specific Examples: Impressive bell tower, intricate ceiling paintings, serene meditation room

Orientation: As you stand before the Mary Immaculate of Lourdes Parish, let your eyes trace the elegant lines of the building designed by architect Edward T. P. Graham. The structure's very bones tell a story, dating back to its cornerstone laid in 1909.

The Mary Immaculate of Lourdes Parish, originally established as St. Mary's Parish in 1870, holds the distinction of being the mother church of Newton and Needham. This parish has been a spiritual anchor in the region, founded during a time when Catholic communities were steadily growing in the area. The physical building you see, with its stately design by Graham, represents the flourishing of this community. Graham was known for his thoughtful, ecclesiastical designs, ensuring that each church was not just a place of worship but also a beacon of architectural grace. Another chapter in its narrative began with Father Charles Higgins, who was named pastor of Mary Immaculate of Lourdes Parish and administrator of St. Philip Neri Parish in Newton. His leadership marked a period of transformation and growth, as he deftly managed the needs of two communities, fostering a sense of unity and purpose among parishioners. Higgins' dual role highlighted the interconnectedness of the Catholic community in Newton, echoing the historical ties you noted at Sacred Heart Parish, which you visited earlier. The parish's significant history is not only rooted in its 1870 founding but also in its architectural and community developments over the years. As you pause here, the past and present intertwine, offering a moment to reflect on the enduring legacy of this sacred space. Ahead, the journey continues deeper into the spiritual heart of the neighborhood, promising further revelations.

Directions: As you leave Mary Immaculate of Lourdes Parish, walk east on Washington Street. Continue straight until you reach Watertown Street, then turn left. Walk a few blocks until you see Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church on your right. You've arrived!

Stop 4: Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church

Coordinates: 42.3250, -71.2096

Orientation: As you approach Our Lady Help of Christians Catholic Church in Newton, Massachusetts, the Gothic Revival architecture emerges prominently against the suburban landscape.

Built in 1881, its foundations were laid by Irish immigrants who faced immense hardships to erect a place of worship that would serve as a spiritual haven for generations. As you stand here, you can almost sense the layers of tradition that have imbued this space with life. The Italian immigrants who followed added their own customs and traditions, enriching the parish and weaving a diverse cultural tapestry that endures to this day. Father Crowley, who served as the third pastor from 2004 to 2010, played a pivotal role in guiding the church through a period of transformation. Under his leadership, the church navigated the challenges of modernity while preserving its historical essence. His efforts ensured that the church remained not only a place of worship but also a community hub, fostering connections among its parishioners. Crowley's tenure exemplified the delicate balance between honoring tradition and embracing change, a theme that resonates throughout the church's history. One of the most notable events in the church's recent history was the visit by Mother Teresa on June 15. Known for her humanitarian work and unwavering dedication to the poor, her presence brought an aura of grace and humility to the congregation. During her visit, she shared stories of her mission, inspiring many to engage more deeply with their faith and community service. Her visit left an indelible mark, reminding the parishioners of the power of compassion and the importance of serving others.
