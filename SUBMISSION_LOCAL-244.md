### What Was Done

**Root cause (D64):** The prolog is generated by a separate LLM call and injected into Stop 1 at assembly time (PHASE 6), AFTER all gates (R9, R10, style retry, CONTRADICTED block, subject routine) have already run on `poi['description']`. The prolog was the only generated text that bypassed every quality gate.

**Fix:** Added PHASE 5.9 (between prolog generation and assembly) that applies the existing gates to `_saved_prolog`:
1. R9 generic-sentence deletion
2. R10 unfulfilled-promise deletion
3. Subject routine (expand or remove promises)

Prolog findings are logged separately with `[LOCAL-244]` prefix, including before/after word counts and verbatim deletions.

### Limitations

1. **R10 context sensitivity:** When the prolog promises "hidden tales" and the following stop paragraph delivers (Monet, Fitzgerald, specific facts), `apply_r10_to_description` decides the promise is fulfilled and does not delete. This is R10 working as designed, but means some Michael-flagged language ("whispers of bygone eras") can survive if followed by delivery. The per-paragraph style validator still FLAGS it — the issue is R10's contextual pass/fail logic, not the gating position.

2. **Style flags (R1, R7) are not deletion rules.** The prolog still shows R1_IMPERATIVE and R7_HALLUCINATED_SENSORY in style analysis. These are flagged for style retry during generation (PHASE 5.1), but the prolog is generated separately and style retry doesn't cover it. A future task could add style retry to the prolog LLM call.

3. **Epilog (P6) also bypasses gates.** The `[EPILOG]` paragraph added during assembly (17 words, always R9_GENERIC) has the same structural problem. It was not in scope for LOCAL-244 but is the same fix.
