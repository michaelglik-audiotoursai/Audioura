--- 1. IT REJECTS THE TRUE SPECIFICS AND PASSES THE VAGUE FILLER -------------

Of Gemini's 30 sentences it flagged 5. Every one of the five carries real,

The 25 it PASSED include every sentence that says nothing checkable. This is
not a coincidence, it is the mechanism: the validator asks "does this
capitalized token appear in the source text?", so a sentence with no new names
and no new years cannot fail. THE VAGUER THE SENTENCE, THE SAFER IT IS.

That is also why GPT-4o's bland answer passed 10 of 11 sentences and this one
passes 25 of 30 while being far more informative. The gradient points at
exactly the output Michael rejected as "not a story at all".

--- 2. IT CANNOT SEE INVENTED PLACES, NUMBERS, OR REVERSALS ------------------

Probe, corpus = the stop text, which contains "1974" but not "Barcelona":

    GROUNDED   In 1974, Salvador Dali illustrated the work in Barcelona.
    GROUNDED   In 1974, Salvador Dali illustrated the work on the surface of
               the moon.
    GROUNDED   In 1974, Salvador Dali illustrated the work using 47 copper
               plates.
    GROUNDED   In 1974, Salvador Dali refused to illustrate the work.

An invented city, an absurd location, an invented count of plates, and a
REVERSED FACT all pass. It checks person-like capitalised names and years.
Places, numbers, materials, quantities and negation are unchecked.

So "TRUE_TO_SOURCES" means: contains no unfamiliar name or year. It does not
mean the sentence is true, and it never did.

It CAN fail — proven, it caught Napoleon Bonaparte, an invented 1802, and
Leonard Woolf. The failure is not that it never fires. It is WHAT it fires on.
