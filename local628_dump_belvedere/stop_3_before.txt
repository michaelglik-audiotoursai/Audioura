On November 2, 1938, German Foreign Minister Joachim von Ribbentrop and Italian Foreign Minister Galeazzo Ciano met at the Belvedere Palace to sign the First Vienna Award. Following directly upon the Munich Agreement's partition of Czechoslovakia, this arbitration redrew the borders of Central Europe on an ethnic basis. The decision forced Czechoslovakia to surrender more than 11,900 square kilometers of southern Slovakia and Carpathian Ruthenia to Hungary. The award’s decree was a poignant reminder of sovereignty and identity lost and regained. The First Vienna Award was more than just a diplomatic act; it was a pivot that influenced politics during a volatile period. Standing in this historic space, it becomes clear how moments like these have indelibly shaped the region's narrative, bringing us toward the subsequent events that would eventually lead to the reunifications and divisions echoing through to our own time.
