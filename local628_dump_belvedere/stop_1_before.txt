Orientation: Witness the transformation of Belvedere from a royal residence to a beacon of political reconciliation through these tangible symbols of history. Your first stop is Austrian State Treaty. Stand in the Marble Hall of the Upper Belvedere, where voices once echoed with the weight of a nation's future. This view, rich with history, places you at the heart of a momentous event: the signing of the Austrian State Treaty on May 15, 1955.

Following the end of World War II, France, the United Kingdom, the United States, and the Soviet Union divided and occupied Austria into four separate zones. For more than eight years, Cold War tensions stalled diplomatic efforts to reach an agreement. A breakthrough in Moscow in April 1955 finally cleared the way, and on May 15, 1955, representatives from the four Allied powers and Austria signed the Austrian State Treaty at Belvedere Palace. Their actions ended a decade-long occupation, officially recognizing Austria as an independent and democratic state once again. The Belvedere, traditionally a locus for art and culture, became a symbol of newfound sovereignty on that spring day. Its choice as the venue underscored its importance; the grandeur of Johann Lucas von Hildebrandt’s Baroque architecture lent gravity to the proceedings. Within its walls, the treaty stipulated the withdrawal of foreign troops, a move that restored freedom and autonomy to the Austrian people. This decision was not without its wider geopolitical implications. The Federal People's Republic of Yugoslavia later acceded to this treaty, highlighting the document's reach and significance. It was a diplomatic achievement that extended beyond Austria's borders, influencing Eastern Europe's political landscape. As you stand in this hall, imagine the palpable sense of hope and relief that enveloped Austria that day—a nation stepping out from the shadows of war into the light of self-determination.
