Orientation: Stand directly in front of the monumental bronze sculpture "Appeal to the Great Spirit" by Cyrus Edwin Dallin to fully appreciate its grandeur and spiritual presence.

As you gaze upon "Appeal to the Great Spirit," you are met with a powerful depiction of a Native American on horseback, facing the sky with arms outstretched in a spiritual plea to the Great Spirit. One notable aspect of this sculpture is Dallin's choice of bronze as the material for this work. The bronze casting not only ensures the durability and longevity of the sculpture but also adds a sense of permanence and timelessness to the spiritual message it conveys. Adding to the significance of the piece is the historical and cultural context in which it was created. In the early 20th century, during a period of contentious debates and cultural shifts, Dallin's representation of a Native American figure in a moment of spiritual communion was a bold statement of recognition and respect for indigenous cultures and beliefs. This iconic sculpture, located in front of the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, serves as a poignant reminder of the importance of acknowledging and honoring diverse histories and spiritual traditions. Its presence not only enriches the museum's collection but also prompts viewers to reflect on the complexities and interconnectedness of cultural heritage and spirituality within the broader context of art and history. This spirit of cultural inclusion and representation, embodied by "Appeal to the Great Spirit," resonates throughout the museum, encouraging visitors to explore other artworks and exhibitions that similarly celebrate diversity and creativity across various cultures and time periods.
