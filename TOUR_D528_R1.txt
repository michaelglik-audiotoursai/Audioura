Art publisher Louis Broder commissioned Joan Miró to create the illustrated book project *Le Lézard aux plumes d’or*. For this work, Miró paired his own poetic writing with original color lithographs printed by Mourlot in Paris. Broder published the complete portfolio edition in Paris in 1971. Over years of dedication, this collaboration resulted in the 1971 creation of Le Lézard aux plumes d’or, an illustrated book featuring an impressive 40 color lithographs. Boris Fridman later donated this work to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, enriching its collection and bringing greater recognition to the art of the printed book. In 1967, Joan Miró and Paris publisher Louis Broder set out to produce the illustrated book *Le Lézard aux plumes d’or*. After the first print run, Miró and Broder discovered defects in the paper that caused the colors of the lithographs to deteriorate. The project was canceled, and an order was given to destroy the defective suites, though surviving prints left it uncertain whether every copy was destroyed. Broder and Miró resumed work on the book and ultimately published a new edition with entirely different compositions in 1971. The book, bound in publisher’s vellum, exemplifies the exhibition's thesis that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative ventures. The vivid lithographs, printed dance across the pages, their colors resonating with the themes of transformation and renewal. Miró's choice of medium, wove paper, allows the colors to absorb and reflect light in captivating ways, enhancing the viewer's experience of each lithograph. This work, gifted by Boris Fridman, the collector who gave this work to the museum, highlights the significance of collaboration in the art world, mirroring the efforts of Juan Gris and Pierre Reverdy in their own livres d'artiste.
