Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Stand directly in front of the open display case showcasing the 1974 illustrations by Salvador Dalí for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." From this vantage point, the intricate details of Dalí's surreal interpretation of Moses and the concept of monotheism come into sharp focus, inviting contemplation and reflection on the radical themes explored in this collaborative work.

In a captivating fusion of image and text, Dalí's illustrations for Freud's psychoanalytic study delve into the complexities of Moses' identity and the evolution of monotheistic beliefs. Each illustration masterfully captures Dalí's unique artistic vision, blending symbolism, surrealism, and psychological depth to challenge traditional notions of faith and leadership. The work reshapes perceptions of religious history and cultural narratives through vivid imagery and intricate compositions. This exhibit showcases the intersection of art and literature, demonstrating the collaborative nature of creativity as Dalí's illustrations bring visual life to Freud's groundbreaking text.
