Joan Miró's "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" is a captivating illustrated book featuring 40 color lithographs on publisher’s vellum. Each plate showcases Miró's unique interpretation of the metamorphosis of a lizard into a mythical creature, blurring the lines between reality and fantasy. The choice of color lithography enhances the dreamlike quality of the imagery, inviting viewers into Miró's surreal world. Published and printed by Mourlot Frères, this work exemplifies the collaborative nature of artistic creation, where Miró's vision was brought to fruition through the expertise of skilled professionals. Gifted to the museum this piece stands as a testament to Miró's creativity and innovation in the realm of illustrated art.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: Museum of Fine Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Position yourself directly in front of Salvador Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." Stand at a distance that allows you to take in the intricate details of the illustrations and appreciate the fusion of image and text on display.

Each illustration intricately depicts scenes from Freud's exploration of the origins of monotheism. Dalí's unique style, characterized by surrealistic imagery and meticulous detail, brings to life the deep symbolism embedded in Freud's text. The choice of illustrations on sheepskin adds a tactile dimension to the viewing experience, inviting visitors to engage with both the visual and tactile aspects of the work. Against the backdrop of Freud's provocative narrative, Dalí's illustrations offer a compelling visual interpretation of the complex themes of cultural and religious transformation. The collaboration between Freud and Dalí in this project underscores the intersection of psychology, art, and theology, challenging viewers to contemplate the profound implications of monotheism's emergence.
