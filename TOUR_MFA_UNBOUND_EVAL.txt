Upon viewing "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," you will be captivated by the vibrant fusion of poetry and sculptural elements that Joan Miró expertly combined in 1971. The illustrated book, featuring 15 lithographs on publisher’s vellum, highlights the artist's exploration of metamorphosis and illusion. Miró's deliberate use of 40 color lithographs enhances the surreal quality of the piece, immersing you in a world of fantasy and transformation. Louis Broder, the visionary publisher behind this project, commissioned Miró as part of a deliberate campaign to revive the livre d'artiste tradition after the war. Broder's dedication to small editions, rarely exceeding 150 copies, ensured direct collaboration between artist, poet, and Mourlot Frères at the same workshop. This collaboration with Mourlot Frères, the renowned printer, elevated the artistic form of the book to new heights, setting a precedent for future collaborative ventures in the genre. Boris Fridman, a Boston-based collector with a passion for livres d'artiste, generously donated this work to the Museum of Fine Arts in 2003. Fridman's gift expanded the museum's holdings of Surrealist-era printed works, adding a critical mass to the collection and preserving the legacy of artistic collaboration for future generations to appreciate.

Orientation: Position yourself at the center of the room, directly in front of Salvador Dalí's illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." This will allow you to fully appreciate the intricate details and symbolism embedded in each of the ten engravings on sheepskin.

In 1974, Dalí's illustrations for Freud's seminal work were published as a set of 10, each crafted meticulously on sheepskin. The choice of material adds a layer of richness and texture to the visual narrative, inviting viewers to engage more deeply with the religious and psychological themes explored in the book. The decision to commission Dalí for this set of illustrations advanced the collaborative book form that the exhibition celebrates. Broder's keen eye for fostering direct collaboration between artist and Mourlot Frères resulted in a series of works that seamlessly integrate image and text, elevating the book as an art form. Boris Fridman, a Boston collector specializing in livres d'artiste, later donated this set to the MFA, enriching the museum's collection with collaborative printed works of significant cultural and artistic value.

"Au Soleil du Plafond" is a mesmerizing livre d'artiste that transports you to a realm where light and shadow play tricks on the eyes. Gris, known for his bold compositions, and Reverdy, a master of poetic prose, joined forces to create a piece that blurs the lines between art and literature. The collaboration between Gris and Reverdy, along with the skilled printing by Mourlot, resulted in a masterpiece that embodies the essence of the livre d'artiste ideal. Through this work, the exhibition's argument that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through deep collaboration is vividly exemplified. Pierre Reverdy, a visionary poet, collaborated with Juan Gris, creating a fusion of words and celestial visual interpretations.
