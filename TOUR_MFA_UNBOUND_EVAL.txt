This allows you to appreciate the intricate interplay between Joan Miró's vibrant lithographs and the soft texture of the Louis Broder’s vellum, a material choice that enhances the visual and tactile experience of this remarkable book.

"Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," created in 1971, is a triumph of collaboration. Louis Broder, known for his dedication to the livre d'artiste tradition, commissioned Miró to craft this portfolio, which consists of 40 color lithographs. Broder's vision was to produce editions where the artist, poet, and Mourlot Frères worked closely together, embodying the exhibition's thesis that the book itself is the artwork. The work's journey continued when Boris Fridman, a passionate collector of such artist's books, donated it to the Museum Boston. Through the vibrant hues and mythical imagery of Miró's lithographs, a simple lizard transforms into a creature of legend, challenging viewers to see beyond reality.

Orientation: Display of Moses and Monotheism is and let your gaze travel from the intricate illustrations to the binding of the book.

In 1939, Sigmund Freud published Moses and Monotheism through The Hogarth Press, presenting a radical psychoanalytic examination of religious transformation. His text delves into the origins of monotheism, suggesting that Moses, an Egyptian priest, introduced this revolutionary concept to the Israelites, altering the course of history. It was this provocative narrative that caught the attention of Salvador Dalí decades later. The book exemplifies the exhibition's thesis, showcasing how artists like Dalí revolutionized the book as an artwork through collaboration. His illustrations, vibrant and surreal, do not merely complement Freud’s words; they reinterpret and expand them, making the book a multi-sensory experience. Yet here, they are unbound, inviting contemplation on how modern artists continue to reflect profound transformations in today's world.
