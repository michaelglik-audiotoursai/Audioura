From this vantage point, the vibrant colors and fantastical imagery leap off the pages, inviting you to explore every corner of Miró's creation.

Miró's use of publisher’s vellum as the material choice adds a unique texture to the illustrations, enhancing the magical quality of the imagery. Louis Broder's deliberate decision to commission Miró for this edition was a pivotal moment in the revival of the livre d'artiste tradition post-war. Broder's commitment to producing limited editions with direct collaboration between artist, poet, and Mourlot Frères at the same workshop in Paris ensured that each copy was a masterpiece of artistic synergy. Boris Fridman, a Boston collector with a passion for livres d'artiste, recognized the significance of this work and generously gifted it to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, enriching the museum's collection of collaborative printed works.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Salvador Dalí's illustrations for Sigmund Freud's provocative work is "Moses and Monotheism," in the Picasso, Miró, Dalí: Unbound exhibition at MFA, Boston. Stand back slightly to take in the full scope of the illustrations, revealing intricate details that may go unnoticed up close.

Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Freud's groundbreaking text on the origins of monotheism are a set of 10 delicately executed pieces on sheepskin. They capture the essence of Freud's controversial theory in a visually compelling manner. Each illustration not only depicts the narrative but also delves into the psychological depth of the subject matter. One must understand the historical and cultural context of Freud's theory, challenging conventional beliefs about the beginnings of monotheistic faith. This work exemplifies the collaborative nature of the livre d'artiste ideal, where Dalí's illustrations seamlessly intertwine with Freud's text. The decision to bring together two titans of their respective fields, under the imprint of The Hogarth Press, advances the notion of the book as a unified artwork, where image and text intersect to create a thought-provoking narrative. As you continue to explore the exhibition, consider how artistic interpretations such as these may offer resolutions to the age-old tensions surrounding faith, identity, and human psychology.

Stop 3: Au Soleil du Plafond

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: As you stand in the midst of the Picasso, Miro, Dali: Unbound exhibition at MFA, Boston, MA, let your gaze drift towards the intriguing "Au Soleil du Plafond" by Juan Gris and Pierre Reverdy.

Story:
Juan Gris, a prominent figure in the Cubist movement, collaborated with Pierre Reverdy on this mesmerizing livre d'artiste, pushing the boundaries of artistic expression. Their decision to work together resulted in a groundbreaking fusion of image and text, where each element complements and enhances the other. Pierre Reverdy, a masterful writer, collaborated on the project, blending his poetic prowess with Gris's visual creations. The limited edition of 220 impressions, meticulously printed by Mourlot, adds exclusivity and rarity to this artistic gem. Each impression embodies Gris and Reverdy's creative vision, inviting exploration into their collaborative genius. The integration of image and text in a single sheet showcases the seamless collaboration between publisher Éditions Verve and Mourlot Frères. This work not only embodies the transformative power of collaboration but also serves as a testament to the enduring legacy of Gris and Reverdy's artistic partnership.
