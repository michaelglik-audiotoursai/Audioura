Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Position yourself directly in front of Salvador Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Sigmund Freud's Moses and Monotheism. The intricate details on sheepskin was created in a set of 10, as a testament to the collaboration between Dalí and Freud.

The illustrations not only visually depict the narrative but also serve as a visual interpretation of Freud's provocative ideas about Moses and his place in history.

Orientation: The lithographs on Arches paper, totaling 11 in count, are a testament to the artistic collaboration between Gris and Reverdy. Dive into this treasure trove of visual and textual marvels without hesitation.

Tucked within the exhibit, "Au Soleil du Plafond" showcases the intricate fusion of artistry and poetry found within its pages. Gris and Reverdy's collaboration is evident in the lithographs, each meticulously crafted on Arches paper by Mourlot Frères, a renowned French lithography workshop. The choice of material enhances the tactile experience, allowing for immersion in the visual richness of the pages. Contextually, this work stands as a prime example of the livre d'artiste genre, where art and text merge seamlessly to create a holistic experience for the viewer. The interplay between Gris's visual storytelling and Reverdy's poetic prowess adds layers of depth and meaning to each lithograph, inviting you to unravel the hidden narratives woven into the pages. This collaboration transcends mere illustration, offering a harmonious blend of artistic expression and literary finesse. As you delve into "Au Soleil du Plafond," ponder the profound impact of artistic partnerships like Gris and Reverdy's on the world of art. The convergence of symbols, techniques, and narratives within this work leaves a lasting impression, showcasing the transformative power of collaborative creativity. Explore each lithograph with a discerning eye, savoring the intricate details and nuances that reveal themselves with every turn of the page.
