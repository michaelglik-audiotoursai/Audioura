Stand in front of "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers)" at the Picasso, Miro, Dali: Unbound exhibition at MFA, Boston. From this vantage point, you'll witness a fantastical creature come to life through vivid colors and intricate details that reveal Joan Miró's surreal imagination at its finest.

Each page of this illustrated book showcases Miró's unique artistic vision, transforming a simple lizard into a whimsical creature adorned with golden feathers. The vibrant hues and whimsical shapes dance across the pages, inviting you into Miró's playful yet profound world. One striking aspect of this work is Miró's choice to depict the lizard with golden feathers, a departure from traditional representations of this creature. Embedded within the pages of "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" is a subtle nod to Catalan folklore, where lizards are symbolic of regeneration and renewal. This piece not only showcases Miró's artistic prowess but also highlights the collaborative efforts of Louis Broder, the publisher, and Mourlot Frères, the printer, who brought this masterpiece to life. Gifted to the museum this work stands as a testament to Miró's enduring legacy and his ability to transform the ordinary into the extraordinary.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Stand directly in front of Salvador Dalí's illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism" at the Picasso, Miró, Dalí: Unbound exhibition at MFA, Boston. Look closely at the intricate details on sheepskin to witness the fusion of image and text.

The choice of material adds a layer of richness and depth to the illustrations, inviting viewers to explore the intersection of image and text in a new light. These illustrations not only showcase Dalí's artistic prowess but also offer a fresh perspective on Freud's exploration of Moses and monotheism. By visually interpreting Freud's text, Dalí brings a contemporary twist to a historical subject, inviting viewers to ponder the complexities of faith, identity, and belief systems across centuries. This exhibit exemplifies the power of collaboration between artist and author, highlighting the seamless integration of visual and textual elements in a thought-provoking manner.

Orientation: From this vantage point, you can admire the mesmerizing presence of "Au Soleil du Plafond" by Juan Gris and collaborator Pierre Reverdy. This unique piece, created in 1955, is a true gem of the collaboration between these two artistic visionaries.

Gazing at "Au Soleil du Plafond," you will be captivated by the intricate details of this one-of-a-kind edition. Produced by Mourlot Frères, a renowned lithographic printing company in Paris, and Éditions Verve, this work is a stunning example of the intersection of image, text, and typography, blurring the lines between visual art and literature. The 220 impressions printed by Mourlot add a sense of exclusivity to this artistic marvel, inviting you to delve deeper into the world of Gris and Reverdy's creative synergy. This piece stands as a testament to the power of collaboration in the realm of livre d'artiste, where each impression tells a unique story, waiting to be explored. The ethereal essence of "Au Soleil du Plafond" envelops you, prompting contemplation about the transformative nature of art and its enduring impact on the world.
