Your first stop is Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers). The vivid colors and intricate details come to life from this viewpoint, allowing you to fully immerse yourself in the surreal world depicted by the artist.

Upon viewing "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," you enter a realm where reality and imagination blend seamlessly. Created in 1971, this masterpiece is an entire illustrated book with 40 color lithographs, including a striking cover featuring publisher’s vellum material. Each lithograph showcases Miró's expertise in color and form, offering a visual feast for the eyes. Miró's exploration of surrealism is evident in the whimsical depiction of a lizard adorned with golden feathers, a symbol of transformation and the fantastical. The vibrant hues and fluid lines guide your eyes across the composition, inviting you to delve deeper into the artist's creative vision. The choice of lithography as the medium adds a layer of depth and texture to the artwork, enhancing the overall viewing experience. Louis Broder, the publisher, played a pivotal role in bringing Miró's artistic vision to life, while Mourlot Frères, the printer, ensured the precision and quality of each lithograph. The generous donation of this work to the museum further enriches its cultural significance. The vibrant colors and intricate details of this enchanting artwork reveal myriad interpretations and narratives.

Stop 2: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Position yourself directly in front of Salvador Dalí's 1974 illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism".

As you stand before Dalí's illustrations, you are greeted by a convergence of art and literature. The pages come alive with Dalí's surrealist style, depicting scenes that challenge traditional interpretations of history and myth. Each stroke of Dalí's pen reveals a deep connection to Freud's controversial narrative, exploring the psychological impact of myth-making. The medium of illustration allows for a unique intersection of image and text, emphasizing the collaborative nature of this artistic endeavor. One cannot help but be drawn into the world of Moses and Monotheism, where Dalí's visual storytelling transcends the boundaries of conventional art forms. Through this work, Dalí and Freud beckon us to question the constructs of culture and religion, provoking introspection and contemplation on the enduring power of myth in shaping human consciousness.

Orientation: As you stand amidst the vibrant energy of the Picasso, Miro, Dali: Unbound exhibition at the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston, a hidden gem awaits your discovery. Direct your gaze towards "Au Soleil du Plafond," a captivating creation that transcends boundaries.

Nestled among the other works, "Au Soleil du Plafond" stands out not for its size or grandeur, but for its intriguing fusion of visual and textual elements. Each page of this book reveals a harmonious blend of lithographs and handwritten text, showcasing the artistic synergy between Gris and Reverdy. The fusion of color, form, and language in "Au Soleil du Plafond" invites you to delve deeper into the rich tapestry of modern art and literature, blurring the lines between visual and literary expression.
