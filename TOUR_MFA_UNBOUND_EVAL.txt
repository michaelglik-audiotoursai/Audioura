In the heart of the exhibition, "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" captivates viewers with its fantastical imagery and innovative artistic techniques. Created by Joan Miró in 1971, this illustrated book features 40 color lithographs on publisher’s vellum, a material that adds a luxurious sheen to each page. Miró's collaboration with Louis Broder, a French art publisher known for collaborations, and the expertise of Mourlot Frères in printing brought this masterpiece to life, showcasing a unique fusion of poetry, printmaking, and sculptural elements. As part of the museum's collection, "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" represents a significant advancement in the livre d'artiste tradition, underscoring the deep collaborative efforts between artists, publishers, and printers during this era. Miró's creation of this illustrated book not only exemplifies the artist's boundless creativity but also highlights the revolutionary impact of deeply collaborative ventures in redefining the boundaries of artistic expression within the realm of the book as an integral artwork. Gifted to the Museum of Fine Arts by Boris Fridman, known for contributions to the art community, this extraordinary piece stands as a testament to Miró's artistic vision and the transformative power of collaborative artistic endeavors.
