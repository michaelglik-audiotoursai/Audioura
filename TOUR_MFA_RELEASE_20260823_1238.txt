In 1971, Louis Broder took the decisive step of publishing Joan Miró's illustrated book "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or" (The Lizard with Golden Feathers). This action brought to life a limited edition portfolio featuring forty vibrant lithographs, showcasing Miró's unique artistic vision. The book, bound in the exquisite texture of publisher’s vellum, is a prime example of how the medium of lithography, particularly when printed by Mourlot Frères, can transform an illustrated book into an object of artistic expression. Miró's work exemplifies the exhibition's argument that artists revolutionized the book as an art form through deeply collaborative ventures. This collaboration between Miró, Broder as the publisher, and the skilled printers at Mourlot Frères highlights the synergy required to elevate the livre d'artiste beyond mere pages and into a vibrant narrative. The vivid colors and dynamic forms within each lithograph suggest a world where myth meets modernity, inviting viewers to ponder the possibilities of transformation inherent in Miró's vision.

Address: Avenue of the Arts, 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Orientation: Here, the interplay of images and text in "Au Soleil du Plafond" reveals itself fully, allowing Gris and Reverdy's collaboration to envelop you in their final artistic dialogue.

In the 1910s, Juan Gris began working on "Au Soleil du Plafond," a book envisioned in collaboration with the poet Pierre Reverdy. Yet, Gris passed away in 1927, leaving the project incomplete, with only half of the intended work finished. The renowned printer Mourlot Frères, a famous lithographic printing company in Paris, brought the book to life, utilizing lithographic techniques to merge image and text seamlessly. This collaboration between Gris and Reverdy exemplifies the exhibition's thesis that books can be revolutionary artworks, melding visual and literary forms into one cohesive piece.

In 1974, Salvador Dalí embarked on a significant artistic endeavor by creating illustrations for Sigmund Freud's "Moses and Monotheism." This project marked a shift for Dalí, who engaged deeply with Freud's controversial theories on religion and history. Dalí translated Freud's exploration of monotheism into visual form, providing a unique surrealist lens on the psychoanalyst's final major work. The Hogarth Press, known for publishing avant-garde literature, printed Freud's original text.
