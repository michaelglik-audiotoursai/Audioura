Louis Broder, a French publisher of illustrated books, commissioned Miró in 1971 to revive the tradition of the illustrated book. In 1974, Salvador Dalí crafted ten drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin as a response to Sigmund Freud’s last publication, "Moses and Monotheism." Freud, the pioneering psychoanalyst, authored this provocative text that reimagines Moses as an Egyptian nobleman, challenging traditional narratives. Dalí's illustrations enhance this text, offering a surrealistic interpretation that aligns with Freud’s radical theories. The decision to create this work in a limited edition of ten copies highlights the exclusivity and significance of the collaboration. Published by The Hogarth Press, this set embodies the exhibition’s thesis that artist-author collaborations revolutionized the book form, treating it as a comprehensive artwork. This legacy is echoed throughout the exhibition, inviting reflection on transformative ideas that continue to resonate today, much like Boston’s historic revolutionary spirit.
