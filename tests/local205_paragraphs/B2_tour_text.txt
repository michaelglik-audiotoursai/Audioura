Stop 1: Nu bleu IV

Address: 164 Avenue des Arènes de Cimiez, 06000 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.7196, 7.2755

Museum Information: Closed on Tuesday. 10:00–17:00 (1 Nov–31 Mar); 10:00–18:00 (1 Apr–31 Oct). €12; free for Métropole residents

You are about to embark on a journey through the interconnected chapters of Henri Matisse's Lasting Legacy at the Musée Matisse in Nice. Step into the seventeenth-century Villa des Arènes, where Matisse's revolutionary spirit comes to life. Witness the vibrant hues and bold forms of 'Nu bleu IV,' a masterpiece from his personal collection, symbolizing his innovative approach to art. As you delve deeper, discover how Matisse's heirs continue his artistic lineage, intertwining nature and imagination in pieces like 'Nature morte à la statuette africaine.' Join us as we uncover the profound impact of Matisse's personal and familial contributions, shaping this museum into a testament of his enduring influence.

Nu bleu IV, created in 1952, is a captivating female nude rendered in blue gouache-covered paper cut and pasted on white Canson paper, mounted on a vertical canvas. This piece is one of four Blue Nudes by Matisse and holds a special place in his oeuvre as it was the first Blue Nude he started and the last one he completed. The choice of blue for the figure's form exudes a sense of tranquility and contemplation, contrasting against the stark white background that enhances the figure's presence. Matisse's revolutionary approach to art is evident in Nu bleu IV through his daring use of color and form. The simplicity of the composition belies the complexity of emotion and movement evoked by the figure's posture and the interplay of light and shadow. This work reflects Matisse's mastery in capturing the essence of the human form through abstraction and stylization, pushing the boundaries of traditional representation. Placed in the context of the Musée Matisse's collection, Nu bleu IV stands as a testament to the artist's innovative spirit and enduring legacy. As part of the museum's holdings since 1989, this piece not only showcases Matisse's artistic evolution but also serves as a bridge to the broader exploration of modern art within the museum's exhibitions. Immerse yourself in the vibrant hues and dynamic composition of Nu bleu IV, experiencing firsthand the brilliance of Matisse's artistic vision. Discover how this iconic work paved the way for new possibilities in the world of art, setting the stage for the ongoing evolution of the Musée Matisse in celebrating innovation and creativity.

Stop 2: Nymphe dans la forêt

Address: 164 Avenue des Arènes de Cimiez, 06000 Nice, France

Step into the ethereal realm of Henri Matisse's "Nymphe dans la forêt," a masterful creation born between 1936 and 1938. As you gaze upon the canvas, a mystical scene unfolds before your eyes: a nude nymph, bathed in soft hues, reclines in a lush forest setting. Her serene presence is juxtaposed by the approach of a satyr, his form dynamic and captivating in its movement. Matisse's choice of oil on canvas lends a rich texture to the work, enhancing the depth and vibrancy of the colors. The artist's skillful brushwork delicately captures the play of light filtering through the foliage, creating a sense of dappled sunlight dancing across the forest floor. This painting, with its mythological subject matter, delves into the timeless themes of nature, beauty, and the interplay between reality and imagination. The juxtaposition of the graceful nymph and the enigmatic satyr invites contemplation on the complexities of desire and the eternal dance between the natural world and the supernatural. As you stand before "Nymphe dans la forêt," consider the profound influence of Matisse's work, where the boundaries between reality and fantasy blur. This piece, generously donated by Matisse's heirs, not only embodies the artist's legacy but also serves as a testament to his enduring impact on the art world.



From Nu bleu IV to Nymphe dans la forêt, you have followed the thread of Henri Matisse's Lasting Legacy. In 1989, the archaeological museum was moved to the nearby ancient site of the city, allowing the Musée Matisse to be expanded.
