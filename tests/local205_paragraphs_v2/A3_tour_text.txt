Stop 1: Nymphe dans la forêt

Address: 164 Avenue des Arènes de Cimiez, 06000 Nice, France

Coordinates: 43.7196, 7.2755

Museum Information: Closed on Tuesday. 10:00–17:00 (1 Nov–31 Mar); 10:00–18:00 (1 Apr–31 Oct). €12; free for Métropole residents

You are about to embark on a captivating journey through the chapters of Henri Matisse's legacy at the Musée Matisse, housed within the enchanting Villa des Arènes in Cimiez. Opened in 1963, this museum underwent a transformative period during a four-year closure, reopening in 1993 with modern wings and renewed spaces. As you delve into the narrative, discover how Matisse's artistic evolution is captured in his early works, setting the stage for the museum's diverse collection. The climax awaits as you explore the profound influence of Nice's landscape on Matisse's artistry, embodied in a piece directly donated by the maestro himself. Unravel the mystery of the museum's evolution over time, each chapter unveiling a new facet of Matisse's enduring impact through his generous donations.

Created between 1936 and 1938, "Nymphe dans la forêt" transports you into a realm where myth and reality intertwine. The painting portrays a naked nymph, bathed in soft light, reclining in a lush forest setting. As your eyes traverse the canvas, you will notice Matisse's deliberate use of color and form to evoke a sense of ethereal beauty and tranquility. The nymph is approached by a satyr, adding a touch of mystery and intrigue to the scene. One striking aspect of this artwork is Matisse's bold brushwork and expressive use of color. The rich hues of green and earth tones create a harmonious backdrop against which the figures come to life. Matisse's skillful technique in capturing the play of light and shadow infuses the painting with a sense of movement and depth, drawing you deeper into the narrative. To truly appreciate "Nymphe dans la forêt," it is essential to consider the broader context of Matisse's evolving style during this period. This early work serves as a precursor to the artist's later explorations in form and color, laying the groundwork for the innovative approach that would define his oeuvre. As you gaze upon this masterpiece, consider the pivotal changes that Matisse's presence in Nice brought to the Musée Matisse. How did his artistic legacy shape the collection housed within these walls, and what new perspectives did his work introduce to the world of art?

Stop 2: Tempête à Nice

Address: 164 Avenue des Arènes de Cimiez, 06000 Nice, France

In 1919-1920, Matisse captured the essence of a storm at the seaside in Nice through "Tempête à Nice." This masterpiece, an oil on canvas, showcases the artist's bold brushwork and keen observation of the natural world. The tumultuous waves crashing against the shore convey a sense of movement and urgency, while the dark, brooding sky hints at the power of nature. Notably, this painting represents Matisse's deep connection to the landscape of Nice, where he found inspiration in the interplay of light, color, and form. The dramatic composition reflects the artist's fascination with the transient and ever-changing aspects of nature, a theme that permeates much of his later work. As you gaze upon "Tempête à Nice," consider the influence of Nice's landscape on Matisse's artistic vision. The stormy scene captures a fleeting moment in time, frozen in the artist's bold strokes and vibrant hues. This direct donation by Matisse himself in 1960 holds a special place in the museum's collection, offering visitors a glimpse into the intersection of nature's raw power and artistic interpretation. This captivating artwork not only stands as a testament to Matisse's mastery but also serves as a window into the evolving artistic landscape of the early 20th century. The museum's curation of Matisse's works, including this powerful piece, highlights the artist's enduring legacy and his profound impact on modern art. As you continue your journey through the museum, ponder how Matisse's vision evolved over time and explore the ways in which his art continues to resonate with audiences worldwide.



From Nymphe dans la forêt to Tempête à Nice, you have followed the thread of The Museum's Evolution Over Time. In 1989, the archaeological museum was moved to the nearby ancient site of the city, allowing the Musée Matisse to be expanded.
