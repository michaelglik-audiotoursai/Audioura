Step-by-step audio guided tour of the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam.

Stop 1: Agostina Segatori Sitting in the Cafe du Tambourin

Address: Museumplein 6, 1071 DJ Amsterdam, Netherlands

Coordinates: 52.3594, 4.8838

Orientation: The venue, dedicated to the works of Vincent van Gogh and his contemporaries, stands as a testament to the artist's profound influence on art history. Your first stop is Agostina Segatori Sitting in the Cafe du Tambourin. Position yourself a few steps back from Vincent van Gogh’s “Agostina Segatori Sitting in the Café du Tambourin,” where you can fully appreciate the interplay of textures and patterns that characterize the scene. This vantage point allows you to see the vibrant tapestry of Japanese ukiyo-e woodblock prints adorning the wall behind Agostina, a detail that encapsulates Van Gogh's infatuation with Eastern art.

Agostina Segatori, a former artist's model who had posed for Edgar Degas, ran the Café du Tambourin in Paris. Vincent van Gogh chose this café as the setting for his painting, making an intentional nod to his personal connection with Segatori and his broader cultural interactions. Agostina’s presence is central in the work; her enigmatic expression suggests a mix of resilience and introspection.

Van Gogh was deeply engaged in the Parisian avant-garde scene during this period, which was marked by his brief romantic relationship with Segatori. Their affair, which lasted only a few months in 1887, granted him intimate insights into the life of her café, a hub for artists like himself. Vincent and his brother Theo also integrated a collection of Japanese ukiyo-e prints into the café's decor as an exhibit. This was not merely decorative, but a way to align themselves with the flourishing Japonisme trend that captivated artists in Europe at the time.

When their relationship ended, Segatori's café went bankrupt, leading to an immediate clash over the artwork inside. The painting offers a window into Van Gogh's Parisian life, reflecting how interpersonal relationships and artistic influences intertwined to shape his evolving legacy. Today, at the Van Gogh Museum, this canvas is celebrated as a vivid snapshot of Van Gogh's Parisian period, inviting viewers to explore the unique blend of Western and Eastern influences that informed his work. The painting not only narrates a moment in Van Gogh's life but also serves as an example of how personal connections inspired his artistic endeavors.

Directions: Continue through Van Gogh Museum — next is The Painter of Sunflowers.

Stop 2: The Painter of Sunflowers

Address: Museumplein 6, 1071 DJ Amsterdam, Netherlands

Coordinates: 52.3594, 4.8838

Orientation: Stand before "The Painter of Sunflowers," and observe from a vantage where the light draws out the depth and warmth of Gauguin's palette.

1888, within the intimate confines of the Yellow House in Arles, Paul Gauguin painted this portrait during a critical phase of collaboration with Vincent van Gogh. The work, known as "The Painter of Sunflowers," reflects a moment of intense artistic synergy between the two artists. Gauguin’s brushstrokes are both bold and deliberate, encapsulating Van Gogh in the act of painting his sunflowers—a subject rife with personal symbolism for Van Gogh. Gauguin’s decision to portray Van Gogh in this manner was more than a mere depiction; it was an acknowledgment of Van Gogh's vision and the pivotal role the sunflower series played in expressing gratitude and friendship. During their nine-week stay in Arles, Van Gogh wrote to his brother Theo, expressing his anticipation for Gauguin’s visit and his hope for artistic camaraderie. Theo van Gogh, who tirelessly supported Vincent both financially and emotionally, later received the painting after it was completed, preserving a tangible memory of his brother’s artistic journey. Standing before this portrait, one can sense the connection between two artists navigating inspiration and conflict. Van Gogh's exploration of symbolism and friendship culminated in his later creations, influenced by his partnership with Gauguin in Arles.

Directions: Your final stop in Van Gogh Museum: Korenveld met kraaien.

Stop 3: Korenveld met kraaien

Address: Museumplein 6, 1071 DJ Amsterdam, Netherlands

Coordinates: 52.3594, 4.8838

Orientation: As you stand before "Korenveld met kraaien," step a little closer and let your eyes move across the canvas. From this position, you can truly appreciate the drama of the scene — the dark crows cutting through the foreboding sky, set against the vibrant golden wheatfield. This perspective draws you into the tension Van Gogh captured in his July 1890 masterpiece.

The painting "Korenveld met kraaien" is an oil on canvas, notable for its bold strokes and rich texture, which convey a sense of urgency and emotion. Critics have long debated the significance of the crows; some suggest they symbolize foreboding and death, while others see them as a representation of freedom. Standing here, you can sense the artist's struggle and brilliance—qualities that have inspired countless artists who followed him. Theo van Gogh, Vincent's brother, was a constant source of support throughout his life.

From "Agostina Segatori Sitting in the Cafe du Tambourin" to "Korenveld met kraaien," you have followed the thread of Vincent van Gogh's artistic legacy. In 2013, the museum unveiled a long-lost Van Gogh painting, "Sunset at Montmajour," the first full-size canvas by him discovered since 1928. That makes three stops, including Agostina Segatori's Café du Tambourin, which went bankrupt after their breakup. This change in location was significant because it allowed "Korenveld met kraaien" to be displayed prominently within a collection dedicated to the artist’s work, ensuring its preservation and accessibility to the public.

This tour highlights Vincent van Gogh's artistic legacy, showcasing how personal relationships and collaborations shaped his creative journey. The emotional depth and symbolism in works like "Korenveld met kraaien" remind us of the enduring impact of his art on future generations. That's 3 stops in all.
