Orientation: The Picasso, Miro, Dali: Unbound exhibition at the MFA in Boston showcases the transformative power of artist books, featuring extraordinary works by Spanish artists. Within Gallery 184, visitors will encounter three captivating pieces: Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers), Au Soleil du Plafond, and Moses and Monotheism. These books had no precedent, revolutionizing the art form through deep collaboration. The exhibition delves into how images, words, and typography intersect, offering a rare opportunity to explore the innovative world of livre d'artiste. As you continue the tour, you will discover the deliberate selection of Éditions Verve and Mourlot Frères for publication and printing at "Au Soleil du Plafond." Your first stop is Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers). Stand close to Le Lézard aux plumes d’or (The Lizard with Golden Feathers) to appreciate its exquisite details. This proximity allows you to see how Miró’s vibrant color lithographs transform the conventional book into a surreal journey, blending text and image seamlessly.

Joan Miró’s 1971 work, Le Lézard aux plumes d’or, is not just an illustrated book; it's an art form that embodies the surreal exploration of the subconscious. Louis Broder, the publisher, was instrumental in bringing this concept to life. His decision to commission Miró underscores Broder's dedication to reviving the tradition of the livre d'artiste, where artwork and poetry are entwined. Broder’s editions encouraged a collaborative synergy between artist, poet, and printer, making the creative process as important as the final product. Here, Miro expands the visual vocabulary of surrealism—his familiar symbols dance across the pages, integrated with poetic text. The intersection of images, words, and typography invites viewers to journey through Miró’s imaginative realm. As you contemplate the deeper layers of reality encapsulated in this work, consider what future revelations lie within the art of the book.

Stop 2: Au Soleil du Plafond

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3401, -71.0885

Orientation: Stand before "Au Soleil du Plafond" where the interplay of light across the lithographs reveals the depth of this collaboration. From here, you can see how each plate captures the essence of Juan Gris's visual language, intertwined with Pierre Reverdy's poetic narrative.

Gris, whose artistic journey had been deeply intertwined with the Cubist movement, brought his unique visual sensibility to this project. Reverdy, a poet renowned for his evocative, surrealist imagery, provided the textual backbone. Together, they crafted a series of 11 lithographs, each printed on Arches wove paper, an esteemed choice for its texture and durability. These lithographs are not mere illustrations but integral components of a cohesive artistic statement, where image and text coalesce into a singular narrative experience. The choice of Éditions Verve as the Louis Broder and Mourlot Frères as the printer was no accident. Éditions Verve was known for its commitment to high-quality art publications, while Mourlot Frères had a reputation for exceptional lithographic printing.This work advances the exhibition's argument by demonstrating how artists and poets of the mid-20th century revolutionized the book as an art form. "Au Soleil du Plafond" stands alongside other significant works in the exhibition, such as "Le Lézard aux plumes d’or," inviting viewers to explore how the boundaries between visual and literary arts were not just blurred but entirely redefined. As you contemplate this piece, consider how such collaborations influence not just what we see, but also what we believe about the possibilities of artistic expression.

Stop 3: Moses and Monotheism

Address: 465 Huntington Ave, Boston, MA 02115

Coordinates: 42.3401, -71.0885

Orientation: As you stand here, look closely at the intricate interplay of words and images on the sheepskin—a detail often missed from afar.

Dalí, always drawn to the surreal elements of the psyche, took on illustrating Freud's controversial text, which posits that Moses was an Egyptian nobleman. Choosing drypoints and lithographs on sheepskin, Dalí added a rich, tactile layer to the narrative, echoing Freud's deep dive into the origins of monotheism. Commissioned by The Hogarth Press in a set of ten, the work highlights the collaborative spirit that defines the exhibition’s theme—that the book itself can be a holistic art form, integrating image and text seamlessly. The work finds a resonant connection to "Au Soleil du Plafond" seen earlier, underscoring the exhibition's thesis that these collaborations changed our perception of both art and literature permanently.
